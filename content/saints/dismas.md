## His story
Luke is the only Gospel that gives the two thieves a conversation. All four say Jesus was crucified between two criminals. Matthew and Mark say both of them taunted him. Luke says one did, and the other answered.

"Do you not fear God, since you are under the same sentence? And we indeed justly, for we are receiving the due reward of our deeds; but this man has done nothing wrong." Then he said, "Jesus, remember me when you come into your kingdom." And Jesus said, "Truly, I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise" (Luke 23:40–43).

That is the whole canonical story. No name, no past, no town. The Church has always read it as the shortest path in the New Testament from a confession to a promise: a man who admits what he has done, and is answered before the day is over.

<div class=legend markdown=1>

**The name.** **Dismas** (or Dysmas) is not in Luke. It comes from the apocryphal Gospel of Nicodemus, which also names the other man **Gestas**. Painters put Dismas on Christ's right, often with a small cross of his own, and Gestas on the left, twisting away. Jacques Callot's print labels the sheet *S. Latro Dimas*, "St Dismas the thief", beside the three crosses. The names are legend. The dialogue is Luke's.

</div>

A traditional saying calls him the one saint canonised by Christ himself, because the words "today you will be with me" needed no later pope. It is a saying, not a decree. The Roman Martyrology enters him on **25 March**, the old traditional date of the Crucifixion, as the Good Thief.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation, on the strength of Luke's Gospel. The name and the legends around it were never required for the devotion. He is not on the General Roman Calendar.

## Patronages
Prisoners, repentant thieves, funeral directors, and people who come to faith at the last hour.

## Trivia
- The feast on 25 March collides with the Annunciation, so where the Annunciation is kept, his commemoration gives way.
- A monastery of the Good Thief stood for centuries near Latrun, on the road between Jerusalem and Jaffa. The place-name was popularly tied to the *latro*, the thief.
- He is almost never shown alone. His attribute is the cross he shares.
