## His story
Bartholomew is in every list of the Twelve (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:14, Acts 1:13), and almost nowhere else in the New Testament. The name is a patronymic, Bar-Tolmai, "son of Tolmai". From early on he was identified with **Nathanael** of John's Gospel, the man Philip brought to Jesus, whom Jesus called "an Israelite indeed, in whom there is no deceit" (John 1:45–51). John never uses the name Bartholomew, and the Synoptics never use Nathanael, so the identification is a conclusion, not a verse. It is an old and reasonable one: the lists often pair Bartholomew with Philip, and John pairs Nathanael with Philip.

After Pentecost the tradition sends him east. Eusebius, quoting earlier report, has him leave a Hebrew Matthew in India. The martyrdom that art never forgot is set at **Albanopolis in Armenia**: he was flayed alive, and then beheaded. The flaying is not in Scripture. It is the constant tradition of both East and West, and the Armenian Church counts him, with Thaddeus, among its first preachers.

Jusepe de Ribera painted the martyrdom again and again: an old body hauled up by the wrists, the knives not yet at work, the face already somewhere else. Michelangelo, on the altar wall of the Sistine Chapel, gave the apostle his skin. In the *Last Judgment* Bartholomew holds a flayed skin in one hand and the knife in the other, and the face on the skin is widely taken to be Michelangelo's own.

His relics, the tradition says, came from the east to the island of Lipari, then to **Benevento**, and in 983 to Rome, to **San Bartolomeo all'Isola** on the Tiber island, where the shrine still is. Otto III is the emperor usually credited with the Roman translation.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation, as an apostle. His feast on **24 August** is on the General Roman Calendar.

## Patronages
Armenia, tanners, leatherworkers, butchers, bookbinders, and plasterers. The trades follow the flaying.

## Trivia
- A butcher's knife and a flayed skin are his attributes. In some pictures the skin is draped over his arm like a coat.
- San Bartolomeo all'Isola is one of the quieter apostle-churches in Rome, on the island that was already a place of healing in pagan times.
- The Nathanael identification, if it holds, gives him the best sentence any apostle receives before he has said a word: "in whom there is no deceit".
