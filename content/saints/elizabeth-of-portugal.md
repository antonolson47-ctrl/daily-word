## Her story
Isabel was born a princess of Aragon, probably in Zaragoza, and named after her great-aunt St Elizabeth of Hungary. At 12 she was married to **King Dinis of Portugal**, a gifted ruler but an unfaithful husband. She raised his children by other women as well as her own, kept a strict life of prayer, and gave generously to the poor, to hospitals and to convents.

She became famous as a **peacemaker**. When civil war broke out between Dinis and their son Afonso, she rode out between the two armies at Alvalade in 1323 and brought them to terms. She did the same between Portugal and Castile, and between her son and his own sons, and was making a peace journey when she died at Estremoz in 1336.

After Dinis's death in 1325 she walked as a pilgrim to **Santiago de Compostela** and gave her crown to the cathedral. She then lived beside the Poor Clare convent she had founded in Coimbra, as a Franciscan tertiary. ([Coimbra lies on the Camino Portugués](/pilgrimage/camino/portugues/).)

**The miracle of the roses (legend):** Dinis, the story goes, caught her carrying bread to the poor in her skirt, against his wishes, and demanded to see. She opened it, and it was full of roses, in January. The same story is told of her great-aunt Elizabeth of Hungary, which suggests it moved between the two.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified in 1516 and canonised by **Pope Urban VIII** on **25 May 1625**.

## Patronages
Peacemakers, Coimbra, queens, victims of adultery.

## Where to visit
Mosteiro de Santa Clara-a-Nova, Coimbra (her silver tomb); Estremoz castle, where she died

## Trivia
- Her body was found incorrupt when her tomb was opened in 1612; it now lies in a silver and crystal shrine at Santa Clara-a-Nova.
- Coimbra celebrates the **Festas da Rainha Santa** in July of even-numbered years, with a great procession of her statue.
- Her pilgrim's staff and the shell-patterned cloak she wore to Santiago are kept at Coimbra.
