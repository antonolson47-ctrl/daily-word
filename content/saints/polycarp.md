## The story
Polycarp was bishop of **Smyrna** (today İzmir). St Irenaeus, who heard him preach as a boy, says he had known **St John the Apostle** and others who had seen the Lord. St Ignatius of Antioch wrote him a letter on his way to martyrdom, and Polycarp's own *Letter to the Philippians* survives.

## The full story

### 86 years
The ***Martyrdom of Polycarp***, written by the church of Smyrna soon after his death, is the oldest account of a Christian martyrdom outside the New Testament. Arrested at a farm, the old bishop fed his captors and asked for an hour to pray. In the stadium the proconsul urged him to swear by Caesar and curse Christ. He answered: "**Eighty-six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong. How can I blaspheme my King who saved me?**"

### The fire
He was sentenced to burn. He asked not to be nailed to the stake. The account says the flames formed an arch around him like a sail, and he did not burn, but "was like bread baking"; a fragrant scent rose. An executioner stabbed him, and so much blood flowed that it quenched the fire. The Christians gathered his bones, "more precious than jewels", and kept the day of his death as his "birthday" each year, the beginning of the cult of martyrs.

<div class=history markdown=1>**History.** The *Martyrdom* is an eyewitness letter and largely historical. The miraculous details (the arch of fire, the dove) reflect the writers' faith.</div>

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint; one of the Apostolic Fathers.

## Patronages
Earache (popular), Smyrna (İzmir).

## Trivia
- The *Martyrdom of Polycarp* is the first document to use the word "catholic" for the whole Church.
- Sant'Ambrogio della Massima in Rome claims relics.
