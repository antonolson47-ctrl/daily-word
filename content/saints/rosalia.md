## Her story
According to Palermo tradition, Rosalia was a young noblewoman of the Sinibaldi family, related to the Norman royal house, who left the court to live as a hermit, first in a cave at Quisquina in the mountains and then in a cave on **Monte Pellegrino**, the headland above Palermo, where she died alone. For centuries she was little known.

**The plague of 1624 (history and legend):** In 1624, as plague struck Palermo, a woman reported a vision of Rosalia, and on **15 July 1624** bones were found in the cave on Monte Pellegrino. A hunter named Vincenzo Bonello then said Rosalia had appeared to him, asking that her relics be carried through the city. When they were carried in procession in 1625, the plague receded, and Palermo made her its patroness. Her life story before 1624 rests mostly on tradition, but a Latin inscription in the Quisquina cave, said to be hers, records her stay there.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint. **Pope Urban VIII** added her to the Roman Martyrology in 1630.

## Patronages
Palermo, against plague and epidemics.

## Where to visit
Sanctuary of Santa Rosalia in her cave on Monte Pellegrino; Palermo Cathedral (her silver urn)

## Trivia
- The **Festino** of 14–15 July is Palermo's greatest festival, with a huge triumphal chariot pulled through the city and fireworks over the sea.
- On the night of 3–4 September, thousands of Palermitans walk barefoot up Monte Pellegrino to her shrine (the *acchianata*).
- **Anthony van Dyck**, in Palermo in 1624 when the plague struck, painted several pictures of her, which spread her fame across Europe.
