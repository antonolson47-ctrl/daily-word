## Her story
Ursula, in the form the later Middle Ages loved, was a British princess who agreed to marry a pagan prince on one condition: three years' delay, and a pilgrimage with **eleven thousand virgins**. They sailed the Rhine. At Cologne the Huns were there. Ursula refused the Hun king's offer, and she and all her company were killed. The *Golden Legend* fills in dreams, popes, and a storm. Venice painted the cycle. Carpaccio gave her a bedroom. Cologne built her a church.

<div class=legend markdown=1>

**What can actually be said.** A stone inscription in the church of St Ursula at Cologne, the **Clematius inscription**, is the oldest piece. It may be fifth century, perhaps fourth. A man named Clematius says he rebuilt, from his own money, a basilica on the ground where holy virgins had shed their blood for Christ. It names no Ursula, and it gives no number eleven thousand. A cult of virgin martyrs at Cologne is early. Ursula, the British princess, the sea voyage and the eleven thousand are legend, piled up from the ninth century onward and fixed by the *Golden Legend* in the thirteenth.

</div>

The number is the part everyone asks about. One explanation, widely repeated and not proved, is a misreading: a text that meant eleven martyred virgins, or a name like Undecimilla, was copied as *XI milia*, eleven thousand. Once the number existed, Cologne had bones enough, from the Roman cemetery under the church, to furnish it. The **Golden Chamber** (*Goldene Kammer*) of the basilica is lined with decorated bones and skulls set in patterns on the walls. It is a real room. It is not evidence that eleven thousand women died there on one day.

Caravaggio's last painting, made in 1610, throws the legend away and keeps one murder. A king, very close, has just shot Ursula with an arrow. She looks down at the wound, surprised, as if the number never mattered and this did.

Her feast, **21 October**, was dropped from the universal calendar in **1969** because the legend could not be sorted from a history. She remains in the Roman Martyrology, and Cologne never stopped keeping her.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation, from the Cologne cult of the virgin martyrs. Not on the General Roman Calendar since 1969.

## Patronages
Cologne, the old order of the Ursulines (founded by St Angela Merici, who took the name), students and teachers, and a good death. The Ursuline schools carried her name much further than the legend could.

## Trivia
- Christopher Columbus named the Virgin Islands after her company: Santa Ursula y las Once Mil Vírgenes.
- The Golden Chamber is still open to visitors. It is one of the strangest rooms in Christian Europe, and it should be read as a reliquary of a legend, not as a census.
- Hans Memling's shrine of St Ursula in Bruges, painted on a gold reliquary the size of a small house, tells the whole voyage in six panels. It is the legend at its most beautiful, and it is still a legend.
