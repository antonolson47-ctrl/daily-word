## His story
John was born in Antioch about 349, baptised as a young man, and trained in rhetoric by the pagan Libanius, the best teacher in the city. Libanius is said to have lamented, dying, that John would have been his successor "if the Christians had not stolen him". John did not stay for a career. He went out to the monks in the mountains south of Antioch, memorised Scripture, and ruined his stomach. Illness drove him back to the city.

Ordained priest in 386, he preached in Antioch for twelve years, often twice a week or more, verse by verse through Genesis, Matthew, John and Paul. The nickname **Chrysostom**, "golden mouth", was given him later. The sermons are still hard work. He told the rich that the Eucharist and the poor were the same Christ: the one who said "This is my body" is the one who said "I was hungry and you gave me no food". He would not let a golden chalice excuse a hungry neighbour.

In 397 he was taken to **Constantinople**, almost by force, and made patriarch. The capital was richer than Antioch and less willing to be told so. He reformed the clergy, cut the bishop's household, and preached against the court's display. The empress **Eudoxia** took it personally. With Theophilus, patriarch of Alexandria, John's enemies held the **Synod of the Oak** in 403 and deposed him. The city rioted. He was recalled, then exiled again in 404 after he would not keep silence when a silver statue of the empress went up near the cathedral.

They marched him on foot toward the edge of the empire. The Menologion of Basil II, a Vatican manuscript of about the year 1000, shows that journey: a small haloed man on a mule between soldiers, in a gold landscape that does not look like mercy. He died at **Comana** in Pontus on **14 September 407**. His last words, as they were reported, were "Glory to God for all things".

In 438 his body was brought back to Constantinople in honour. Most of the relics later rested in St Peter's in Rome. In **2004** John Paul II returned a portion to Patriarch Bartholomew; they are in the church of **St George** at the Phanar in Istanbul. The Divine Liturgy used by the Eastern churches on most Sundays bears his name.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation. Doctor of the Church. His memorial on **13 September** is on the General Roman Calendar. The East keeps 13 November, and 27 January for the translation of his relics.

## Patronages
Preachers, orators, lecturers, and epileptics. Constantinople claimed him as one of its three holy hierarchs, with Basil and Gregory Nazianzen.

## Trivia
- "Golden mouth" was a compliment paid after his death. In life his enemies found the mouth the problem.
- His sermon series on the statues, preached in Antioch in 387 after a tax riot, talked the city down from panic while imperial commissioners decided whether to punish it.
- He is one of the four giant Doctors whose statues Bernini placed around the Chair of Peter, with Athanasius, Ambrose and Augustine.
