## His story
Almost nothing about Christopher is historically certain. An early martyr of that name seems to have been venerated in Asia Minor, but the famous story comes from medieval legend, especially the 13th-century *Golden Legend*: a huge man named Reprobus wanted to serve the greatest king, and ended up carrying travellers across a dangerous river. One night a small child asked to be carried. The child grew heavier and heavier until Reprobus could barely reach the far bank, and the child revealed he was Christ, carrying the weight of the world. Reprobus became *Christophoros*, "Christ-bearer".

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint. In the **1969 reform of the General Roman Calendar**, his feast was removed from the universal calendar and left to local calendars, because so little is known about him historically. He was **not** "de-canonised": he remains a saint and is listed in the Roman Martyrology.

## Patronages
Travellers, drivers, sailors, against storms.

## Trivia
- The famous St Christopher medal carried by motorists and travellers comes from the river-crossing legend.
- In Eastern icons he is sometimes shown with a dog's head, an image that probably grew out of a legend that he came from a land of *cynocephali* (dog-headed people).
- He was one of the Fourteen Holy Helpers, a group of saints invoked together in medieval Germany.
