## His story
Raymund Kolbe became a Conventual Franciscan, taking the name Maximilian. He founded the Militia of the Immaculata, a huge Catholic press, and the friary-town of **Niepokalanów** near Warsaw, and spent years as a missionary in Nagasaki, Japan. Arrested by the Gestapo, he was sent to **Auschwitz** in 1941. When ten prisoners were chosen to starve to death in reprisal for an escape, he volunteered to take the place of **Franciszek Gajowniczek**, who cried out that he had a wife and children. After two weeks in the starvation bunker, Kolbe was killed with a lethal injection on **14 August 1941**.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified in 1971 by Pope Paul VI and canonised by **Pope John Paul II** on **10 October 1982**, as a martyr of charity.

## Patronages
Drug addicts, prisoners, journalists, families, the pro-life movement.

## Trivia
- Franciszek Gajowniczek **survived the war** and attended both the beatification and the canonisation; he died in 1995.
- Kolbe had a lively interest in science and sketched ideas for a spacecraft as a young man.
- His starvation cell (Block 11) at Auschwitz is a place of pilgrimage.
