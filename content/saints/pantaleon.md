## His story
Pantaleon, or **Panteleimon** in the East, was a physician of **Nicomedia**, the emperor's city on the sea of Marmara, martyred under Diocletian about 305. That much is the early cult. The Roman Martyrology still calls him a doctor who was racked, burned with torches, and killed with the sword, comforted, it says, by an appearance of the Lord.

The passion adds a plot. He was the son of a pagan father and a Christian mother, trained in medicine, and physician to the emperor **Maximian**. He drifted from the faith. A Christian priest, Hermolaus, brought him back. He healed a child, or a blind man, in the name of Christ and would take no fee. The Eastern church counts him among the **Holy Unmercenaries**, the doctor-saints who treated without payment. Veronese painted that moment: Pantaleon in a red cloak, looking up, a patient at his feet, a little angel coming down with a palm, which is already the sign of how the story ends.

Denounced at court, he was sentenced to die. The legend runs through the usual refusals of the instruments: the torch, the lead, the wheel, the wild beasts, until a sword finally worked, and only after he had forgiven the executioner. From the wound, milk and blood. His body, various churches later claimed. His name means "all-compassionate".

He was one of the **Fourteen Holy Helpers**, the group of saints medieval Europe invoked together against sickness and sudden death. His feast, **27 July**, is in the Roman Martyrology and in the Eastern calendar. It is not a universal memorial on the General Roman Calendar.

At **Ravello**, above Amalfi, the cathedral keeps a small ampoule said to contain his dried blood. On his feast, and sometimes on other days, the dark mass is reported to **liquefy**, as the blood of [Januarius](/saints/januarius/) does in Naples. The devotion is old and local. The Church has not defined it as a miracle. People in Ravello go anyway.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation, from an early cult at Nicomedia. Honoured in East and West. Optional or local in the West since the calendar reform; fixed in the East on 27 July.

## Patronages
Physicians, midwives, livestock, and against headaches and tuberculosis. Invoked, with the other Unmercenaries, by people who are sick and cannot pay.

## Trivia
- Nicomedia is İzmit, in Turkey. The martyrdom-site is not a major modern pilgrimage. Ravello is.
- A pantaleon, the large hammered dulcimer, was named later and has nothing to do with him, though the coincidence pleases instrument-makers.
- In the Eastern icons he holds a medicine box and a spoon, not a sword. The West preferred the sword and the wheel.
