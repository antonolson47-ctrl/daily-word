## The story
Apollonia is one of the best-documented early martyrs because we have a contemporary letter. Bishop Dionysius of Alexandria, quoted by Eusebius, describes the pogrom of 249 under Decius' predecessor: an aged virgin Apollonia was seized by a mob.

## The full story

### Eusebius' letter
Dionysius writes that they broke her jaw and teeth with blows and threatened to burn her alive unless she blasphemed. Given a moment to think, she threw herself into the fire — a detail early Christians themselves debated (Augustine discusses whether such self-offering is lawful) and which art softened into execution.

### Pincers and tooth
Medieval art shows her calmly holding pincers gripping a tooth — instantly recognisable, and invoked by everyone who has ever had toothache. Her cult spread from Alexandria through the West; relics were claimed by many churches, satirised already in the Reformation for their improbable number.

<div class=history markdown=1>**History.** The core comes from Dionysius via Eusebius, *Church History* VI.41 — near-contemporary testimony. The pincers iconography and many relic claims are medieval additions.</div>

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation; in the Roman Martyrology on 9 February from antiquity.

## Patronages
Dentists, dental patients and toothache sufferers.

## Trivia
- She is shown in hundreds of late-medieval altarpieces serenely holding the pincers — dentistry's patron long before dentistry existed.
- Augustine uses her case in *City of God* (I.26) when discussing voluntary death.
