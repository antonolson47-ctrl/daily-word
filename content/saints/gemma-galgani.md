## Her story
Gemma Galgani was born near **Lucca**, in Tuscany, the daughter of a pharmacist. Her mother died when she was seven; her father died bankrupt when she was 19, leaving the children in poverty. Gemma fell seriously ill with spinal tuberculosis and was given up by the doctors; in 1899 she recovered suddenly, attributing her cure to St Gabriel of Our Lady of Sorrows, a young Passionist.

That same year, on **8 June 1899**, she reported receiving the **stigmata**, which appeared on Thursday evenings and Fridays. She said she saw and spoke with her guardian angel and with Jesus, and suffered attacks from the devil. Taken in by the kind Giannini family, she longed to become a Passionist nun, but was refused because of her health. She died of tuberculosis on Holy Saturday, 11 April 1903, aged 25.

Her experiences are recorded in her diary and letters and in the testimony of her spiritual director, the Passionist Fr Germano Ruoppolo, who wrote her life. Some contemporaries, including doctors, were sceptical, and the Church canonised her for her heroic virtue, **without ruling on** her mystical phenomena.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified in 1933 and canonised by **Pope Pius XII** on **2 May 1940**.

## Patronages
Pharmacists, students, those suffering from back pain or tuberculosis, the loss of parents.

## Where to visit
Santuario di Santa Gemma (Passionist monastery), Lucca, where her body rests; Casa Giannini, where she lived

## Trivia
- Only a handful of photographs of her survive, all taken in the last years of her life.
- She is often called the "**Daughter of the Passion**".
- Her body rests in the Passionist monastery in Lucca, which she longed to enter but could not.
