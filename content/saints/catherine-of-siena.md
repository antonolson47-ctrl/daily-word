## Her story
The 24th of 25 children of a Sienese dyer, Catherine refused marriage and became a Dominican tertiary, living a life of prayer and fasting at home before turning to care for the sick and poor. Though she learned to write only late, she dictated hundreds of letters to popes, kings and ordinary people, and a book of mystical theology, *The Dialogue*. She urged **Pope Gregory XI** to return the papacy from Avignon to Rome, which he did in 1377, and she worked for peace in the Church during the Western Schism. She died in Rome aged 33.

## Path to sainthood
Canonised by **Pope Pius II** in **1461**. Declared a **Doctor of the Church** by **Pope Paul VI** in 1970 (with Teresa of Ávila, the first women so named), and **co-patroness of Europe** by Pope John Paul II in 1999.

## Patronages
Italy (with Francis of Assisi), Europe, nurses.

## Trivia
- Her **head** is venerated in the Basilica of San Domenico in Siena; most of her body lies in Santa Maria sopra Minerva in Rome.
- She reported receiving the **stigmata**, invisible to others during her life.
- She wrote to the pope with remarkable frankness, telling him to "be a man" and return to Rome.
