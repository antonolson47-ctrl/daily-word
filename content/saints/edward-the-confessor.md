## His story
Edward was the son of Ethelred the Unready and Emma of Normandy, born about 1005, and sent as a boy to safety in Normandy while Danish kings held England. He came back in 1041 and was crowned in 1042, the last king of the old West Saxon line. He was about forty. He had lived more of his life in Rouen than in Winchester, and his earls, above all **Godwin** of Wessex, knew it.

He married Godwin's daughter **Edith**. He had no child. Later hagiographers said the marriage was chaste, a vow kept from his youth. The earliest Life, written for Edith herself soon after 1066, does not tell it that way, and historians treat the vow as a later explanation of a political marriage that produced no heir. What that childlessness did produce was **1066**: a kingdom with no agreed son, a promise said to have been made to William of Normandy, a claim from Harold Godwinson, and an invasion from Norway. Edward did not live to see any of the three battles. He died on **5 January 1066** and was buried the next day in the church he had just finished.

That church was his real monument. The old abbey on Thorney Island, west of London, was rebuilt from about 1045 in the Norman manner he had learned overseas, and consecrated on **28 December 1065**, a week before he died. It is **Westminster Abbey**. Every English and British coronation since 1066 has happened there, over or beside his grave. The shrine behind the high altar, broken at the Reformation and put back together under Mary I, is still the centre of the building. Henry III rebuilt the abbey in the thirteenth century largely as a bigger setting for that shrine. The Coronation Chair Edward I commissioned stands by it.

He was called **the Confessor** to distinguish him from Edward the Martyr, the boy-king murdered in 979, and because he died in his bed. "Confessor" here means a saint who was not a martyr. **Alexander III** canonised him in **1161**, with Henry II's support. On **13 October 1163** his body was moved, or "translated", to a new shrine. That day, not the day of his death, became the feast.

## Path to sainthood
Canonised in 1161 by **Pope Alexander III**. The translation of 13 October 1163 is the feast. He is not on the universal General Roman Calendar. England keeps him, and Westminster never stopped.

## Patronages
The kings and queens of England, difficult marriages, and separated spouses. Westminster Abbey is his church in practice, whoever the legal patron is.

## Trivia
- A chronicle made for a later Edward, about 1307, draws him crowned, with a sceptre and a book, under the words *Rex Edwardus*. It is a king's portrait, not a likeness.
- Richard II, who had his arms impaled with the Confessor's, prayed at the shrine before riding out to meet the Peasants' Revolt. The Wilton Diptych shows him presented to the Virgin by Edward, Edmund and John the Baptist.
- His deathbed is where Shakespeare, in *Macbeth*, puts the holy king who can touch for scrofula, "the Evil". The healing touch was claimed for him later and practised by English and French kings for centuries. It is royal legend built on his cult.
