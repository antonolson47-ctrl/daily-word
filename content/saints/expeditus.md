## The story
An **Expeditus** appears in the early *Hieronymian Martyrology* among martyrs of **Melitene** in Armenia (today Malatya, Turkey), probably under Diocletian. Nothing else is known of him for certain.

## The full story

### The crow
The popular story says Expeditus was a Roman centurion who decided to become a Christian. The devil appeared as a crow, crying "*Cras! Cras!*", Latin for "tomorrow, tomorrow". Expeditus stamped on it and said "*Hodie!*", "Today!" He is shown in Roman armour, holding a cross marked HODIE and crushing a crow marked CRAS. He is invoked for urgent causes and against procrastination.

### A packing-case saint?
A famous tale says a crate of relics arrived at a convent in Paris marked "*Spedito*" ("sent", or "expedited"), and the nuns took this for the name of the saint. It is told about many places and is probably a joke, but it shows how the cult grew from his name.

<div class=legend markdown=1>**Legend.** Almost everything about Expeditus is legend. His cult grew strongly only in the 17th–19th centuries, in Sicily, Germany, France, Brazil and the Caribbean. In 1905 the Vatican discouraged images and cults not grounded in history, but devotion continued.</div>

## Path to sainthood
Listed among martyrs of Melitene in early martyrologies; venerated by popular devotion.

## Patronages
Urgent causes, procrastinators, students, IT workers (popular), Acireale (Sicily).

## Trivia
- His feast in São Paulo, Brazil, draws huge crowds on 19 April.
- His Latin name suggests "expedite", which helps explain his patronage.
