## The story
Her *Life*, attributed to **St Sophronius of Jerusalem** (7th century), says Mary left home at 12 and lived for 17 years in Alexandria as a dissolute woman, seeking pleasure, not money. Out of curiosity she joined a pilgrim ship to Jerusalem for the feast of the Holy Cross.

## The full story

### The invisible barrier
At the door of the **Church of the Holy Sepulchre** she tried to enter with the crowd, but an invisible force held her back three times. Seeing an icon of the Virgin in the courtyard, she wept and promised to change her life. She then entered freely and venerated the Cross. A voice told her: "Cross the Jordan, and you will find rest."

### The desert
She crossed the Jordan with three loaves and lived alone in the desert for **47 years**. Near the end the monk **Zosimas** met her: she knew Scripture without having read it and seemed to walk on the Jordan. He brought her Communion. A year later he found her dead, with a message in the sand asking him to bury her; a **lion** helped dig the grave.

<div class=warn markdown=1>**History or legend?** The *Life* is a much-loved spiritual story read in Orthodox churches every Lent. Historians regard many details as legendary, but the Church has long held her up as an example of repentance.</div>

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint, venerated in East and West.

## Patronages
Penitents, chastity, deliverance from sins of the flesh.

## Trivia
- The Orthodox Church dedicates the 5th Sunday of Lent to her.
- Paintings show her as a thin old woman covered only by her long hair, holding three loaves.
