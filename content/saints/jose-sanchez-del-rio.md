## His story
José Luis Sánchez del Río grew up in **Sahuayo**, Michoacán, during the **Cristero War** (1926–29), a rebellion against the Mexican government's harsh anti-Catholic laws, which closed churches, expelled foreign priests and banned public worship.

At 13 he joined the Cristero forces, despite his mother's objections, serving as a flag-bearer and helper. In a battle in February 1928, he gave his horse to a general whose mount had been killed and was captured. Held in Sahuayo's own parish church, which had been turned into a jail, he was offered his freedom if he renounced his faith, and refused. On **10 February 1928** the soles of his feet were cut, and he was forced to walk to the cemetery. There he was stabbed and shot, still crying out "**¡Viva Cristo Rey!**" ("Long live Christ the King!"). He was 14.

His martyrdom is documented by witnesses and letters he wrote to his mother. Some vivid details, such as tracing a cross in his own blood on the ground, come from later tradition.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified in Guadalajara on 20 November 2005 and canonised by **Pope Francis** on **16 October 2016**.

## Patronages
Young people, Mexican Catholic youth (popularly).

## Where to visit
Santuario de San José Sánchez del Río, Sahuayo (Michoacán), with his tomb

## Trivia
- While held in the church, he wrung the necks of the fighting cocks a local official kept there, because they were profaning the sanctuary.
- "*¡Viva Cristo Rey!*" was the Cristeros' battle cry, from which they took their name.
- About 90,000 people died in the Cristero War.
