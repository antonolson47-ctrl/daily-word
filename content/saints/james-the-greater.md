## His story
James, son of Zebedee and brother of **John the Apostle**, was a Galilean fisherman called by Jesus from his nets (Mark 1:19–20). With Peter and John he was one of the inner three, present at the Transfiguration and in Gethsemane. Jesus called the brothers "sons of thunder". He is called "the Greater" to tell him apart from the other apostle James.

He is the only apostle whose death is recorded in the New Testament: **King Herod Agrippa I "had James, the brother of John, killed with the sword"** (Acts 12:2), around AD 44. That is history.

**Santiago (tradition and legend):** A tradition first written down in the 7th century says James preached in Spain, and that after his martyrdom his disciples brought his body by boat to Galicia. In the early 9th century a hermit named Pelagius saw lights over a field, and Bishop Theodemir of Iria Flavia identified a Roman-era tomb there as the apostle's. King Alfonso II built a church, and **Santiago de Compostela** became, with Rome and Jerusalem, one of the three great pilgrimages of Christendom. A later legend has James appearing on a white horse at the (probably mythical) Battle of Clavijo in 844 as **Santiago Matamoros**, "Moor-slayer". Historians debate how the tomb came to be identified; the Church has not ruled on it, but popes have visited and honoured the shrine.

## Path to sainthood
An apostle, venerated from the beginning. No formal canonisation (pre-Congregation). In 1884 Pope Leo XIII's bull *Deus Omnipotens* endorsed the authenticity of the relics after excavations.

## Patronages
Spain, pilgrims, Galicia, Guatemala City, labourers.

## Where to visit
Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela (his tomb, the Botafumeiro); see the <a href="/pilgrimage/camino/">Camino de Santiago pages</a>

## Trivia
- When 25 July falls on a Sunday, Santiago celebrates a **Holy Year**. The next one is **2027** (see [Holy Year 2027](/pilgrimage/camino/holy-year-2027/)).
- His symbol, the **scallop shell**, is carried by pilgrims to this day and marks the Camino's waymarks.
- The giant censer, the **Botafumeiro**, swings across the cathedral transept on great feasts, pulled by eight men called *tiraboleiros*.
