## His story
Jean-Baptiste-Marie Vianney was born on 8 May 1786 at Dardilly, near Lyon, to a farming family. The Revolution had closed the churches. He made his first Communion in a shuttered room, with hay bales against the windows. He wanted to be a priest and could barely learn Latin. The seminary at Lyon nearly sent him away. He was ordained on 13 August 1815 with a dispensation, because his teachers did not think he could pass, and because his bishop decided holiness might be allowed to outweigh an examination.

In 1818 he was sent to **Ars**, a village of about 230 people who had not seen much of a priest. He stayed forty-one years. He visited every house, reopened the church, founded a shelter for girls called La Providence, and fought the village's Sunday drinking and dancing with a directness that made enemies. Then the confessionals filled. By the 1830s and 1840s people were coming from all over France, sixteen hours a day of it in the wooden box, up to 80,000 pilgrims a year by the end. He ate almost nothing and slept a few hours. He said his method was simple: a light penance for the sinner, and the rest he would do himself.

He called the devil the **grappin**, the grappling-hook. For about thirty-five years he and the village reported nights of noise in the presbytery, furniture dragged, the bed shaken, and one night a fire in the curtains of his bed that burned the cloth and left him unhurt. He talked about it without drama, as a nuisance that kept him from sleep, and he took the worst nights as a sign that the next day's confessions would matter. These are his words and the words of neighbours who later testified in the cause. They are not a laboratory record. They are part of why Ars felt, to the people who walked there, like a place where the stakes were not theoretical.

He tried to run away from the crowd more than once, and was brought back. He died on **4 August 1859**, sitting in his chair. His body is in the basilica at Ars, the face covered by a wax mask. About half a million people a year still go.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified in 1905 by **Pius X** and canonised on **31 May 1925** by **Pius XI**. In 1929 Pius XI named him patron of parish priests. John XXIII wrote the encyclical *Sacerdotii nostri primordia* for the centenary of his death in 1959. For the 150th anniversary, Benedict XVI opened the Year for Priests (2009–2010) on Vianney's feast, and wrote the letter that set its terms.

## Patronages
Parish priests, confessors, and the village of Ars. Invoked by priests who feel they are not clever enough for the work.

## Trivia
- The portrait the Bibliothèque nationale holds was made from life: an old man in profile, hands up, already looking like the figure on the holy cards.
- He failed Latin and became the patron of the priests who have to preach. The diocese of Lyon has never been embarrassed by the order of those facts.
- "I'll tell you my recipe," he said. "I give sinners a small penance, and the rest I do in their place."
