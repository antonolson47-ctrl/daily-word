## His story
Januarius (Gennaro) was **bishop of Benevento** and was martyred near **Pozzuoli**, by the Bay of Naples, around 305 under Diocletian, together with companions. Little more is known for certain; his cult is early, and his remains were brought to the catacombs of Naples in the 5th century, now named after him.

**The legend:** His later *Acts* say he was thrown into a furnace and came out unharmed, then exposed to wild beasts in the amphitheatre of Pozzuoli, which would not touch him, before being beheaded at the Solfatara volcanic crater.

**The blood:** Naples cathedral keeps two glass ampoules said to contain his dried blood. Three times a year (the Saturday before the first Sunday of May, 19 September, and 16 December), the archbishop holds them up, and the dark solid mass usually **liquefies**, sometimes within minutes, sometimes after hours of prayer. The first record is from **1389**. The Church allows the devotion but has **never officially declared it a miracle**; it calls it a *prodigio*. Scientists have proposed natural explanations, such as a thixotropic gel, but the ampoules have never been opened for testing. Neapolitans see a failure to liquefy as a bad omen.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint. His memorial remains on the General Roman Calendar as optional.

## Patronages
Naples, blood banks, against volcanic eruptions.

## Where to visit
Naples Cathedral: the Chapel of the Treasure of San Gennaro; the Catacombs of San Gennaro; the Solfatara at Pozzuoli

## Trivia
- During the great eruption of Vesuvius in **1631**, his relics were carried in procession, and Neapolitans credit him with stopping the lava.
- The September **Feast of San Gennaro** in New York's Little Italy began in 1926.
- The **Treasure of San Gennaro**, given over centuries by kings and popes, includes a jewelled mitre with more than 3,000 diamonds.
