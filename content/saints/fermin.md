## His story
Very little about Fermín (Latin *Firminus*) is historically certain. A bishop named Firminus was venerated as a martyr at **Amiens** in northern France from early times. The link with **Pamplona** comes from a later tradition, written down in the Middle Ages.

**The Pamplona tradition (legend):** Fermín was the son of a Roman senator of Pamplona named Firmus. He was converted by the priest Honestus, a disciple sent by St Saturninus (Sernin) of Toulouse, and baptised; a well in Pamplona is still pointed out as the place. He became the city's first bishop, preached in Gaul and was beheaded at Amiens. His cult in Pamplona really began in **1186**, when Bishop Pedro de París brought a fragment of his skull from Amiens; it is kept in a silver bust in Pamplona Cathedral.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint, known from liturgical tradition rather than reliable records. He is co-patron of **Navarre** with St Francis Xavier (made official in the 1650s). Pamplona's official patron is in fact St Saturninus; Fermín is its patron by popular devotion.

## Patronages
Pamplona, Navarre (co-patron), bakers, wine-makers.

## Where to visit
Capilla de San Fermín in the Church of San Lorenzo, Pamplona; Pamplona Cathedral; on the <a href="/pilgrimage/camino/frances/">Camino Francés</a>

## Trivia
- The famous **Sanfermines** festival (6–14 July), with its *encierro* (running of the bulls) through the old town each morning, honours him. The feast moved from 10 October to 7 July in **1591**, at the town council's request, to catch better weather and the summer fair.
- Before each run, the runners sing a prayer to a small statue of the saint set in a niche on the Cuesta de Santo Domingo, asking for his protection.
- His statue is carried through the city in a great procession on 7 July.
