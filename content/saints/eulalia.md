## Her story
Eulalia of **Mérida** was a girl of about 12 who, during the persecution of Diocletian, slipped away from her family's country house and presented herself to the Roman governor, denouncing the worship of idols. She was tortured and burned to death. The poet **Prudentius**, writing around 400, devoted a hymn (*Peristephanon* III) to her: he says a white **dove** flew from her mouth as she died and that snow fell to cover her body. Her tomb in Mérida was a great pilgrimage site in late antiquity, and her basilica stands on it.

**Barcelona** honours its own St Eulalia, a 13-year-old said to have suffered 13 tortures under Diocletian, including being rolled down a street in a barrel of glass and nails and crucified on an X-shaped cross.

The Mérida martyr is early and well attested (Prudentius, early calendars). Most historians think **Eulalia of Barcelona** is a local copy of the Mérida saint whose story grew in the 7th century, though Barcelona venerates her separately. Her relics were placed in the crypt of Barcelona Cathedral in 1339.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation saint, among the most venerated martyrs of Roman Spain.

## Patronages
Mérida, Barcelona (co-patron), sailors, against drought.

## Where to visit
Basílica de Santa Eulalia, Mérida (on the <a href="/pilgrimage/camino/via-de-la-plata/">Vía de la Plata</a>); Barcelona Cathedral crypt and the 13 geese in its cloister

## Trivia
- Barcelona Cathedral keeps **13 white geese** in its cloister, one for each year of Eulalia's life.
- The Baixada de Santa Eulàlia, a steep street in Barcelona's Gothic Quarter, is said to be where she was rolled in the barrel.
- The *Sequence of Saint Eulalia* (c. 880), about her martyrdom, is one of the oldest surviving texts in French.
