## His story
One of the Twelve is **James the son of Alphaeus** (Matthew 10:3, Mark 3:18, Luke 6:15, Acts 1:13). Mark also mentions a **James the younger** (*ho mikros*, "the small" or "the less"), son of a Mary who stood near the cross, whose brother was Joses (Mark 15:40). Paul and Acts speak of another James again: **James the brother of the Lord**, the leader of the church in Jerusalem, whom Paul met (Galatians 1:19; 2:9), who spoke at the council in Acts 15, and who is the traditional author of the Epistle of James.

From early centuries many Western writers folded these into one man, and called him James the Less to distinguish him from [James the Greater](/saints/james-the-greater/), the son of Zebedee, whose feast is 25 July and whose tomb is the goal of the Camino. Other readers, and a strong Eastern tradition, keep them apart: the apostle son of Alphaeus on one side, and James of Jerusalem, the Lord's brother, on the other. The Church has loved both ways of naming him, and this page gives both.

James of Jerusalem is the one the sources describe. Paul knew him. Josephus says that the high priest Ananus had "the brother of Jesus, who was called Christ, whose name was James" stoned, around the year 62 (*Antiquities* 20.9). Hegesippus, a second-century Christian whom Eusebius quotes, tells a longer death: James the Just, thrown from the pinnacle of the Temple and clubbed. The club became the attribute in art. Rubens puts a wooden club in this apostle's hand, following the Western way of naming him. A James son of Alphaeus is in every list of the Twelve. If the names are one man, this feast with [Philip](/saints/philip-apostle/) is the bishop of Jerusalem, the author of the epistle, and the martyr of the pinnacle. If they are kept apart, the apostle son of Alphaeus has almost no story beyond the list, and the rich narrative belongs to James of Jerusalem. Either way, he is not James the Greater, and he is not the James of Compostela.

## The calendar
Catholics keep Philip and James on **3 May**. Lutherans and the Church of England keep them on **1 May**, which was also the Roman date until 1955, when Pius XII moved the apostles to make room for [Joseph the Worker](/saints/joseph/#worker). From 1955 to 1969 the Roman date was 11 May. The James of this feast is James the Less, not the pilgrim of Compostela.

## Path to sainthood
An apostle, or, on the other reading, the first bishop of Jerusalem named in the New Testament. Pre-Congregation.

## Patronages
Fullers and hatters, from the club of the martyrdom story; pharmacists in some local lists; the dying, because of the manner of James of Jerusalem's death. Those patronages sit on the traditional identification.

## Trivia
- "The Less" translates *minor* or *mikros*. It may mean younger, or shorter, or simply "the other James".
- The Eastern churches keep James the brother of the Lord on 23 October, and do not automatically make him the son of Alphaeus.
- Rubens's apostle series gives each man his attribute. The club is the legend's prop.
