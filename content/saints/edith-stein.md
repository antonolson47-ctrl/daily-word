## Her story
Edith Stein was born in Breslau, now Wrocław, on **12 October 1891**, which that year was Yom Kippur. Her family was Jewish and observant. Her father died when she was not yet two, and her mother, Auguste, ran the timber yard and kept the festivals. Edith was the brilliant child. By fourteen she had decided, on her own, to stop praying. If God was not there, she would not address him.

She studied philosophy, first at Breslau and then at Göttingen with **Edmund Husserl**, and followed him to Freiburg as his assistant. Her dissertation was on empathy: how one person actually comes to know what another is living. She served as a nurse in a field hospital in the First World War. The war and a friend's widowhood shook the clean atheism. She wrote later that she watched a young widow pray and saw, for the first time, a strength that grief did not cancel. In the summer of 1921 she stayed up all night with the autobiography of **Teresa of Ávila** and said, at the end of it, "This is the truth." She was baptised on **1 January 1922**. She went home and told her mother. Auguste wept. Edith did not pretend that the baptism had cancelled the people she came from.

She taught, wrote, and tried for a university post that a woman who was also classified as a Jew was not going to be given, and then that no Jew was going to be given. In 1933 she entered the **Carmel of Cologne** and took the name **Teresa Benedicta of the Cross**. She kept writing. *Finite and Eternal Being* was finished as the net closed. On Kristallnacht in 1938 her prioress moved her across the Dutch border to the Carmel at **Echt**, where her sister Rosa, also baptised, later joined her as the convent's portress.

On 26 July 1942 the Dutch bishops had a protest against the deportations of Jews read in every church. The Nazi answer was to seize Jewish Catholics who until then had been spared, including priests, religious, and the Stein sisters. They were arrested on **2 August**. A witness reported Edith taking Rosa's hand and saying, "Come, we are going for our people." They were taken to Westerbork, then to Auschwitz. Edith Stein was killed in the gas chamber on **9 August 1942**.

She was murdered because she was a Jew. That is the Nazi category, and it is the truth of the transport list. The Church also canonised her as a **martyr**, because the roundup that took her was retaliation for the bishops' public protest: she died, on that reading, in hatred of the faith the bishops had preached. Both sentences are needed. John Paul II, at her canonisation, called her a daughter of Israel and of Carmel. Some Jewish voices objected, then and since, to a Christian claim on a woman the Nazis killed as a Jew, and to a Carmelite convent that had been opened at Auschwitz and was later moved. The argument is part of her memory. It should not be smoothed out.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified at Cologne on 1 May 1987 and canonised on **11 October 1998** by **John Paul II**. On 1 October 1999 he named her a co-patron of Europe, with Bridget of Sweden and Catherine of Siena. Her optional memorial is **9 August**.

## Patronages
Europe (co-patron), philosophers, and converts. Also, in John Paul II's intention, a bridge between Jews and Christians, which is a hope as much as a patronage, and a disputed one.

## Trivia
- The late photograph shows her in the Carmelite habit at Echt or just before, the face thinner, the veil the same shape as Teresa of Ávila's.
- Her memoir *Life in a Jewish Family* was written so that Germans taught to hate Jews would have to meet one household in detail. It stops in 1916. She did not live to finish it.
- Cologne keeps her room at the Carmel. Auschwitz-Birkenau keeps the platform. Pilgrims go to both, and they are not the same kind of visit.
