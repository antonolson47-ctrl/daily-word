## His story
Pere Claver was born in **Verdú** in Catalonia and joined the Jesuits. On Majorca he met the elderly doorkeeper **St Alphonsus Rodríguez**, who urged him to go to the Americas. In 1610 he arrived at **Cartagena de Indias** (now Colombia), the main port for the transatlantic slave trade in Spanish America, and was ordained there in 1616.

For nearly 40 years he met every slave ship as it docked, going down into the holds with food, water, medicine and interpreters, caring for the sick and dying, baptising, teaching and defending enslaved Africans. At his final vows he signed himself ***Petrus Claver, aethiopum semper servus*** ("Peter Claver, slave of the Africans forever"). He is said to have baptised about 300,000 people. He also visited prisoners and lepers. Paralysed by illness for his last four years and largely neglected, he died on 8 September 1654.

His life is well documented from the testimony of his companions. Some miracle stories collected for his canonisation are pious tradition.

## Path to sainthood
Beatified in 1850 and canonised by **Pope Leo XIII** on **15 January 1888**, together with Alphonsus Rodríguez. Leo XIII named him patron of missions to Africans in 1896.

## Patronages
Colombia, enslaved peoples, African missions, human rights.

## Where to visit
Iglesia de San Pedro Claver, Cartagena, Colombia (his body under the high altar); his birthplace in Verdú, Catalonia

## Trivia
- Colombia marks **9 September**, his feast, as its national Human Rights Day.
- His body, in a glass casket, lies under the altar of the church that bears his name in Cartagena's walled old city.
- He did not condemn slavery as an institution, which few did in his time, but his work became a powerful witness to the dignity of enslaved Africans.
