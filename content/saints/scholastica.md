## Her story
We know Scholastica from one chapter. **Gregory the Great**, in the *Dialogues*, says she was the twin sister of [Benedict](/saints/benedict/), born at Nursia, and that she dedicated herself to God as a child. He says nothing else about her childhood. Near Monte Cassino she lived in a community of women. Once a year she and Benedict met. The place, Gregory says, was a house on the mountainside, because Benedict would not spend a night outside his monastery, and she would not enter it.

The meeting Gregory tells is the last one. They ate, and they talked of the life of the soul until it was late. She asked him to stay until morning. He refused. He would not break his rule for her. She folded her hands on the table and laid her head on them and prayed. The sky, which had been clear, broke open. Rain came down so hard that Benedict and the monks with him could not step outside. He said, "God forgive you. What have you done?" She answered, Gregory says, "I asked you, and you would not listen. I asked my Lord, and he did. Go now, if you can, back to your monastery."

They talked all night of holy things. In the morning he left. Three days later, in his cell, Benedict saw his sister's soul leave her body in the form of a **dove**. He sent the monks to bring her body, and he laid it in the tomb he had prepared for himself. Gregory, who tells this about fifty years later, says he had it from people of the monastery. It is the only early account. There is no second source to set beside it. Luca Giordano, centuries later, painted the death at Santa Giustina in Padua: nuns around a bed, and the dove already rising into a crowd of angels.

## Path to sainthood
Pre-Congregation, on Gregory's witness and the Benedictine memory. Her memorial on **10 February** is on the General Roman Calendar. Tradition puts her tomb with Benedict's at **Monte Cassino**. The abbey has been destroyed and rebuilt more than once, last after the bombing of 1944; the crypt shrine of Benedict and Scholastica was made again.

## Patronages
Benedictine nuns, convulsive children, and, because of the storm, protection against rain and lightning. Schools of the Benedictine tradition keep her with her brother.

## Trivia
- Gregory's point is not that a nun outranked an abbot. It is that she loved more, and that love was allowed to bend the timetable.
- She is rarely painted without Benedict, or without the dove. The dove is his vision of her death, not a bird she kept.
- Her feast falls the day before the old feast of Our Lady of Lourdes, which is why 10 February can feel crowded in a parish calendar.
