The Way starts at the gates of **Melrose Abbey**, the most beautiful ruin in the Borders, and climbs at once over the three summits of the **Eildon Hills**, from which, on a clear day, you can see the whole road ahead to the Cheviots.

## Good to know
- **Route:** Melrose – Eildon Hills – Bowden – Newtown St Boswells – along the **Tweed** past **Dryburgh Abbey** (across the river) – St Boswells – the Roman road of **Dere Street** – **Harestanes** visitor centre near Ancrum.
- **Melrose Abbey** is cared for by Historic Environment Scotland; check opening times and tickets on its site. The tradition is that the **heart of Robert the Bruce** is buried here.
- **Old Melrose**, the site of Cuthbert's monastery, is on a loop of the Tweed 3 km east of the town, on private land; it can be seen from the riverside path.
- **Getting there:** the Borders Railway from Edinburgh to **Tweedbank**, 3 km from Melrose.

## Deep Dive

### Old Melrose and Cuthbert's monastery
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Cuthbert's Melrose was not the abbey in the town but **Mailros**, a monastery founded by St Aidan's monks around **640** on a promontory in a loop of the Tweed, now **Old Melrose**. There Cuthbert was trained by Prior **Boisil**, after whom St Boswells is named, and **Abbot Eata**. Boisil died of plague in **664** and Cuthbert succeeded him as prior. The present **Melrose Abbey** was founded by the **Cistercians** under King **David I** in **1136**; it was burned by English armies several times and fell into ruin after the Reformation.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legends.** The Eildons are said to have been split into three by the wizard **Michael Scot**, and **Thomas the Rhymer** to have met the Queen of Elfland under the Eildon Tree. Christian tradition is gentler: Bede tells that Boisil foretold Cuthbert's future on his deathbed while they read St John's Gospel together.
</div>

### Dryburgh and Dere Street
Across the Tweed stands **Dryburgh Abbey** (Premonstratensian, 1150), where Sir Walter Scott is buried. Beyond St Boswells the Way follows **Dere Street**, the Roman road from York to the Forth, through woodland, to Harestanes.
