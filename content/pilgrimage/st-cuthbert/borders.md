The longest day crosses rich farmland and then climbs into the hills. At the top of **Wideopen Hill**, at 400 m the highest point of the Way, you are exactly halfway from Melrose to Holy Island, and can look back to the Eildons and forward to the Cheviots.

## Good to know
- **Route:** Harestanes – Monteviot suspension bridge over the **Teviot** – Jedfoot (back on Dere Street) – woodland and farm tracks – **Cessford Castle** – **Morebattle** (shop, inn) – Grubbit Law – **Wideopen Hill** – Town Yetholm – **Kirk Yetholm**.
- **Jedburgh Abbey** is a 5 km detour from Jedfoot and worth it if you have a spare half day.
- **Kirk Yetholm** is also the northern end of the **Pennine Way**; the Border Hotel has welcomed walkers from both trails for decades. Book beds early.
- Break the day at Morebattle if 28 km is too far.

## Deep Dive

### A border country
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** For centuries this was the land of the **Border Reivers**, raiding families on both sides of the frontier. **Cessford Castle**, a massive L-plan tower house of the 15th century, was the stronghold of the **Kers**, wardens of the Middle March; it was besieged by the English in **1523** and is now a roofless shell beside the path. In Cuthbert's day there was no Scotland or England here: the whole region lay in the Anglian kingdom of **Northumbria**.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Tradition.** Kirk Yetholm was long known as the home of the "Gypsy kings" of Scotland, Romani families who settled here in the 17th and 18th centuries; the last king, Charles Faa Blythe, was crowned on the green in 1898.
</div>
