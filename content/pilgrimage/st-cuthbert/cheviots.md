"They shall mount up with wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary; they shall walk and not faint" (Isaiah 40:31, ESV). On the third day the Way crosses the **border into England** and the **Northumberland National Park**, among Iron Age hillforts, then descends to the market town of **Wooler**. On the fourth it crosses the moors to the sandstone overhang of **St Cuthbert's Cave**, and from the Kyloe Hills above it walkers see **Holy Island** for the first time.

## Good to know
- **Kirk Yetholm – Wooler** (19 km, 6–7 hours): shared at first with the Pennine Way; over the border near Eccles Cairn; down to **Hethpool** at the mouth of the **College Valley**; under **Yeavering Bell** (361 m), Northumberland's largest hillfort; into Wooler.
- **Wooler – Fenwick** (19 km, about 4 hours): Weetwood Moor (prehistoric rock art nearby), the 16th-century Weetwood Bridge over the Till, the Roman **Devil's Causeway**, then woods to **St Cuthbert's Cave** (National Trust).
- **Shelter is scarce** in the hills; carry waterproofs and food. Mobile signal is patchy.
- **Fenwick** has few beds; many walkers continue the last 10 km to Holy Island the same day if the tide allows, or use transport to stay in Belford or on the island.

## Deep Dive

### St Cuthbert's Cave
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** In **875**, fleeing the Danish army, the monks of Lindisfarne left the island with Cuthbert's coffin, the head of St Oswald and the Lindisfarne Gospels. Local tradition holds that they rested with the body in this cave on their seven-year journey. Whether or not they did, the cave has been called after Cuthbert for centuries, and it is likely that the hermit himself, who travelled these hills on foot, knew places like it.

### Ad Gefrin
Below Yeavering Bell lies **Ad Gefrin**, the royal hall of the Northumbrian kings. Bede tells that in **627** the Roman missionary **St Paulinus** spent 36 days here, instructing and baptizing crowds in the River Glen, the beginning of Christianity in Northumbria. A visitor centre in Wooler tells the story.
