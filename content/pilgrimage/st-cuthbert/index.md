**St Cuthbert's Way** joins the two places that framed the life of the North's best-loved saint: **Melrose**, where the young shepherd Cuthbert became a monk in **651**, and **Holy Island**, **Lindisfarne**, where he was prior and bishop and where he was first buried. The 100 km route (62.5 miles), opened in **1996**, crosses the Scottish Borders and Northumberland and ends with an unforgettable walk over the tidal sands, following the poles of the old **Pilgrims' Way**.

## Good to know
- **Stages** (official): **Melrose – Harestanes** 24 km; **Harestanes – Kirk Yetholm** 28 km; **Kirk Yetholm – Wooler** 19 km; **Wooler – Fenwick** 19 km; **Fenwick – Holy Island** 10 km. Most walkers take **4–6 days**. The highest point is **Wideopen Hill** (400 m), halfway.
- **Tides decide the last day.** Holy Island is cut off twice a day. Check the Northumberland County Council **safe crossing times** before you set out from Fenwick, and add 30 minutes for weather. The walking route over the sands has its own rule: cross only in daylight, never on a rising tide, and finish **before the mid-point** of the safe period.
- **Waymarks:** a Celtic cross symbol, with the Way's name. The route is one of **Scotland's Great Trails**.
- **Certificate:** the official completion certificate is sent to walkers who return the questionnaire at the back of the official guidebook.
- **Season:** April to October. In wet weather the Cheviot stretch is boggy and exposed. Baggage transfer is available.
- **Extend it:** many walk on to **Durham**, where Cuthbert's body now lies; see [Durham Cathedral](/pilgrimage/st-cuthbert/durham/).

## Deep Dive

### Who was Cuthbert?
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Our sources are the *Life of St Cuthbert* by an anonymous monk of Lindisfarne (about **700**) and two lives by **St Bede** (about 721), with Bede's *Ecclesiastical History*. Cuthbert was born around **634**, probably in the Lothian or Borders area, and as a youth kept sheep. He entered **Melrose**, then a daughter house of Lindisfarne in the Irish tradition of **St Aidan**, in **651**. He became prior of Melrose and, after the **Synod of Whitby** (664) chose Roman over Irish customs, prior of Lindisfarne, where he worked patiently to reconcile the community. He walked and rode the hill country preaching to the poorest. In **676** he withdrew to live as a hermit on **Inner Farne**; in **685** he was made bishop, unwillingly, and two years later went back to his island, where he died on **20 March 687**.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legends.** Bede tells that on the night **St Aidan** died, 31 August 651, the young shepherd saw angels carrying a soul to heaven, and set out for Melrose the next day. He tells of sea otters that warmed his feet after he had prayed all night standing in the sea at Coldingham, of eider ducks he protected on the Farnes (still called *Cuddy ducks*), and of ravens who apologized for stealing his thatch.
</div>

### The wandering saint
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** When his tomb was opened in **698**, eleven years after his death, his body was found **incorrupt**, and Lindisfarne became the North's great shrine. Around this time the monk **Eadfrith** made the **Lindisfarne Gospels** in his honour. The Vikings raided Lindisfarne in **793**. In **875** the monks left the island with Cuthbert's coffin, and wandered for seven years before settling at **Chester-le-Street** (883). In **995** they brought him to **Durham**, where the great Norman cathedral was begun in **1093** around his shrine.
</div>

"Be still, and know that I am God" (Psalm 46:10, ESV). The Way is a walk of open country and silence, as Cuthbert's life was, and of a holy island where prayer has been made for 1,400 years.
