Built over the grave of St Olav, **Nidaros Cathedral** is the northernmost medieval cathedral in the world and the largest in Scandinavia. Its west front, crowded with statues of saints, kings and prophets, is the sight that ends every St Olav Way. Outside it stands the **zero stone**, the last pilgrim milestone: 0 km to go.

## Good to know
- **Tickets (official site):** adults **NOK 140**, ages 8–25 NOK 70, under 8 free; a combined ticket for the cathedral, the **Crown Regalia** and the **Archbishop's Palace** museum is NOK 260. The west front can be seen free from the churchyard. Pilgrims with a pilgrim passport should ask at the reception.
- **Hours** vary through the year and around services; check the [official opening hours](https://www.nidarosdomen.no/en/opening-hours) before you go.
- **Tower climb:** June–August, every hour and half hour, extra NOK 70.
- **Pilgrim reception and the Olav Letter:** at the **Nidaros Pilgrim Centre**, Kjøpmannsgata 1, by the river behind the cathedral, which also has a pilgrim hostel.
- **Pilgrim services:** a short pilgrim prayer is held in the cathedral in summer; check the daily programme.

## Deep Dive

### History
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** After Olav's body was found incorrupt in **1031**, a small wooden chapel and then **St Clement's Church** held his shrine. King **Olav Kyrre** began a stone church over the grave around **1070**. When Nidaros became an archdiocese in **1152–53**, Archbishop **Eystein Erlendsson** (died 1188) rebuilt it on a grand scale, inspired by English Gothic, and the work went on until about **1300**. Fires in 1328, 1531, 1708 and 1719 left the nave a ruin. In **1537** the Reformation ended the pilgrimage, and the shrine was taken away.
</div>

The great **restoration**, begun in **1869**, rebuilt the nave and west front over more than 130 years, and was declared complete in **2001**. The statues of the west front were carved over the 20th century; the last were put in place in the 1980s. Norway's kings were crowned here: **Haakon VII** was the last to be crowned, in 1906, and **Harald V** and Queen Sonja received a consecration blessing here in **1991**.

### Altars, relics and architecture
- **The octagon** at the east end, built over the place where Olav's shrine stood behind the high altar. The silver shrine is gone and his grave has never been found, but the **site of the shrine** is still the focus of pilgrim prayers.
- **The west front**: tiers of statues in niches, with Christ in majesty, Mary, the apostles, saints and Norwegian kings.
- **The rose window** (1930s), with stained glass by **Gabriel Kielland**, who made much of the cathedral's modern glass.
- **St Olav's well**, inside the cathedral, a spring traditionally linked to the saint's first grave.
- **The Archbishop's Palace** (Erkebispegården) beside it, one of Scandinavia's oldest secular buildings, with a museum of the cathedral's original medieval sculpture and the **Crown Regalia** of Norway.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** Medieval writers said a **spring** rose from St Olav's first grave in the sandbank and healed the sick, and that his hair and nails kept growing so that his bishop trimmed them. The *Passio Olavi*, written at Nidaros in the 12th century, collected these and many other miracles of the saint.
</div>
