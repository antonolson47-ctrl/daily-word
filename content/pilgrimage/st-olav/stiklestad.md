On a farm slope above the Verdal valley, the course of Norwegian history turned. Here King Olav Haraldsson, back from exile, was killed in battle by his own people on **29 July 1030**, and here, within a year, began the cult that made him Norway's patron saint. Stiklestad is the most important stop on **St. Olavsleden** before Trondheim, and every summer it stages Norway's greatest open-air drama.

## Good to know
- **Stiklestad National Culture Centre** runs the site: a museum, a folk museum of old farm buildings, an open-air theatre and a hotel. The church is open in summer.
- **Olsok** (around 29 July): the **St Olav Festival** at Stiklestad culminates in ***Spelet om Heilag Olav*** ("The Saint Olav Drama"), an outdoor play performed on the hillside every year since **1954**, with hundreds of actors and volunteers.
- **Getting there:** train to Verdal station, then 5 km; St. Olavsleden walkers arrive from the east over the mountains. From Stiklestad the inner way to Trondheim is about 139 km (about 7 days).

## Deep Dive

### The battle
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Our main account is **Snorri Sturluson**'s *Saga of St Olav* in *Heimskringla*, written about 200 years later, together with older skaldic poems. Olav led perhaps 3,000–4,000 men; the farmers' army under **Kalv Arnesson**, **Tore Hund** and **Harek of Tjøtta** was much larger. Snorri says Olav's men had "Christ's men, cross men, king's men!" as their battle cry. Olav was wounded in the knee, then struck in the neck and finally speared by Tore Hund. The skald **Tormod Kolbrunarskald** died on the field after the battle with a poem on his lips. The exact date, place and size of the armies are discussed by historians, but the battle itself and its outcome are not in doubt.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legends.** In Snorri's telling the sun darkened during the battle (a total eclipse really did cross Norway in 1030, but on 31 August), and **Tore Hund's** wounded hand was healed by the king's blood, making him the first to proclaim Olav a saint. A blind man who touched his eyes after wiping blood from them regained his sight. A spring rose where Olav's body lay the first night.
</div>

### What to see
- **Stiklestad church**, built around 1150–80, by tradition on the very spot where Olav died, with the high altar over the stone against which he leaned. Its 17th–18th-century interior and the large modern fresco of the battle are well worth seeing.
- **The Olav monument** and the battlefield slope with the open-air theatre.
- **The museum**, with exhibits on the Viking age, the battle and the saint, and a folk museum of Trøndelag farm buildings.
