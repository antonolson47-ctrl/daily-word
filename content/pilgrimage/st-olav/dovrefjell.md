Between Dombås and Oppdal, Gudbrandsdalsleden climbs onto **Dovrefjell**, a vast, treeless high plateau that was the hardest and most feared stage of the medieval pilgrimage. Its highest point on the trail is a little over **1,300 m**. It's also one of the most beautiful: open sky, the peak of **Snøhetta** on the horizon, and herds of **musk oxen** and **wild reindeer**.

<div class=warn markdown=1>
**Don't go too early.** Until about **10 June** the path over Dovrefjell is "technically closed": the footbridges over the rivers **Hondyrju** and **Vesle Hondyrju** are not yet in place, the rivers are full of ice water and can't be waded, and snow hides the waymarks. Two stretches cross wild reindeer calving areas. Take the train over the mountain if in doubt, and check the weather on yr.no: temperatures near **0 °C** can occur in midsummer.
</div>

## Good to know
- **Pilgrimssenter Dovrefjell** gives advice on conditions; **Budsjord pilgrim farm**, the last stop before the climb, is a historic lodging.
- **Musk oxen** are large and dangerous if approached: keep at least **200 m** away.
- **Getting there:** the Dovre railway stops at Dombås, Hjerkinn and Kongsvoll.

## Deep Dive

### The king's road and the mountain lodges
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** The road over Dovre was Norway's main north–south route for kings, merchants and pilgrims. King **Øystein Magnusson** (died 1123) is credited with building **sælehus**, shelters for travellers, on the mountain. Their successors, the royal mountain lodges (*fjellstuer*) at **Fokstugu**, **Hjerkinn**, **Kongsvoll** and **Drivstua**, kept open for travellers for centuries; Kongsvoll and Fokstugu still take guests. Along the way are old hunting pits for reindeer and remains of the medieval king's road.
</div>

- **Eysteinkyrkja** at Hjerkinn, a modern church (1969) named after King Øystein, built near the medieval sælehus site as a church for travellers.
- **Kongsvoll fjellstue**, a historic lodge in the Drivdalen valley, with the **Kongsvoll alpine garden** of mountain flowers.
- **The musk ox**: musk oxen were brought from Greenland in the 1930s and 1940s–50s and now roam the Dovrefjell–Sunndalsfjella National Park.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** In Norwegian folklore, **Dovregubben**, the Mountain King of Dovre, rules the trolls inside the mountain (Ibsen's *Peer Gynt* visits his hall). Pilgrim tradition says that Olav himself passed over Dovre and that the mountain spirits gave way to him. The constitution of 1814 was sworn "**United and loyal until Dovre falls**," a phrase every Norwegian knows.
</div>
