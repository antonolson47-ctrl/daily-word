<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** A Benedictine abbey founded around 983–987, perched on a rocky peak above the Susa Valley on the pilgrim road into Italy. It is the official symbol of the Piedmont region, and is often said to have inspired the abbey in Umberto Eco's *The Name of the Rose*.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** The hermit St Giovanni Vincenzo planned to build on the mountain opposite, but each night angels carried his building materials across to Monte Pirchiriano. Another tale, the *Bell'Alda*, tells of a girl who leapt from the walls to escape soldiers, was saved by angels, and fell to her death trying it again out of pride.
</div>


## The Sacra in depth

### Founding stories: legend and history

<div class=warn markdown=1>**History or legend?** The abbey was founded between about **983 and 987**. This is documented, and the main source is the *Chronicon Coenobii Sancti Michaelis de Clusa* written by a monk of the Sacra in the 11th century. The hermit John Vincent, the angels who carried his building materials and the fall of Bell'Alda are legend.</div>

**The hermit and the angels.** Before the monks came, legend says a hermit called **Giovanni Vincenzo** (John Vincent), sometimes linked to Ravenna, lived on Monte Caprasio across the valley. He wanted to build a chapel to St Michael there, but every night his tools and stones disappeared. In the morning he found them on top of **Monte Pirchiriano**, the opposite peak. He took this as a sign and built the chapel there, helped, it was said, by angels. Another story says the chapel was consecrated by heavenly fire, seen as flames over the summit by people in the valley.

**The founder.** The monastery itself was founded by **Hugh of Montboissier**, a nobleman from the Auvergne in France. According to the chronicle, he made a pilgrimage to Rome to ask forgiveness for a serious sin. As a penance he was told to found a monastery, and he chose the Pirchiriano. A group of **Benedictine** monks came and built around the earlier chapel. The abbey grew into one of the most powerful in the region, with dozens of dependent churches and priories in Italy, France and Spain at its height.

**After the monks.** The Benedictines declined in the later Middle Ages. In **1622** Pope Gregory XV closed the monastery, and it lay nearly abandoned for two centuries. In **1836** King **Charles Albert of Savoy** brought in the **Rosminian Fathers** (the Institute of Charity, founded by the priest-philosopher Antonio Rosmini). They still care for the Sacra. The king also had the remains of 24 members of the House of Savoy moved here from Turin cathedral. In 1994 a regional law made the Sacra the official **symbol of Piedmont** ([official history](https://sacradisanmichele.com/la-sacra/)).

### Architecture and special features

The abbey stands at **962 m**, on top of the Pirchiriano. Because the summit is so narrow, the builders put the church **on top of** a gigantic base of masonry and rock, several storeys tall. This base is what makes the building look as though it grows out of the mountain.

- **The Scalone dei Morti (Staircase of the Dead).** The staircase climbs steeply up through the base of the church. It got its name because the monks' bodies were once laid in niches in the walls. Until the 20th century, skeletons were still visible there. Today the niches are empty, and the stairs are lit by small windows. At the top, a huge rock pillar rises through the building and becomes part of the church floor.
- **The Porta dello Zodiaco (Zodiac Doorway).** It stands at the top of the staircase. The 12th-century doorway carries carvings of the **zodiac signs** and constellations, signed by **Master Nicolao**, one of the great Romanesque sculptors of northern Italy.
- **The church.** It is Romanesque-Gothic and built on several levels. The floor of the nave is partly bare rock: the summit of the mountain, worn smooth by pilgrims. The three original chapels underneath (the oldest part, 10th century) hold the **Savoy tombs**.
- **The Sepolcro dei Monaci (Monks' Tomb).** It is a small round ruin near the entrance, from the 10th century. It was once thought to be a cemetery chapel. The Sacra's own guide suggests it was a copy of the **Holy Sepulchre** in Jerusalem.
- **The Torre della Bell'Alda (Tower of Fair Alda).** It is the ruined tower on the north side, named after a legend. A young woman named Alda, chased by soldiers, jumped from the tower to escape them. Angels carried her down unharmed. When she boasted about it and jumped again to prove it, there was no miracle and she died. The tale is told as a warning against pride.

### Relics and sacred objects

The Sacra never claimed a major relic like Michael's cloak in Normandy. Its sacred heart is the **rock itself**, rising through the church floor, and the old chapel built over the hermit's oratory. The church holds 16th-century frescoes and paintings of the Virgin and of St Michael, and a large wooden crucifix. The Savoy tombs make it a royal pantheon. Every year on **29 September**, the Michaelmas feast, there is a pilgrimage and solemn Mass.

### Notable pilgrims through history

- **Medieval travellers on the Via Francigena** and the route over the Mont Cenis pass. These were pilgrims heading for Rome, crusaders on their way to the ports and merchants. The Sacra stood at the gate of the Susa valley, and many stopped there.
- **Monks from across Europe.** The abbey's school and library were famous. One 11th-century abbot, **Benedict II**, fought the bishop of Turin, a sign of the abbey's power.
- **Antonio Rosmini** (1797–1855), the philosopher-priest whose order took over the abbey in 1836. He was beatified in 2007.
- **Pope John Paul II** visited in 1991.

### The surrounding area

**The landscape.** The Sacra stands at the mouth of the **Val di Susa**, where the Alps meet the Po plain. On a clear day you can see Turin and the plain, and Monviso to the south. Below are the moraines of the old glaciers and the two **Avigliana lakes** (Lago Grande and Lago Piccolo), a regional nature park.

**The towns.** **Sant'Ambrogio di Torino** is the village at the foot of the mountain, with the railway station. **Avigliana** has a medieval centre and lakeside walks. **Susa**, further up the valley, has a Roman arch built in 9–8 BC for Augustus, a Roman amphitheatre and a cathedral.

**Local food.** Piedmontese cooking is hearty:
- **agnolotti** (small stuffed pasta) and **tajarin** (egg-yolk tagliatelle);
- **bagna cauda**, an anchovy and garlic dip;
- **toma** and other mountain cheeses;
- the chestnuts of the Susa valley, *Marrone della Valle di Susa* (protected status), roasted in autumn;
- Avigliana's wines and Turin's chocolate and **bicerin**, a coffee and chocolate drink.

**Getting there.** Take the regional train from Turin (Porta Nuova or Porta Susa) to **Sant'Ambrogio di Torino**, about 35 minutes. From the village, the **mule track** climbs to the Sacra in about 1.5 hours. It is steep, with some 600 m of ascent. In summer there may be a shuttle bus. Check the [official information](https://sacradisanmichele.com/informazioni/). By car, follow the road via Avigliana and Colle Braida to the car park near the abbey.

**Best time to visit.** Late spring and early autumn are best: clear air, green or golden woods, and the chestnut season in October. Summer is busy and can be hazy. In winter the weather on top can be harsh.

**Nearby sights.**
- **The Precettoria of Sant'Antonio di Ranverso**, with frescoes by Giacomo Jaquerio (15th century).
- **The Abbey of Novalesa**.
- **The Forte di Exilles**.
- **The Avigliana lakes**.
- **Turin**, with the Egyptian Museum and the Shroud chapel.
