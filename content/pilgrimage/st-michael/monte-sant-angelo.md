<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** One of the oldest shrines to St Michael in the West: a natural cave on the Gargano promontory, venerated since late antiquity. It was a major sanctuary of the Lombards and a great medieval pilgrim goal, where Crusaders and pilgrims stopped on their way to the Apulian ports. Since 2011 it has been part of the UNESCO site "The Longobards in Italy: Places of Power" ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1318)).
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** According to the *Liber de apparitione*, Michael appeared to the Bishop of Siponto in the 490s and later declared that he had consecrated the cave himself, so it's called the "Celestial Basilica" that was never consecrated by human hands.
</div>


## The Grotto in depth

### Founding stories: legend and history

<div class=warn markdown=1>**History or legend?** The three apparitions (490–493) come from the *Liber de apparitione sancti Michaelis in monte Gargano*, a Latin text most scholars date to the 8th century, perhaps with older roots. Worship in the cave by the late 5th or 6th century is likely. Lombard patronage from the 7th century, the bronze doors (1076), the Angevin staircase and the bell tower (1274–82) are documented history. The link to **Pope Gelasius I** (pope 492–496) comes from later tradition.</div>

**The bull (490).** According to the *Liber*, a rich man of Siponto named **Garganus** lost the best bull of his herd. He found it kneeling at the mouth of a cave high on the mountain. In anger he shot an arrow at it, but the arrow turned in mid-air and struck him. The bishop of Siponto, **St Lawrence Maiorano**, ordered three days of prayer and fasting. Then Michael appeared to him and said the cave was under his special protection: "Where the rock opens, the sins of men will be forgiven." These are the words carved over the entrance today ([sanctuary site](https://www.santuariosanmichele.it/grotta-di-san-michele/)).

**The battle (492).** When pagan Neapolitans attacked Siponto, Michael appeared again and promised victory. The defenders won, helped by a storm, thunder and lightning. This "Victory" apparition is the root of Michael's role as protector of the Lombards and later of Christian armies.

**The consecration (493).** The bishop wanted to consecrate the cave as a church. The later tradition says he asked **Pope Gelasius I**, who told him to fast and pray. Michael appeared a third time and said: "There is no need for you to dedicate this basilica I have built. I myself, who founded it, have also consecrated it." The words are carved in Latin at the entrance. When the bishop and the people went in, they found an altar already covered in a red cloth, and footprints pressed into the stone, "like a child's in snow". This is why the cave is called the **Celeste Basilica**, the "heavenly basilica", the only church in Christendom said never to have been consecrated by human hands.

**The plague (1656).** In 1656, during a plague epidemic, Michael is said to have appeared to Archbishop **Giovanni Alfonso Puccinelli**. He told him that anyone who carried a stone from the grotto marked with a cross and the letters "M.A." would be protected. The plague ended in the town. The statue of the Archangel on the square commemorates the event.

**The history.** The Gargano was a Byzantine and then a **Lombard** area. From the 7th century the Lombard dukes of Benevento made the grotto their national sanctuary. The UNESCO site *Longobards in Italy: Places of Power* includes the Lombard works here: steps, stairways and inscriptions ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1318)). Later the Normans, Swabians and Angevins added to the shrine. The sanctuary became one of the great pilgrim goals of medieval Europe, with Jerusalem, Rome and Santiago, and the town that grew up around it took its name: Monte Sant'Angelo.

### Architecture and special features

The [sanctuary's own guide](https://www.santuariosanmichele.it/grotta-di-san-michele/) walks you through it in order.

- **The bell tower.** This octagonal tower was built by **Charles I of Anjou** in thanks for his conquest of southern Italy, starting on 27 March 1274 and completed in **1282**. It was designed by the local architect Giordano. It was originally 40 m tall and is now 27 m.
- **The upper atrium and façade (1865).** There are two pointed arches. The left door is a modern bronze with panels telling the sanctuary's story up to John Paul II's 1987 visit.
- **The Angevin staircase.** It has **86 steps** in five flights, under Gothic arches, with old family tombs in niches along the walls. It ends at the **Porta del Toro**, named after the fresco of the bull above it. An inscription there invites the pilgrim to kneel: "the place where you stand is holy ground".
- **The bronze doors.** These are the famous doors into the grotto. They were made in **Constantinople in 1076** for **Pantaleone of Amalfi** and are of Byzantine craftsmanship. There are 24 panels inlaid with silver, showing angels in the Bible and the apparitions to Bishop Lawrence. Pilgrims' hands have worn them smooth.
- **The grotto (Celeste Basilica).** This is the cave itself, a natural cavern with an uneven rock ceiling, joined to a Gothic **Angevin nave**. Water drips from the rock, and pilgrims once collected it as a remedy.

### Relics and sacred objects

- **The statue of St Michael** stands at the heart of the cave on the **Altar of the Footprints**. It is made of white Carrara marble, 130 cm tall, placed in **1507** and commonly attributed to **Andrea Sansovino** (Andrea Contucci), according to the [Sanctuary's own description of the grotto](https://www.santuariosanmichele.it/grotta-di-san-michele/). Michael is shown as a young Roman soldier, smiling, his sword raised over a devil with a monkey face, goat's legs, lion's claws and a serpent's tail. On **29 September** his sword is taken from his hand and carried in procession through the town.
- **The Altar of the Footprints.** Archbishop Domenico Ginnasio enclosed the "footprints" in copper in the early 1600s. The old Platea document of 1678 describes them as "like a child's in the snow".
- **The bishop's throne.** A carved 11th-century marble **cathedra**, a bishop's chair, stands in the grotto.
- **The Altar of Our Lady of Perpetual Help.** It stands under a canopy, and Pope Gregory XIII (1572–1585) granted it a daily indulgence for the dead.
- **The "stones of St Michael".** Pilgrims still take home small pieces of rock from the grotto, carved with a cross and "M.A.".

### The Lombard crypts and the Lapidary Museum

Under the Angevin staircase are the **Cripte Longobarde**, the Lombard crypts. They are the remains of the earlier entrances to the cave, with **pilgrim inscriptions**, a few of them in **Anglo-Saxon runes**, cut by pilgrims from England in the 7th–9th centuries. They are rare evidence of how far people travelled to reach the cave. The museum also holds sculpture, frescoes and the old altar.

### Notable pilgrims through history

- **Emperor Otto III** came barefoot from Rome to the Gargano in **999** as a penance.
- **St Francis of Assisi.** Tradition says that around 1216 he felt unworthy to enter the grotto. He stayed at the entrance and carved a cross (the Tau) on a stone there. The traditional spot is still shown.
- **Crusaders and Norman knights.** The Norman presence in southern Italy is often traced to Norman pilgrims at the Gargano around 1016. A Lombard rebel recruited them there.
- **Popes and saints.** The sanctuary's guide records the pilgrimage of **Pope John Paul II** in **1987**. Over the centuries many other saints, kings and popes are said to have come.

### The surrounding area

**The town.** Monte Sant'Angelo sits at about 800 m on the edge of the Gargano promontory, with views to the Gulf of Manfredonia. Above the sanctuary is the **Junno**, the old quarter of low white houses in rows, with steps and alleys. Next to the sanctuary are the **church of Santa Maria Maggiore** and the **so-called Tomb of Rotari**. Despite its name, the "tomb" was a 12th-century baptistery. The Normans, Swabians and Aragonese rebuilt the **castle** at the top of the town.

**Local food.**
- **Ostie ripiene**, wafers filled with almonds and honey, the town's signature sweet.
- **Pancotto**, a bread and vegetable soup.
- **Orecchiette** and cavatelli pasta.
- **Caciocavallo podolico**, a cheese made from the milk of the rare Podolica cattle that graze the Gargano.
- Gargano citrus (protected status) and olive oil.

**Getting there.** The nearest airport is **Bari** (about 2 hours by road), and **Foggia** is the nearest main railway station. From Foggia or Manfredonia, buses climb to the town, about an hour from Foggia. By car, the road from Manfredonia climbs the hairpins in about 30 minutes. Monte Sant'Angelo is also a stop on the **Via Francigena del Sud** and the **Cammino di San Michele**.

**Best time to visit.** The feast days are 8 May (the apparition) and **29 September** (dedication), with processions and crowds. Spring and autumn are mild. Summer is hot down on the coast but cooler up in the town. Winters can bring fog and even snow.

**Nearby sights.**
- **The Gargano National Park** and the **Foresta Umbra**, an ancient beech forest (part of a UNESCO beech forest site).
- The coastal towns of **Vieste**, **Peschici** and **Mattinata**.
- **San Giovanni Rotondo**, the shrine of St Padre Pio, about 25 km away.
- **Siponto**, the place of Bishop Lawrence's cathedral, with a wire-mesh sculpture of the lost early Christian basilica.

## Visiting
- You go down the 86-step Angevin staircase and through bronze doors cast in Constantinople (11th century) into the grotto ([sanctuary site](https://www.santuariosanmichele.it/grotta-di-san-michele/)).
- **October hours:** weekdays 7:30–12:30 and 14:30–19:00; Sundays and holidays 7:00–13:00 and 14:30–20:00. Hours change by season ([official hours](https://www.santuariosanmichele.it/hours/?lang=en)). Tel. +39 0884 561150.
- Nearest airports: Bari and Foggia. The town is also home to the Lombard tomb of Rotari and an old quarter of white houses.
