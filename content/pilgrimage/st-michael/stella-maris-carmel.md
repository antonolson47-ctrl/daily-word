<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Mount Carmel is where the prophet Elijah challenged the prophets of Baal (1 Kings 18). In the 12th century hermits imitating Elijah settled on the mountain and became the **Carmelite Order**. Stella Maris ("Star of the Sea") is the mother house of the Discalced Carmelites. The Carmelites returned here in 1631, and the present church opened in 1836 and became a minor basilica in 1839. A grotto under the high altar is honoured as a cave of Elijah ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Maris_Monastery); [Carmelite leaflet](https://www.carmelholylanddco.org/stella-maris-church/)).
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Why it's on the "line."** Stella Maris is dedicated to Our Lady and Elijah, not to St Michael. It is included as the eastern end of the modern Sacred Line story, not because of any ancient Michael shrine here.
</div>

## Visiting
- **Church hours:** daily 6:00–12:00 and 15:00–18:00 (per the Carmelite custody site; call +972 4 833 7758 to confirm).
- Don't confuse it with the separate, larger **Elijah's Cave** on Allenby Road below the mountain.
- The Haifa cable car links the Bahá'í Gardens area and Bat Galim to the Stella Maris ridge ⚠ (check that it's running).
