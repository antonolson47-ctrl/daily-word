"Now war arose in heaven, Michael and his angels fighting against the dragon" (Revelation 12:7, ESV). Across Europe and the Mediterranean, Christians built shrines to the Archangel Michael on rocks, islands and mountaintops. Seven of the most famous are often said to lie on a single straight line.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**The legend of the line.** A popular modern story says these seven sanctuaries form a perfect line, the stroke of Michael's sword as he drove the devil into hell, and that it lines up with the summer-solstice sunset. It's a lovely image, but it is **folklore, not history**. On a globe the sites don't sit on a single straight (great-circle) line; they only look aligned on flat Mercator-style maps, with gaps of tens of kilometres, and the solstice claim doesn't hold up either ([analysis](https://sebastianof.github.io/GeoDsBlog/posts/gds-2024-04-20-cathedrals/index.html), [Italian study](https://doaj.org/article/1e4b3a3b82a340acbd37ce45885a55ae)).
</div>

<div class=history markdown=1>
**What's true.** Each sanctuary is real, ancient and beloved, and devotion to St Michael genuinely connects them: Mont-Saint-Michel and St Michael's Mount were linked by monks, and Monte Sant'Angelo inspired the Norman shrine. That's a pilgrimage worth making, line or no line.
</div>
