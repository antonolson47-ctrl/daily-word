<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** A jagged sea rock off the Iveragh Peninsula holds one of the best-preserved early Christian monasteries in Europe: drystone beehive huts and oratories built by monks who lived there for centuries in the early Middle Ages. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996 ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/757)).
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** In one tradition, St Michael appeared here to help St Patrick drive the serpents and demons out of Ireland.
</div>

## Visiting
- **Landing only by licensed boat**, mostly from Portmagee, in a short summer season run by the Office of Public Works. In 2026 the season opened 9 May and closed early, on **21 September**, because of the weather ([OPW](https://www.gov.ie/en/office-of-public-works/press-releases/closure-of-visitor-season-at-sceilg-mhich%C3%ADl/)).
- Book months ahead. Landings are often cancelled by swell.
- The climb is hundreds of steep, uneven stone steps with no handrails. Not for anyone unsteady on their feet.
- Can't land? "Eco tours" circle the island without landing, and the Skellig Experience on Valentia Island tells the story.
