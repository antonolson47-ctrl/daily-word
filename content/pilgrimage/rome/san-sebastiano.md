On the old **Appian Way**, among umbrella pines and the catacombs, stands the church of **St Sebastian**, the Roman soldier martyred under Diocletian. Long before it bore his name it was the *Basilica Apostolorum*, a place where Christians honoured **Peter and Paul** together. It is also where **Philip Neri** spent nights praying in the catacombs and where, in 1544, he had his famous experience of the Holy Spirit. Read Sebastian's full story on our [St Sebastian](/saints/sebastian/) page.

## Good to know
- **The church** is free. **The catacombs** below can only be seen on a guided tour (fee), typically about 30–40 minutes; check the day it's closed before you go.
- **Getting there:** bus 118 from Circo Massimo or Piramide, or walk the Via delle Sette Chiese from San Paolo. Sundays the Appian Way is largely closed to cars, which makes it lovely on foot.
- **Nearby:** the catacombs of **San Callisto** and **Domitilla**, and the little church of **Domine Quo Vadis**, about 1 km back toward the city.

## Deep Dive

### History: the Memoria Apostolorum
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Under the church, archaeologists found a 3rd-century gathering room (the *triclia*) whose walls are covered with hundreds of scratched prayers to the apostles, such as *Paule et Petre petite pro Victore*, "Paul and Peter, pray for Victor." Why the apostles were honoured here is debated: a tradition holds that their relics were hidden here for a time during the persecution of Valerian in 258. In the first half of the 4th century a large U-shaped funerary basilica, the *Basilica Apostolorum*, was built over the site, and over the centuries the cult of the martyr Sebastian, buried in the catacomb, took over its name.
</div>

The present church is a Baroque rebuilding of **1608–1613** for Cardinal **Scipione Borghese**, by **Flaminio Ponzio** and **Giovanni Vasanzio**. It keeps the old site's memory in its relics and the catacomb galleries beneath.

### Altars, relics and art
- **The Chapel of St Sebastian**, over his tomb, with the marble statue of the dead martyr by **Antonio Giorgetti** (1671–72), designed in Bernini's circle.
- **The Chapel of Relics**: an **arrow** said to have struck Sebastian and part of the **column** he was tied to, and a stone with the imprint of two feet, venerated as **the footprints of Christ** from the *Quo Vadis* story.
- **Bernini's *Salvator Mundi***, a marble bust of Christ, the last sculpture of his life (1679). It was identified here in **2001**, displayed in a niche by the entrance.
- **The catacomb**: galleries on several levels, three 2nd-century pagan mausolea with beautifully preserved stucco and paintings, the triclia graffiti, and the crypt of Sebastian.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend: *Quo vadis, Domine?*** According to the 2nd-century apocryphal *Acts of Peter*, Peter was fleeing Rome during Nero's persecution when he met Christ on the road. "Lord, where are you going?" he asked. "I am going to Rome to be crucified again," Jesus replied, and Peter turned back to his martyrdom. The church of Domine Quo Vadis marks the spot by tradition; the "footprints" stone (in fact probably a Roman votive offering for a safe journey) is kept here in San Sebastiano, with a copy in Quo Vadis.
</div>

### Why it dropped off the list in 2000
For the Great Jubilee of 2000, the Jubilee churches list replaced San Sebastiano with the [Sanctuary of Divine Love](/pilgrimage/rome/divino-amore/). San Sebastiano remains a station of Philip's traditional walk, and most walking groups still come here.
