Once a year, and in Holy Years far more often, Romans and pilgrims set out before dawn to walk to seven great churches in a single day: the tombs of the apostles **Peter** and **Paul**, the catacomb shrine of **St Sebastian**, the Pope's cathedral of **St John Lateran**, the Passion relics of **Santa Croce in Gerusalemme**, the martyr's tomb of **St Lawrence**, and the great Marian basilica of **Santa Maria Maggiore**. The walk is about 20 to 25 km, and its modern form was given to it in the 1550s by a cheerful Florentine priest, **St Philip Neri**, the "Apostle of Rome."

## Good to know
- **Distance and time:** about 20–25 km depending on the streets you choose; allow 10–12 hours with time inside each church. Many pilgrims split it over two days, or use Metro Line B (San Paolo, Circo Massimo, Termini) and buses 118 and 218 for the Appian Way legs.
- **Order:** the traditional order is the one on the map: St Peter's → St Paul's → San Sebastiano → St John Lateran → Santa Croce → San Lorenzo → Santa Maria Maggiore. Starting at Philip Neri's own church, Santa Maria in Vallicella (the *Chiesa Nuova*), is a nice touch.
- **Dress code:** shoulders and knees covered in every basilica; St Peter's enforces it at security.
- **Lunch the Neri way:** his pilgrims picnicked in the Mattei family's vineyard on the Caelian Hill, today the public park of **Villa Celimontana**, near the Lateran leg.
- **Holy Doors** in the four papal basilicas (Peter, Lateran, Paul, Mary Major) are opened only in Jubilee years. The 2025 Jubilee, *Pilgrims of Hope*, closed on 6 January 2026; the doors are walled up again until the next one.
- **Hours change** and Masses close off parts of each church. Check each church's page here and its official site.

## Deep Dive

### Before Philip: the stations and the Jubilee
Pilgrims came to Rome *ad limina apostolorum*, "to the thresholds of the apostles," from the earliest centuries, and Rome's churches had a calendar of "station" liturgies in which the Pope celebrated in a different church each day of Lent. The Jubilee added a legal frame. When **Boniface VIII** proclaimed the first Holy Year in **1300**, the indulgence required visits to the basilicas of **St Peter** and **St Paul**. **Clement VI** added **St John Lateran** for 1350, and **Santa Maria Maggiore** joined later in the 14th century, giving the four "major" basilicas each with its Holy Door. The other three of the Seven, **San Lorenzo**, **Santa Croce** and **San Sebastiano**, were ancient pilgrim churches beside martyrs' tombs and Passion relics, and medieval guidebooks for pilgrims, the *Mirabilia* and the indulgence lists, already grouped seven churches together.

<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Philip Neri did not invent the idea of visiting seven churches, which already existed as a devotion. What he did was turn it into a public, joyful, communal walk, with hymns, sermons and a shared meal, and make it one of the best-loved customs of Catholic Rome.
</div>

### Philip Neri, the "Apostle of Rome"
Philip was born in **Florence** in **1515** and came to Rome as a young man around 1533–34. For years he lived as a layman, tutoring, praying at night in the **catacombs of San Sebastiano**, and talking to anyone on the street about God. In 1544, according to his biographers, he was praying in those catacombs on the eve of Pentecost when he felt a ball of fire enter his heart; his heartbeat was so strong for the rest of his life that the doctors who examined his body after death recorded two broken ribs. (The experience is told by his early biographers; the physical findings were recorded at his autopsy.) He was ordained in 1551, gathered the group that became the **Congregation of the Oratory**, and was famous for jokes, music and gentleness. He died in 1595 and was canonized by **Gregory XV** in **1622**, the same day as Ignatius of Loyola, Francis Xavier, Teresa of Ávila and Isidore the Farmer.

### How the walk began (from about 1552)
The sources disagree by a year or so: Vatican News dates Philip's first organized pilgrimage to **1553**, other accounts to 1552. What is agreed is that he started with a few friends who would gather before dawn and walk the circuit, stopping in each church for prayer, a hymn and a short sermon. The group grew quickly. Philip deliberately set the big pilgrimage in the days of **Carnival**, as a joyful alternative to the drinking, masked balls and horse races of the season, and especially for **Giovedì grasso** (Fat Thursday). By the late 1550s **thousands** joined, walking in procession, with musicians, and a huge open-air lunch of bread, salami, eggs, cheese and wine in the **Mattei vineyard** on the Caelian Hill.

<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Success brought trouble. In **1559** critics told Pope **Paul IV** that Philip was gathering crowds and founding a new sect. He was suspended from hearing confessions and ordered to stop. The ban was soon lifted, and the walk carried on. The Oratorians still lead it, and Roman parishes and student groups walk it every year, often overnight.
</div>

### The route, church by church
1. **[St Peter's Basilica](/pilgrimage/rome/st-peters/)**: over the tomb of the apostle Peter on the Vatican Hill.
2. **[St Paul Outside the Walls](/pilgrimage/rome/st-paul-outside-the-walls/)**: over Paul's tomb on the Via Ostiense, about 7 km away across the city.
3. **[San Sebastiano fuori le mura](/pilgrimage/rome/san-sebastiano/)**: on the Appian Way, over the catacomb where Philip prayed. Walkers take the quiet Via delle Sette Chiese, the street literally named after the pilgrimage.
4. **[St John Lateran](/pilgrimage/rome/st-john-lateran/)**: the cathedral of Rome, with the **Scala Santa** across the square.
5. **[Santa Croce in Gerusalemme](/pilgrimage/rome/santa-croce/)**: the relics of the Passion brought, by tradition, by St Helena.
6. **[San Lorenzo fuori le mura](/pilgrimage/rome/san-lorenzo/)**: the tomb of the deacon martyr Lawrence, beside the Verano cemetery.
7. **[Santa Maria Maggiore](/pilgrimage/rome/santa-maria-maggiore/)**: the oldest great Marian church of the West, and the icon *Salus Populi Romani*.

### The substitution of 2000: Divino Amore
For the **Great Jubilee of 2000**, the Jubilee church list replaced San Sebastiano with the **[Sanctuary of Our Lady of Divine Love](/pilgrimage/rome/divino-amore/)** (*Santuario della Madonna del Divino Amore*), on the Via Ardeatina south of the city. It is the shrine Romans credit with saving the city in 1944, and **John Paul II** had consecrated its new church in 1999. Many walkers today still go to San Sebastiano, and some visit both. We give both a page.

### Indulgence and the Jubilee
In Catholic teaching an **indulgence** is a remission before God of the temporal punishment due to sins already forgiven, granted by the Church under the usual conditions: sacramental confession, Holy Communion, prayer for the Pope's intentions, and freedom from attachment to sin. In a **Jubilee year** the Pope sets out how it may be gained, chiefly by pilgrimage to the papal basilicas and through their Holy Doors. Pope Francis's bull for 2025, ***Spes non confundit***, did this for the Jubilee of Hope. Outside Jubilees, the Church's *Enchiridion of Indulgences* also grants indulgences for devout visits to the papal basilicas on set days. For the walk itself, what matters most is what Philip wanted: prayer, confession, the sacraments, and charity along the way.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend and lore.** Romans say Philip could tell, by the smell, who had unconfessed sin, and that he once levitated while saying Mass. These stories come from the canonization testimony and his early lives; the Church canonized him for his holiness, not for any one of these stories.
</div>

### Saints and stories along the way
The walk is also a tour of the saints: [St Peter](/saints/peter/) and [St Paul](/saints/paul/), [St Sebastian](/saints/sebastian/) on the Appian Way, St Lawrence and [St Stephen](/saints/stephen/) sharing a tomb, St Helena and the Cross, and Mary as protector of Rome.
