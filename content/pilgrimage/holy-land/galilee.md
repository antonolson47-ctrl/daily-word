"Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men" (Matthew 4:19, ESV). Most of the public ministry of Jesus took place in a small triangle on the north shore of this lake: **Capernaum**, "his own city", **Tabgha** and the hill of the **Beatitudes**. It is the gentlest and most prayerful part of a Holy Land pilgrimage.

## Good to know
- **Capernaum** (House of Peter): 8:00–12:00 and 14:00–17:00, entry 10 ILS.
- **Tabgha:** the Benedictine **Church of the Multiplication** (Monday–Saturday 9:00–15:00, closed Sunday) and the Franciscan **Primacy of St Peter** (8:00–17:00) are a short walk apart.
- **Mount of Beatitudes:** 8:00–11:30 and 14:00–16:30.
- **Magdala** (Duc in Altum and 1st-century synagogue): 8:00–18:00.
- **Mount Tabor** (Basilica of the Transfiguration): 8:00–17:00; **groups by appointment only**, at least one day ahead; the road up is by taxi or minibus.
- **On the water:** wooden "Jesus boats" leave from Tiberias and Ginosar; the **Ancient Boat**, a 1st-century fishing boat found in 1986, is at Ginosar.
- **Travel advice:** the UK advises against all travel to the far north (north of Route 89 and on parts of the Golan); the lake's Gospel sites lie south of these zones. Check before you go.

## Deep Dive

### Capernaum
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Capernaum was a fishing town of perhaps 1,500 people. The white limestone **synagogue** standing today dates from the 4th–5th century, built over the black basalt foundations of an earlier, probably 1st-century synagogue, where Jesus taught (Mark 1:21). Franciscan excavations under an octagonal Byzantine church found a **house** that was made into a place of Christian worship in the 1st century, with graffiti naming Jesus and Peter: the likely **house of Peter**, where Jesus healed Peter's mother-in-law. A modern church (1990) hovers over it, with a glass floor.
</div>

### Tabgha
At **Tabgha** (from Greek *Heptapegon*, "seven springs"), the Benedictine church keeps the famous 5th-century mosaic of **loaves and fishes** beside the rock remembered as the table of the multiplication. On the shore, the black-basalt **Church of the Primacy** (1933) encloses the rock called *Mensa Christi*, where the risen Christ cooked breakfast for the disciples and asked Peter three times, "Do you love me?" (John 21).

### The Beatitudes, Magdala and Tabor
The octagonal **Church of the Beatitudes** (1938) has one side for each Beatitude. At **Magdala**, home of **Mary Magdalene**, archaeologists found a 1st-century **synagogue** in 2009, where Jesus may well have preached; the **Duc in Altum** centre has a chapel with an altar shaped like a boat. **Mount Tabor**, rising alone above the plain, has been honoured as the mountain of the **Transfiguration** since the 4th century; the Franciscan basilica (1924) is by Antonio Barluzzi.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Tradition.** The Gospels do not name the "high mountain" of the Transfiguration; some scholars prefer Mount Hermon, near Caesarea Philippi, but Tabor has been the place of the feast since Origen and Cyril of Jerusalem.
</div>
