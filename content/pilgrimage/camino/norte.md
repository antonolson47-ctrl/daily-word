Cliffs, beaches and green hills through the Basque Country, Cantabria and Asturias, passing San Sebastián, Bilbao and Santander, then turning inland in Galicia. Tougher than the Francés (constant ups and downs) and much quieter; its share has fallen nine years running.

## Good to know
- **Fly into** Bilbao, San Sebastián or Biarritz, then go to Irún.
- At Villaviciosa you can switch to the Primitivo via Oviedo.

## Deep Dive

### History and origins
In the early Middle Ages the Christian kingdoms of northern Spain held a narrow strip between the mountains and the sea, and pilgrims often followed the coast, where the roads were safer. Many arrived by sea at Cantabrian ports. As the Reconquista pushed south and the Francés became the main road in the 11th–12th centuries, the coastal way became less used, but it never disappeared: monasteries, hospitals and churches dedicated to St James line it. In 2015 UNESCO extended the Camino's World Heritage listing to the "Routes of Northern Spain", including the Norte.

### Regions crossed
The Norte crosses four regions: the **Basque Country** (Gipuzkoa and Bizkaia), **Cantabria**, **Asturias** and **Galicia**. That means four distinct cultures and two regional languages besides Spanish: Basque in the east and Galician in the west, with Asturian (*asturianu*) present in between. Food is a highlight: San Sebastián's *pintxos*, Bilbao's *bacalao al pil-pil*, Cantabrian anchovies from Santoña and *quesada pasiega*, Asturian *fabada*, cider poured from a height (*escanciado*) and Cabrales cheese, then Galician octopus and Mondoñedo almond tart.

### Elevation and landscape
Expect constant ups and downs rather than one great pass. The route switches between beaches, cliff paths, estuaries and green hills, with a few stretches through industrial towns. Some of the hardest climbs are in the Basque Country early on, where the path crosses steep ridges between fishing towns. After Ribadeo, the route turns inland into Galicia and climbs out of Mondoñedo, one of its stiffest climbs, before crossing the plateau of the Terra Chá. It joins the Francés at Arzúa for the final two days.

The variety is the attraction: one day may start on a beach and end in a mountain village. The trade-off is effort, since the path rarely stays level for long, plus some urban and industrial stretches around Bilbao, Santander, Gijón and Avilés. Several places offer alternatives, including bus or ferry links to bypass the less attractive suburbs. Rain is common at any season; the coast is greener for it.

### Notable past travellers
Few individual pilgrims on the Norte are documented by name; the record lies mainly in the hospitals, monasteries and churches founded for pilgrims along the coast. Many pilgrims also detoured from the Norte to Oviedo to join the Primitivo, following the medieval saying about visiting "the Saviour" (see Camino Primitivo).

### Notable stops
- **Irún**: border town on the Bidasoa, facing Hendaye in France.
- **San Sebastián (Donostia)**: the shell-shaped Concha bay and its old town full of *pintxo* bars.
- **Zarautz, Getaria and Deba**: a dramatic stretch of coast; Getaria was home of Juan Sebastián Elcano, who completed the first circumnavigation.
- **Gernika**: symbolic town of the Basques, with its oak tree and the memory of the 1937 bombing.
- **Bilbao**: the Guggenheim Museum and the old Casco Viejo with the cathedral of Santiago.
- **Santander**: a ferry crossing of the bay and the capital of Cantabria.
- **Santillana del Mar and Comillas**: a medieval village (near the Altamira cave, on the UNESCO list) and Gaudí's *El Capricho*.
- **Llanes and Ribadesella**: Asturian fishing towns below the Picos de Europa.
- **Ribadeo**: the bridge over the Eo estuary into Galicia, near the Beach of the Cathedrals (Praia das Catedrais).
- **Mondoñedo**: an old episcopal town with a fine cathedral.
- **Sobrado dos Monxes**: a large Cistercian monastery, still with monks, which hosts pilgrims.
- **Gijón and Avilés**: Asturias's largest coastal cities; Gijón has the Roman baths of Campo Valdés.

### Stop by stop
The Camino del Norte follows the Bay of Biscay for most of its length, through the Basque Country, Cantabria and Asturias, before turning inland into Galicia. It is the greenest and most dramatic of the routes, with sea views, beaches and cliffs nearly every day, and plenty of steep climbs.

#### Irun
The route starts on the border river **Bidasoa**, across from Hendaye in France. Pilgrims often begin at the bridge or in Hondarribia, the pretty walled fishing town next door, at the river mouth. The first stage climbs over **Jaizkibel**, a coastal ridge with wide views of the sea and France. Try *txuleta* (Basque steak) and cider in a *sagardotegi*.

#### San Sebastián (Donostia)
After a ferry across the inlet at Pasaia, the way comes down to **San Sebastián**, one of the most beautiful seaside cities in Europe, with the shell-shaped **La Concha** bay, Monte Igueldo and Monte Urgull, and the island of Santa Clara. Its old town has the world's greatest concentration of **pintxo** bars and more Michelin stars per head than almost anywhere. Try a *gilda* (olive, chilli and anchovy), grilled hake cheeks or the burnt *La Viña* cheesecake.

#### Getaria
A small fishing port on the coast road, **Getaria** is the birthplace of **Juan Sebastián Elcano**, who completed the first voyage around the world in 1522. The rocky island at the harbour mouth, the "**Mouse of Getaria**", looks like a mouse lying in the sea. In the old lanes, restaurants grill whole turbot (*rodaballo*) over charcoal in the street, served with the local white wine, **txakoli**.

#### Zumaia and the flysch cliffs
At **Zumaia** the cliffs of the **Basque Coast Geopark** are made of **flysch**: thin layers of rock tilted on end like the pages of a book. They record more than **50 million years** of earth history, including the boundary layer laid down when the dinosaurs disappeared. The hermitage of San Telmo stands over the Itzurun beach, and at low tide the rock platforms are exposed. The views made this coast a filming location for *Game of Thrones* (Dragonstone).

#### Gernika
Inland from the coast, **Gernika** (Guernica) is the symbolic heart of the Basque people. Under the **Tree of Gernika**, an oak beside the **Casa de Juntas**, the lords of Biscay swore to respect Basque laws. The town was destroyed by German and Italian bombers on **26 April 1937**, during the Spanish Civil War, an attack made famous by Picasso's *Guernica*. The Peace Museum tells the story. On Mondays the market sells local produce and *pimientos de Gernika*.

#### Bilbao
The way crosses the hills to **Bilbao**, the old iron and shipbuilding city reborn around Frank Gehry's titanium **Guggenheim Museum** (1997). The church of **Santiago**, now the cathedral, has been a pilgrim stop since the Middle Ages. Pilgrims cross the Nervión and leave through the Seven Streets (*Zazpi Kaleak*) of the old town. Try **bacalao al pil-pil**, *marmitako* tuna stew and pintxos in the Plaza Nueva.

#### Laredo
In **Cantabria**, **Laredo** lies along the 4 km curve of **La Salvé** beach, one of the longest on the coast. Pilgrims either walk the beach or take the little boat across the estuary to Santoña, famous for its **anchovies**, salted and packed in oil by hand. Laredo's old town (*Puebla Vieja*) and Gothic church of Santa María de la Asunción are on the hill above the beach.

#### Santander
Pilgrims cross from Somo to **Santander** by ferry, an approach over the bay with views of the city. On the Magdalena peninsula stands the **Palacio de la Magdalena**, a summer palace built for King Alfonso XIII. The cathedral, rebuilt after the great fire of 1941, has a Gothic crypt where tradition says the heads of the martyrs St Emeterius and St Celedonius are kept. Seafood stews (*marmita*) and *sobaos* (butter sponge cakes) are local favourites.

#### Santillana del Mar and Altamira
"The town of three lies", it is said, because it is not holy (*santa*), flat (*llana*) or by the sea (*mar*). Its name comes from **St Juliana**, whose relics are kept in the 12th-century Romanesque **collegiate church**. It is a perfectly preserved medieval village of stone houses and balconies with flowers. Nearby, the **Cave of Altamira** has some of the finest Palaeolithic paintings in the world, made about 36,000 to 13,000 years ago. Visitors see the exact replica in the museum. Try a glass of fresh milk and a *quesada pasiega* (cheese cake).

#### Comillas
**Comillas** is a seaside town of palaces built by the rich Marquis of Comillas in the late 19th century. Among them is **El Capricho**, an early work by **Antoni Gaudí** (1883–85), covered in sunflower tiles and topped with a minaret-like tower. The huge former Pontifical University stands on the hill. From here the coast becomes wilder, with green hills running down to the sea and the **Picos de Europa** behind.

#### Llanes
In **Asturias**, **Llanes** is a fishing town between mountains and sea. On the harbour wall, the Basque artist Agustín Ibarrola painted the concrete breakwater blocks in bright colours, as the **Cubes of Memory** (*Cubos de la Memoria*). The coast nearby has cliffs, hidden beaches and blowholes (*bufones*) where the sea shoots up through the rock. Asturias is cider country: watch the waiter pour it from high above the head (*escanciar*), and order **fabada asturiana** (bean stew) and *cabrales* blue cheese.

#### Gijón
Asturias's biggest city spreads along the long **San Lorenzo** beach. Its old fishermen's quarter, **Cimadevilla**, is on the headland, with Chillida's sculpture *Elogio del Horizonte* at the top. Gijón is the place for a night in a *sidrería*. After Gijón, many pilgrims turn inland to **Oviedo** to join the [Camino Primitivo](/pilgrimage/camino/primitivo/).

#### Avilés
**Avilés** has one of the best-kept old towns in Asturias, with arcaded streets, a medieval church of San Nicolás, and the striking **Niemeyer Centre** by the Brazilian architect Oscar Niemeyer on the estuary. Try the local cake *carajitos* (hazelnut biscuits) and the market at the Plaza Hermanos Orbón.

#### Ribadeo and As Catedrais beach
The way crosses into Galicia on the high bridge over the **Ría de Ribadeo**. West of the town lies **Praia das Catedrais** (Cathedrals Beach), where the sea has carved great arches and caves in the cliffs, like the flying buttresses of a cathedral. They can only be walked under at **low tide**, and access is controlled in summer and Easter (free online permit). Ribadeo itself has Indiano mansions built by emigrants who returned from the Americas.

#### Mondoñedo
Inland, **Mondoñedo** is a quiet cathedral town in a green valley. Its **cathedral** (13th century) has a wide façade with a rose window, and the town was the seat of one of the ancient Galician bishoprics. The local cake, *tarta de Mondoñedo* (almond, angel hair and candied fruit), is famous in Galicia. From here the Norte climbs over the hills to join the Francés at **Arzúa**, two days from Santiago.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** In the cathedral stands the **Virxe Inglesa** (English Virgin), a Gothic statue of Mary. According to tradition, it was brought from **St Paul's Cathedral in London** in the 16th century by Galicians who rescued it during the English Reformation. Historians accept that the statue is English work, but how it reached Mondoñedo is uncertain.
</div>
