The second most popular Camino, now more than a third of all pilgrims. Most start in **Porto** (10–13 days). The **Central** route goes inland through Barcelos and Ponte de Lima and crosses into Spain at Valença/Tui; the **Coastal** route follows the Atlantic through Vila do Conde, Viana do Castelo and A Guarda. A **Spiritual Variant** from Pontevedra includes a boat stretch up the Ría de Arousa.

## Good to know
- **Fly into** Porto (OPO) or Lisbon (LIS).
- **Short version:** Tui → Santiago, ~116 km in 5–6 days.
- Gentler terrain than the Francés; plenty of cobblestones in Portugal.

## Deep Dive

### History and origins
Pilgrims from Portugal have walked to Santiago since the Middle Ages, along roads that often followed the old Roman route between Lisbon (Olisipo), Braga (Bracara Augusta) and Astorga. The kingdom of Portugal itself took shape in the 12th century, and its kings and queens kept close ties with the shrine. The best-known royal pilgrim is **Queen Isabel of Portugal** (St Elizabeth of Portugal), who went to Santiago in 1325 after her husband King Dinis died and left her crown at the altar; tradition says she returned on foot in 1335. Medieval pilgrims also travelled north on the coastal roads, where towns such as Vila do Conde and Viana do Castelo were already busy seaports. The modern revival is recent: the Portuguese Way is now the second most walked route.

### Regions crossed
From Lisbon the Central route runs up the **Ribatejo** and **Centro** regions past Santarém, Tomar and Coimbra to **Porto**, then through the green, vine-covered **Minho**, crossing the Minho river at **Valença**/**Tui** into **Galicia** (the province of Pontevedra, then A Coruña). The Coastal route follows the Atlantic through Vila do Conde and Viana do Castelo to A Guarda and Vigo. Portuguese and Galician are close cousins, and many pilgrims notice how easily the languages blend at the border. Highlights at the table: Porto's *francesinha* and tripe (*tripas à moda do Porto*), Minho's *vinho verde* and *caldo verde*, *bacalhau* in countless forms, and on the Galician side Padrón peppers (from the town on the route), Albariño wine and seafood of the Rías Baixas.

### Elevation and landscape
This is the gentlest of the long routes. Most of it is lowland farmland, eucalyptus and pine woods, river valleys and coastal paths. The one real climb on the Central route from Porto is the **Portela Grande de Labruja**, a forested pass of roughly 400 m between Ponte de Lima and Rubiães. The Coastal route stays close to sea level, with boardwalks, beaches and a few headlands. Expect cobblestones in towns and some stretches on paved roads; the coastal variant can be windy.

### Notable past travellers
- **Queen Isabel of Portugal** (1325): her pilgrimage and gifts to the cathedral are documented; her return on foot in 1335 is traditional.
- **King Manuel I of Portugal** (1502): made a documented pilgrimage and funded works along the way.
- **Giovanni Battista Confalonieri** (1594): an Italian cleric who wrote an account of the journey from Lisbon to Santiago.

### Notable stops
- **Lisbon**: the Sé (cathedral) is the traditional start for the full route.
- **Tomar**: the Templar castle and Convent of Christ, a World Heritage Site.
- **Coimbra**: Portugal's old university city, also a World Heritage Site, and the burial place of Queen Isabel at the Monastery of Santa Clara-a-Nova.
- **Porto**: the most popular start, with its cathedral and riverside Ribeira (historic centre listed by UNESCO).
- **Barcelos**: home of the legend of the **Barcelos rooster**, which crowed to save a falsely accused pilgrim (a legend close to that of Santo Domingo de la Calzada).
- **Ponte de Lima**: one of Portugal's oldest towns, with its long medieval and Roman-era bridge over the Lima.
- **Tui**: a fortress cathedral on the Spanish bank of the Minho, facing Valença.
- **Pontevedra**: the scallop-shell-shaped church of the Virxe Peregrina, dedicated to the Pilgrim Virgin.
- **Padrón**: where tradition says the boat bearing St James's body moored; the stone *pedrón* is kept under the altar of Santiago church.
- **Caldas de Reis**: a spa town with hot springs, where tradition places the birth of King Alfonso VII.
- **Vigo and A Guarda** (Coastal route): A Guarda's Monte de Santa Trega has a Celtic hillfort (*castro*) overlooking the mouth of the Minho.
