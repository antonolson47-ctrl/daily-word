The **Way of St Francis** (*Via di Francesco*) follows the hill paths of Tuscany, Umbria and Lazio that **Francis of Assisi** (1181/82–1226) walked as a preacher and a beggar. It joins the places of his life: **La Verna**, where he received the stigmata; **Gubbio**, where legend says he tamed the wolf; **Assisi**, his home town and the place of his tomb; the **Rieti valley**, where he made the first Christmas crib at **Greccio**; and **Rome**, where Pope Innocent III approved his way of life. The year **2026** is the **800th anniversary of his death** (3–4 October 1226).

## Good to know
- **Two halves.** The **northern way** runs La Verna – Assisi (about 8 to 10 days); the **southern way** runs Assisi – Rieti valley (about 8 days) and on to Rome (another 5 to 6). The whole way from La Verna to Rome is roughly 500 km.
- **Waymarks:** the yellow **Tau** and yellow-and-blue signs.
- **Credential:** order the official pilgrim credential from the Via di Francesco at least **3 weeks** ahead, or **2 months** if it must be posted outside Italy.
- **Testimonium:** for at least **100 km on foot** or **200 km by bike**, collected at the **Statio Peregrinorum**, Piazza Inferiore di San Francesco 2, Assisi (10:00–12:30 and 15:00–17:30; +39 075 819001). Every pilgrim also receives the **Chartula Peregrini**.
- **Pilgrim Mass** at the Basilica of St Francis, April to October: **18:00** on weekdays, **17:00** on Sundays.
- **Season:** April–June and September–October. Umbrian summers are hot and some stages (Città di Castello – Pietralunga, 31 km) are long and hilly with few services.

## Deep Dive

### Who was Francis?
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone was born in Assisi in **1181 or 1182**, son of a rich cloth merchant. After a year as a prisoner of war in Perugia and a long illness, he turned from his ambitions. Praying in the ruined chapel of **San Damiano** he heard the crucifix say "Francis, go and repair my house." Before the bishop of Assisi he gave back his clothes to his father and renounced his inheritance (1206). He embraced lepers, begged stones to rebuild churches, and from 1208 gathered brothers who lived the Gospel literally, in poverty. **Innocent III** approved their first rule by word of mouth in **1209/10**; **Honorius III** confirmed the final Rule in **1223**. With **St Clare** he founded the Poor Ladies (1212). He went to the Holy Land and met **Sultan al-Kamil** at Damietta (1219). He received the stigmata on La Verna in **1224**, wrote the **Canticle of the Creatures** while sick and almost blind, and died at the **Portiuncula** on the evening of **3 October 1226**. **Gregory IX** canonised him in **1228**. He is patron of Italy and of ecology.
</div>

### Legend and memory
<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legends.** The *Little Flowers of St Francis* (*Fioretti*, 14th century) collect the stories that made Francis loved: the **sermon to the birds** at Bevagna, the **wolf of Gubbio**, the "perfect joy" of being turned away at the door in the rain. They were written a century after his death and are devotional stories, not history, though many keep the flavour of the early brothers.
</div>

### The way in 2026
The 800th anniversary (the *Centenari Francescani*, 2023–2026) has brought record numbers. Francis's remains were exposed for public veneration from **22 February to 22 March 2026**, and more than 370,000 people came to pray before them (Vatican News).

### Praying the way
"Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:3, ESV). Francis took the Gospel at its word. Pilgrims often pray his *Canticle of the Creatures* along the way, or the prayer he said whenever he entered a church: "We adore you, Lord Jesus Christ, here and in all your churches throughout the world, and we bless you, because by your holy cross you have redeemed the world."
