After La Verna the way leaves Tuscany for Umbria. It follows the young Tiber past **Sansepolcro** and **Città di Castello**, climbs through lonely woods to **Pietralunga** and reaches **Gubbio**, the stone town of the wolf story, before the last long days to Assisi.

## Good to know
- **Stages:** Sansepolcro – Citerna 11.7 km; Citerna – Città di Castello 19.4 km; Città di Castello – Pietralunga 31 km; Pietralunga – Gubbio 26 km; Gubbio – San Pietro in Vigneto 16.3 km; San Pietro in Vigneto – Valfabbrica 21.9 km; Valfabbrica – Assisi 13.4 km.
- Near Sansepolcro, the hermitage of **Montecasale** is a short detour where Francis stayed.
- In **Gubbio**, the little church of **Santa Maria della Vittoria** (*La Vittorina*) marks where Francis is said to have met the wolf; the **Corsa dei Ceri** (15 May) honours St Ubaldo, the town's bishop and patron.
- The last stage enters Assisi on the slope beneath the Basilica, a famous first sight.

## Deep Dive

### Gubbio and Francis
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Early sources tell that after he renounced his inheritance in 1206, Francis went to Gubbio, where a friend gave him a tunic, and that he cared for lepers there. The friars later settled at La Vittorina.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** The *Fioretti* (chapter 21) tell of a fierce wolf that terrorised Gubbio. Francis went out to meet it, called it "Brother Wolf", and made a pact: the town would feed it and the wolf would harm no one. It lived among the people until it died of old age. The story is a parable of peace, not a historical record.
</div>
