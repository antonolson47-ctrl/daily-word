Assisi is where the way gathers. Every place in this small pink-stone town on Monte Subasio speaks of Francis and Clare: his tomb, her basilica, the chapel where Christ spoke from the cross, the little church where he died, and the caves where the brothers prayed. Assisi's Franciscan sites have been a **UNESCO World Heritage** site since **2000**.

## Good to know
- **Basilica of St Francis:** Upper Church with **Giotto's** cycle of the life of Francis; Lower Church with frescoes by **Cimabue**, **Simone Martini** and **Pietro Lorenzetti**; the **tomb** in the crypt. Dress modestly; silence is kept in the Lower Church.
- **Pilgrim desk:** the **Statio Peregrinorum**, Piazza Inferiore di San Francesco 2, issues the Testimonium (10:00–12:30, 15:00–17:30).
- **Basilica of Santa Chiara:** tomb of St Clare and the **San Damiano crucifix** that spoke to Francis.
- **San Damiano:** 15 minutes' walk below the walls, still a friary; Clare lived here for 41 years.
- **Santa Maria degli Angeli:** the great basilica in the plain enfolds the tiny **Portiuncula** and the **Transitus** chapel where Francis died.
- **Eremo delle Carceri:** 4 km uphill on Monte Subasio, in holm-oak woods.
- **Shrine of the Renunciation** (Santa Maria Maggiore): where Francis stripped before the bishop; **St Carlo Acutis** (canonised 2025) is buried here.

## Deep Dive

### The Basilica and the tomb
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Gregory IX laid the first stone the day after the canonisation, in **1228**, on the "Hill of Hell" where criminals were executed, renamed the Hill of Paradise. **Brother Elias** brought the body here in **1230** and hid it so well that it was only found in **1818**. The earthquake of **26 September 1997** brought down part of the Upper Church vault and killed four people; it was reopened in 1999.
</div>

### St Clare
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** **Clare Offreduccio** (1193/94–1253) fled her noble family on Palm Sunday night **1212** to join Francis at the Portiuncula. She led the Poor Ladies at San Damiano, and her own Rule, the first written by a woman, was approved two days before her death in **1253**. She was canonised in **1255**.
</div>

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** It is told that when Saracen soldiers of Frederick II climbed the walls of San Damiano (1240), Clare, sick, had herself carried to the door with the Blessed Sacrament, and the soldiers fled. The story is in her early *Legend* and in the canonisation process.
</div>

### The Portiuncula
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Francis repaired this little chapel of St Mary of the Angels, the brothers' first home, around 1208. Here he understood his call on hearing the Gospel of the sending of the disciples, received Clare, held the chapters of the brothers, and died, laid on the bare ground, on **3 October 1226**. The **Portiuncula Indulgence** ("Pardon of Assisi") is kept on **2 August**. The basilica over it was built 1569–1679.
</div>
