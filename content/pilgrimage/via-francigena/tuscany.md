In Italy the road becomes busy with pilgrims. After the rice fields of the Po plain and the crossing of the Apennines, the Via Francigena enters **Tuscany**: walled towns, Romanesque churches, cypress-lined white roads and three of Italy's great pilgrim cities, **Lucca**, **San Gimignano** and **Siena**.

## Good to know
- **Route:** Aosta – Ivrea – **Vercelli** – Mortara – **Pavia** – the **Po** crossing (a ferry at Soprarivo, the *Transitum Padi*, booked in advance, or a road detour) – **Piacenza** – **Fidenza** – the **Cisa Pass** (1,041 m) – **Pontremoli** – **Sarzana** – Massa – **Lucca** – Altopascio – **San Miniato** – Gambassi – **San Gimignano** – **Monteriggioni** – **Siena**.
- **Pilgrim hostels** are frequent, many run by parishes and confraternities, often for a donation. Book ahead in May, June and September.
- **Heat:** in summer start at dawn. Water fountains are fewer in Tuscany than you might expect.
- **Dress** for churches: shoulders and knees covered.

## Deep Dive

### Lucca and the Volto Santo
The **Volto Santo** ("Holy Face"), a large wooden crucifix of Christ robed and crowned, has been venerated in Lucca's cathedral of **San Martino** since at least the 11th century. Medieval pilgrims came to Lucca specially to see it, and King William II of England swore "by the Holy Face of Lucca".

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** The crucifix was said to have been carved by **Nicodemus**, who helped bury Jesus, with the face finished by angels; hidden for centuries, it was put on an unmanned boat that drifted to the Italian coast, and oxen pulling it unguided brought it to Lucca in **742**.
</div>

<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** A radiocarbon study published in **2020** dated the wood to between the late 8th and the 9th century, making it one of the oldest wooden sculptures in the West, and far older than art historians had believed. Every **13 September**, the *Luminara di Santa Croce*, Lucca lights its streets with candles for a procession of the Volto Santo.
</div>

### San Gimignano and Monteriggioni
**San Gimignano**, with its medieval towers, grew rich on pilgrims and saffron; the **Collegiata** has frescoes of the Old and New Testament and the chapel of **St Fina**. **Monteriggioni**, a ring of walls and fourteen towers built from **1213** by Siena, is the sight Dante compared to the giants of hell (*Inferno* XXXI).

### Siena
Siena's **Duomo**, with its striped marble, inlaid floor and Pisano pulpit, was the home church of **St Catherine of Siena** (1347–1380), Doctor of the Church and co-patron of Europe, whose head is kept in the Basilica of San Domenico. The great pilgrim hospital of **Santa Maria della Scala**, opposite the cathedral, cared for travellers from about the 11th century until the 1990s, and is now a museum.

### Stop by stop
#### Pavia
In the basilica of **San Pietro in Ciel d'Oro** lies **St Augustine**, in a marble Gothic tomb (the *Arca*), his relics brought from Sardinia in the 8th century. Boethius is buried in the crypt. Pavia was the Lombard capital, a key stop for Sigeric.

#### The Cisa Pass
The Apennine crossing at **1,041 m** has a small sanctuary of Our Lady of the Guard at the top. The descent leads to **Pontremoli**, whose museum holds the mysterious prehistoric *stele statues* of the Lunigiana.

<figure class=photo><picture><source srcset="/static/art/st2-vi-tuscan-the-cisa-pass-600.webp 600w,/static/art/st2-vi-tuscan-the-cisa-pass.webp 1280w" sizes="(max-width:760px) 100vw,760px" type="image/webp"><img src="/static/art/st2-vi-tuscan-the-cisa-pass.jpg" alt="The Cisa Pass" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px"></picture><figcaption class=src>The Cisa Pass · Photo: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3APasso-della-Cisa-2012.JPG">Passo-della-Cisa-2012</a> · Bjørn Christian Tørrissen · CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons</figcaption></figure>

#### San Miniato
A hill town on the ridge between Pisa and Florence. Sigeric's stage *Sce Dionisii* was nearby. Its Rocca tower, rebuilt after 1944, looks over the Arno valley; the town is famous for white truffles in November.

<figure class=photo><picture><source srcset="/static/art/st2-vi-tuscan-san-miniato-600.webp 600w,/static/art/st2-vi-tuscan-san-miniato.webp 1280w" sizes="(max-width:760px) 100vw,760px" type="image/webp"><img src="/static/art/st2-vi-tuscan-san-miniato.jpg" alt="San Miniato" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px"></picture><figcaption class=src>San Miniato · Photo: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ASan_Miniato%2C_Tuscany_-_2009.jpg">San Miniato, Tuscany - 2009</a> · Arun Marsh · CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons</figcaption></figure>

#### Monteriggioni
A perfect ring of walls and 14 towers on a hill, built by Siena in 1213–19. Dante compared its towers to the giants circling the pit of hell (*Inferno* 31). Pilgrims enter by the Porta Franca, the gate that faced France.
