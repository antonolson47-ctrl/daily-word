The last two weeks run through the **Val d'Orcia**, a UNESCO landscape of rolling hills and cypresses, and then into **Lazio**: volcanic lakes, Etruscan towns carved in tuff and, at the end, the sight of the dome of St Peter's. The 100 km needed for the Testimonium begin roughly at **Viterbo**.

## Good to know
- **Route:** Siena – Ponte d'Arbia – **San Quirico d'Orcia** – **Radicofani** – Acquapendente – **Bolsena** – **Montefiascone** – **Viterbo** – Vetralla – **Sutri** – Campagnano – La Storta – **Monte Mario** – **St Peter's**.
- **Testimonium:** walking from Viterbo (about 110 km) is the classic minimum. Have two stamps a day in the credential.
- **Arriving in Rome:** enter St Peter's Square, follow the signed *Percorso Oranti* from the obelisk and ask at the Basilica's reception desk for the Testimonium. Security checks apply; large packs are not allowed in the basilica (ask about the cloakroom). To reserve the Testimonium, e-mail the reception point (accoglienza@fsp.va).
- **Rome itself:** see our page [Rome and the Seven Churches](/pilgrimage/rome/).

## Deep Dive

### The miracle of Bolsena
"I am the living bread that came down from heaven" (John 6:51, ESV).

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Tradition.** In **1263** a priest from Bohemia, **Peter of Prague**, who doubted that Christ is truly present in the Eucharist, stopped at Bolsena on his way to Rome. As he said Mass in the church of **Santa Cristina**, blood flowed from the Host onto the altar linen, the **corporal**. Pope **Urban IV**, then at nearby Orvieto, had the corporal brought to him, and in **1264** extended the feast of **Corpus Christi** to the whole Church, with texts by **St Thomas Aquinas**.
</div>

<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Urban IV's bull *Transiturus* (1264) does not mention Bolsena, and the earliest surviving record of the story is the enamelled reliquary made for the corporal in **1337–38**; the 1913 *Catholic Encyclopedia* already noted this. The devotion is ancient all the same. The corporal is kept in the **Cathedral of Orvieto**, in the Chapel of the Corporal; at Bolsena pilgrims venerate the stones said to be stained with the blood, in the Basilica of Santa Cristina, built over the tomb of the virgin martyr **Cristina**.
</div>

### Viterbo, the city of the popes
In the 13th century Viterbo was often the popes' residence. After the death of Clement IV in 1268 the cardinals here could not agree for almost three years, the longest election in history, until the townspeople locked them in the **Palazzo dei Papi**, cut their food and took off the roof. The result, Gregory X, brought in the strict rules of the *conclave* ("with a key").

### Sutri and the last day
**Sutri** has an amphitheatre cut out of the living tuff and an Etruscan rock tomb that became the church of the **Madonna del Parto**. On the last day pilgrims climb **Monte Mario**, the medieval *Mons Gaudii*, and see St Peter's below them. They come down through the city to the basilica and the tomb of the Apostle.
