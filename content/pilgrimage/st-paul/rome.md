Paul had written to the Romans that he longed to see them (Romans 1:11). He arrived as a prisoner. Landing at **Puteoli** near Naples, he walked up the **Appian Way**, and believers came out to meet him at the **Forum of Appius** and **Three Taverns**: "On seeing them, Paul thanked God and took courage" (Acts 28:15, ESV). Acts ends with Paul spending two whole years in Rome in his own rented lodging, under guard, "proclaiming the kingdom of God… with all boldness and without hindrance" (Acts 28:31).

## Good to know
- **Tre Fontane Abbey** (*Abbazia delle Tre Fontane*) is free, run by Trappist monks; the monastery shop sells their eucalyptus liqueur, chocolate and beer. Local buses, or about 30 minutes' walk from Metro B *Laurentina*.
- **St Paul's tomb** is at the basilica of [St Paul Outside the Walls](/pilgrimage/rome/st-paul-outside-the-walls/), 2.5 km north.
- **The Mamertine Prison** (*Carcere Mamertino*) by the Roman Forum is open daily with a paid multimedia visit.
- Rome's [Seven Churches walk](/pilgrimage/rome/) includes Paul's basilica as its second station.

## Deep Dive

### The end of the story
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Acts stops before Paul's death. The earliest sources outside the Bible, **1 Clement** (c. AD 96) and later **Eusebius**, say he was martyred in Rome, and since he was a Roman citizen, by **beheading**, not crucifixion. Around AD 200 the Roman priest **Gaius** wrote that he could show the "trophies" of the apostles, Peter's on the Vatican and Paul's on the road to Ostia. The traditional date is under **Nero**, in the years after the fire of AD 64.
</div>

The letter 2 Timothy, presented as written from prison as death approached, contains the words many take as his farewell: "I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith" (2 Timothy 4:7, ESV).

### Tre Fontane
<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Tradition.** At *Aquae Salviae*, on the old Via Laurentina, Paul was beheaded; his head bounced three times, and at each spot a spring burst out: the "three fountains." The story is medieval, but the place was a cult site by the 5th–6th century.
</div>

- **San Paolo alle Tre Fontane**: the small church over the three springs (rebuilt in 1599 by **Giacomo della Porta**), with three shrines in a row and, in a corner, the **pillar** to which, by tradition, Paul was tied. Ancient Roman mosaic floors from Ostia lie in the nave.
- **Santi Vincenzo e Anastasio**: the Trappist abbey church (12th–13th century), austere and beautiful.
- **Santa Maria Scala Coeli**: where, the story goes, St Bernard saw souls ascending to heaven on a ladder while saying Mass; it was rebuilt by della Porta too.

### The Mamertine Prison
Beneath the church of San Giuseppe dei Falegnami, by the Forum, are two ancient chambers: the upper prison and the lower **Tullianum**, Rome's state dungeon. A medieval tradition says **Peter and Paul** were held here and that Peter baptized his jailers with water from a spring that rose miraculously. There is no ancient evidence for the tradition, but the dungeon itself is genuinely ancient, and the site is visited by many pilgrims.
