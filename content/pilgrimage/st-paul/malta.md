After fourteen days adrift in a storm, the grain ship carrying Paul, 276 people in all, ran aground off an island. Everyone reached land. "Afterward we learned that the island was called Malta. The native people showed us unusual kindness" (Acts 28:1–2, ESV). Paul stayed three months. To the Maltese, he is their father in the faith, and the shipwreck is the founding story of one of the most Catholic countries in the world.

## Good to know
- **Getting there:** Malta International Airport (MLA) is 20–30 minutes by bus or taxi from Valletta and Rabat.
- **St Paul's Grotto** and the **Wignacourt Museum** in Rabat (paid); **St Paul's Catacombs**, Rabat (Heritage Malta, paid); **St Paul's Shipwreck Church**, Valletta (free).
- **St Paul's Islands** can be seen from the Mistra or Xemxija shore, or from summer boat trips out of Bugibba.
- **Feast of St Paul's Shipwreck:** **10 February**, a public holiday with a great procession in Valletta.

## Deep Dive

### What Acts tells
Sailing late in the season, against Paul's warning, the ship was caught off Crete by a north-easterly gale (the *Euraquilo*), was driven west and drifted for two weeks. The sailors sounded "twenty fathoms," then "fifteen," cast four anchors from the stern, and at daybreak tried to beach the ship in a bay; it struck a reef and broke apart. On shore, as Paul gathered sticks for a fire, a **viper** fastened on his hand; the islanders expected him to swell up and die, and when he did not, said he was a god. **Publius**, "the chief man of the island," welcomed them; Paul healed his father of fever and dysentery, and then others came to be healed (Acts 28:7–10).

<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Luke's account of the voyage is famous among sailors and historians for its precise nautical detail. An inscription found in Malta calls a leading official *prōtos Melitaiōn*, "first of the Maltese," the same unusual title Acts gives Publius. The exact place of the wreck is debated: **St Paul's Bay** and St Paul's Islands are the traditional site, and other proposals include St Thomas Bay and Mellieħa. A few scholars have argued for Kefalonia instead of Malta, but most still favour Malta.
</div>

### What to see
- **St Paul's Grotto, Rabat**: a cave under the church of St Paul where, by tradition, Paul lived or was imprisoned during his three months. **John Paul II** prayed here in **1990** and **Benedict XVI** in **2010**. Above it, the **Wignacourt Museum** has Knights' period treasures and wartime shelters.
- **St Paul's Catacombs, Rabat**: a large complex of underground Roman and early Christian burial places (3rd–4th centuries), with agape tables carved in the rock, managed by Heritage Malta.
- **St Paul's Shipwreck Church, Valletta**: a 16th–17th-century church with a gilded wooden statue of Paul by **Melchiorre Cafà** (1659), a relic of his **wrist bone**, and part of the **column** on which, by tradition, he was beheaded in Rome.
- **St Paul's Bay**, with the little church of *San Pawl Milqi* ("St Paul welcomed"), on the traditional site of Publius's villa, where Roman farm remains were excavated.
- **Mdina Cathedral**, dedicated to St Paul, on the traditional site of Publius's house.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Legend.** Maltese tradition says Paul made **Publius** the island's first bishop and that, since the viper, **no snake in Malta is venomous**, which happens to be true today. Pious lore also held that stone dug from St Paul's Grotto had healing power; pilgrims carried it home for centuries, yet, it was said, the cave never grew larger.
</div>
