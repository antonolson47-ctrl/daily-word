No one carried the Gospel further, or wrote more of the New Testament, than **Paul of Tarsus**. A strict Pharisee who hunted Christians, he met the risen Christ on the road to Damascus and spent the next thirty years walking and sailing some 15,000 km around the eastern Mediterranean, founding churches and writing them letters that are still read every Sunday. The Lord called him "a chosen instrument of mine to carry my name before the Gentiles" (Acts 9:15, ESV). This guide follows him from **Damascus** to **Rome**, place by place.

## Good to know
- **Two sources, read together.** Our knowledge of Paul's travels comes from the **Acts of the Apostles** (written by Luke, probably in the 80s) and from **Paul's own letters** (the 50s). They mostly agree; where they differ, scholars weigh them, and we flag it.
- **Countries today:** Syria, Turkey, Cyprus, Greece, Malta, Italy and Israel. Turkey and Greece hold most of the sites; group tours often do "Seven Churches and Paul" in Turkey, or "Footsteps of Paul" in northern Greece (Kavala–Philippi–Thessaloniki–Veria) plus Athens and Corinth.
- **Safety:** Syria and southern Lebanon carry serious travel warnings. **Check your government's advice** (e.g. the US State Department) before planning Damascus. Antakya (Antioch) is rebuilding after the February 2023 earthquakes.
- **Season:** spring (April–May) and autumn (late September–October) are best for walking the ruins; summers at Ephesus and Corinth are very hot.
- **Bible references** on these pages link to the ESV text. Quotations marked ESV are from the English Standard Version.

## Deep Dive

### Who was Paul?
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Paul was born in **Tarsus** in Cilicia, probably in the first decade AD, a Jew of the tribe of Benjamin and, according to Acts, a Roman citizen by birth. His Hebrew name was **Saul**; **Paul** (*Paulus*) was his Roman name. He trained as a Pharisee in Jerusalem "at the feet of Gamaliel" (Acts 22:3) and worked with his hands as a tentmaker or leatherworker. He approved of the stoning of [St Stephen](/saints/stephen/) and persecuted the young Church, until, around **AD 33–36**, his encounter with Christ near Damascus turned him into its greatest missionary.
</div>

### A timeline (dates are approximate)

|---|---|---|
| c. 33–36 | Conversion on the road to Damascus; time in "Arabia" (Nabataea) and Damascus | Acts 9; Galatians 1:15–17 |
| c. 36–45 | First Jerusalem visit to Peter; years in Tarsus and Cilicia; called to Antioch by Barnabas | Galatians 1:18–21; Acts 11:25–26 |
| c. 46–48 | **First journey:** Cyprus and southern Galatia (Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe) | Acts 13–14 |
| c. 49 | **Council of Jerusalem:** Gentile converts need not be circumcised | Acts 15; Galatians 2 |
| c. 49–52 | **Second journey:** into Europe: Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens, 18 months in Corinth | Acts 15:36–18:22 |
| c. 53–57 | **Third journey:** nearly three years in Ephesus; Macedonia and Greece again; farewell at Miletus | Acts 18:23–21:17 |
| c. 57–59 | Arrest in the Temple at Jerusalem; two years in prison at Caesarea; appeal to Caesar | Acts 21–26 |
| c. 59–60 | **Voyage to Rome:** storm and shipwreck on Malta | Acts 27–28 |
| c. 60–62 | Two years under house arrest in Rome | Acts 28:30–31 |
| c. 64–67 | Martyrdom by beheading in Rome under Nero, by tradition at Tre Fontane | 1 Clement 5; Eusebius |

One firm anchor for this chronology is an inscription found at **Delphi** naming **Gallio** as proconsul of Achaia around **AD 51–52**, the very official before whom Paul was tried in Corinth (Acts 18:12). See [Corinth](/pilgrimage/st-paul/corinth/).

### The letters
Thirteen letters in the New Testament carry Paul's name. Seven are accepted by almost all scholars as his own: **Romans, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians and Philemon**. 1 Thessalonians, written from Corinth around AD 50–51, is probably **the oldest book of the New Testament**. The authorship of the others (Ephesians, Colossians, 2 Thessalonians, and the Pastoral letters to Timothy and Titus) is debated; the Church receives all thirteen as Scripture.

### The journeys in brief
1. **First journey (Acts 13–14).** Sent out from **Antioch** with **Barnabas** and young **John Mark**, Paul crossed to **Cyprus**, where the proconsul **Sergius Paulus** believed at **Paphos**, then to the Anatolian highlands. At **Lystra** the crowd took Barnabas and Paul for Zeus and Hermes, then stoned Paul and left him for dead.
2. **Second journey (Acts 15:36–18:22).** With **Silas**, and soon **Timothy** and **Luke**, Paul crossed from **Troas** after a vision of a man of Macedonia, founding the first church in Europe at **Philippi**, then on to **Thessalonica**, **Berea**, **Athens** and **Corinth**.
3. **Third journey (Acts 18:23–21:17).** Based at **Ephesus**, the great city of Asia, for nearly three years, he wrote to Corinth and Galatia, then gathered a collection for the poor of Jerusalem, wrote **Romans** from Corinth, and said a tearful farewell to the elders of Ephesus at **Miletus**.
4. **Voyage to Rome (Acts 27–28).** A prisoner appealing to Caesar, he sailed in autumn, was caught by the north-easterly gale (*Euraquilo*), drifted fourteen days and was wrecked on **Malta**, reaching **Rome** the next spring.

<div class=legend markdown=1>
**Tradition.** Outside the Bible, early Christian writers add to the story. **Clement of Rome** (c. AD 96) says Paul reached "the limits of the West," which some take to mean **Spain** (see Romans 15:24). The apocryphal *Acts of Paul and Thecla* (2nd century) gives the only physical description: "a man small in size, bald-headed, bandy-legged… with eyebrows meeting and nose somewhat hooked, full of friendliness." Tradition places his death in Rome on the same day as Peter's, celebrated together on **29 June**.
</div>
