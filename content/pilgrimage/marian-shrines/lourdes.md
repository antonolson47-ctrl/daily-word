"Que soy era Immaculada Councepciou", "I am the Immaculate Conception". With these words, in the dialect of the Pyrenees, the Lady of the grotto of **Massabielle** gave her name to **Bernadette Soubirous**, a poor, sickly, illiterate girl of fourteen. Since then Lourdes has become the great shrine of the **sick**, visited by millions each year. In **September 2026** **Pope Leo XIV** came here on his apostolic journey to France.

## Good to know
- **The Grotto** is open day and night. Pilgrims touch the rock, light candles and drink from or wash at the **fountains** of the spring Bernadette uncovered.
- **The baths** (*piscines*): pilgrims can be immersed or washed in the spring water with the help of volunteers. Times vary by season; ask at the sanctuary.
- **Daily processions** (April–October): the **Eucharistic procession** with the blessing of the sick in the afternoon and the **Marian torchlight procession** at 21:00, singing the *Lourdes Ave Maria*.

- **Churches:** the **Basilica of the Immaculate Conception** (upper), the **Rosary Basilica** with its mosaics, the **crypt**, and the underground **Basilica of St Pius X**, which holds 25,000.
- **In town:** Bernadette's **cachot**, the former prison cell where her destitute family lived, her birthplace at the **Boly Mill**, and the parish church with the font where she was baptized.
- **Getting there:** Lourdes station is on the line from Toulouse and Bordeaux; Tarbes-Lourdes-Pyrénées airport is 10 km away. The **Accueil Notre-Dame** looks after sick pilgrims.

## Deep Dive

### The apparitions
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** On **11 February 1858**, Bernadette (born 1844) was gathering firewood by the river Gave with her sister and a friend when she saw, in a niche of the rock, "a small young lady" dressed in white, with a blue sash and a yellow rose on each foot, and a rosary. She saw her **18 times** until **16 July**. On 25 February, at the Lady's word, she scraped at the ground and a **spring** appeared; it still flows. On **25 March**, the feast of the Annunciation, the Lady said, "I am the Immaculate Conception", words Bernadette did not understand, four years after Pope Pius IX had defined the dogma. The Lady asked for **prayer and penance for sinners**, and that "people come here in procession and a chapel be built". After an inquiry, Bishop **Laurence** of Tarbes declared in **1862** that the apparitions were authentic.
</div>

### St Bernadette
Bernadette never claimed any glory: "The Blessed Virgin used me like a broom. What do you do with a broom when you have finished sweeping? You put it back in its place." She entered the **Sisters of Charity of Nevers** in **1866** and died there, after long illness, on **16 April 1879**, at 35. She was canonized in **1933**. Her body lies in a glass shrine in the convent chapel at **Nevers**.

### Healings
<div class=history markdown=1>
**History.** Of more than **7,000** healings reported at Lourdes since 1858, **72** have so far been recognised as miraculous by the Church, after examination by the sanctuary's **Medical Bureau** and the **International Medical Committee of Lourdes**. The 72nd, proclaimed on **16 April 2025**, was **Antonia Raco**, an Italian woman with primary lateral sclerosis, healed during a pilgrimage in **2009**. The Church speaks of a miracle only when a cure is sudden, complete, lasting and medically unexplained; most pilgrims speak instead of the "miracle" of peace, faith and strength to bear illness.
</div>

### Stop by stop
#### The Grotto of Massabielle
The rock cave by the Gave river where Bernadette saw the Lady 18 times in 1858. Pilgrims pass under the rock, touching it, and drink from taps of the spring she uncovered.

<figure class=photo><picture><source srcset="/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-grotto-of-massabielle-600.webp 600w,/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-grotto-of-massabielle.webp 1280w" sizes="(max-width:760px) 100vw,760px" type="image/webp"><img src="/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-grotto-of-massabielle.jpg" alt="The Grotto of Massabielle" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px"></picture><figcaption class=src>The Grotto of Massabielle · Photo: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGrotte_de_Massabielle_2019.jpg">Grotte de Massabielle 2019</a> · Thomon · CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons</figcaption></figure>

#### The Basilicas
Three churches are stacked above the Grotto: the Crypt (1866), the Upper Basilica of the Immaculate Conception (1871) and the Rosary Basilica (1889), with its Byzantine mosaics.

<figure class=photo><picture><source srcset="/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-basilicas-600.webp 600w,/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-basilicas.webp 1280w" sizes="(max-width:760px) 100vw,760px" type="image/webp"><img src="/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-basilicas.jpg" alt="The Basilicas" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px"></picture><figcaption class=src>The Basilicas · Photo: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ABasilique_de_l%27Immacul%C3%A9e-Conception_%28Lourdes%29.jpg">Basilique de l'Immaculée-Conception (Lourdes)</a> · Van Der Meulen Christofle · CC0, via Wikimedia Commons</figcaption></figure>

#### The Torchlight Procession
Every evening from April to October, thousands carry candles round the esplanade singing the *Lourdes Ave Maria*, in many languages.

<figure class=photo><picture><source srcset="/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-torchlight-procession-600.webp 600w,/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-torchlight-procession.webp 1280w" sizes="(max-width:760px) 100vw,760px" type="image/webp"><img src="/static/art/st2-ma-lourde-the-torchlight-procession.jpg" alt="The Torchlight Procession" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px"></picture><figcaption class=src>The Torchlight Procession · Photo: <a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ASoir_de_p%C3%A8lerinage_%C3%A0_Lourdes_01.jpg">Soir de pèlerinage à Lourdes 01</a> · Celeda · CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons</figcaption></figure>

#### The Cachot
The tiny former jail where Bernadette's destitute family lived in one room in 1858, a few streets from the sanctuary. It explains her poverty better than any book.
