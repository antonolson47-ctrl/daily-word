---
title: The Ministry of Jesus: A Timeline
subtitle: From the Jordan to the Mount of Olives, with the places you can still visit
---
The Gospels do not give calendar dates, so every date below is an **approximate scholarly estimate** (marked c.). Most historians place Jesus' birth about **6–4 BC**, before Herod the Great died, and his public ministry in about **AD 27/28–30 or 33**. John's Gospel mentions at least three Passovers, which suggests a ministry of two to three years; the other Gospels could fit a shorter span. Where the order of events differs between the Gospels, we follow the most common harmony and say so.

<div class=timeline markdown=1>

### c. 6–4 BC · Birth in Bethlehem
Born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of King Herod (Matthew 2:1; Luke 2:1–7). Shepherds and Magi come to worship; the family flees to Egypt and later settles in Nazareth. → [Bethlehem](/pilgrimage/holy-land/bethlehem/) · [Nazareth](/pilgrimage/holy-land/nazareth/)

### c. AD 8 · The boy in the Temple
At twelve, Jesus stays behind in the Temple, "in my Father's house" (Luke 2:41–52). → [Jerusalem](/pilgrimage/holy-land/jerusalem/)

### c. AD 28 · Baptism in the Jordan
Luke dates John the Baptist's preaching to "the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius" (Luke 3:1), about AD 28–29. Jesus is baptised; the Spirit descends and the Father speaks (Mark 1:9–11).

### c. AD 28 · Forty days in the wilderness
Jesus fasts and is tempted in the Judean desert (Matthew 4:1–11). Tradition places it on the Mount of Temptation above Jericho.

### c. AD 28 · First disciples and the wedding at Cana
Andrew, Peter, Philip and Nathanael follow him (John 1:35–51). At Cana he turns water into wine, "the first of his signs" (John 2:1–11). → [The Jesus Trail](/pilgrimage/holy-land/jesus-trail/)

### c. AD 28–29 · Galilee: Capernaum becomes home
Rejected at Nazareth (Luke 4:16–30), Jesus makes Capernaum his base. He calls fishermen from their nets, heals Peter's mother-in-law and teaches in the synagogue (Mark 1:16–34). → [Sea of Galilee](/pilgrimage/holy-land/galilee/)

### c. AD 29 · The Sermon on the Mount
The Beatitudes, the Lord's Prayer and the call to love enemies (Matthew 5–7). Tradition places it on the hill above Tabgha. → [Verses of the Lord's Prayer](/verses/)

### c. AD 29 · Parables, storms and the Twelve
Jesus chooses the Twelve (Mark 3:13–19), teaches in parables by the lake (Mark 4), calms the storm and raises Jairus's daughter (Mark 4:35–5:43).

### c. AD 29 · Feeding the five thousand
The only miracle, apart from the Resurrection, told in all four Gospels (Mark 6:30–44; John 6). Tabgha remembers it with its famous loaves-and-fish mosaic.

### c. AD 29 · Caesarea Philippi and the Transfiguration
Peter confesses, "You are the Christ" (Matthew 16:13–20). Days later Jesus is transfigured on a high mountain, by tradition Mount Tabor (Mark 9:2–8).

### c. AD 29–30 · The road to Jerusalem
Jesus "set his face to go to Jerusalem" (Luke 9:51). On the way: the Good Samaritan, the Prodigal Son, Zacchaeus in Jericho. At Bethany he raises Lazarus (John 11). → [St Martha](/saints/martha/)

### c. AD 30 (or 33) · Holy Week
**Palm Sunday:** entry into Jerusalem. **The days after:** the cleansing of the Temple and teaching in its courts. **Thursday:** the Last Supper, Gethsemane and the arrest. **Friday:** trial before Pilate, crucifixion and burial. → [Via Dolorosa](/pilgrimage/holy-land/via-dolorosa/) · [Jerusalem](/pilgrimage/holy-land/jerusalem/)

<div class=history markdown=1>**Dating the Passion.** Pontius Pilate governed Judea AD 26–36. The Gospels place the crucifixion on a Friday at Passover; astronomical calculations make **7 April AD 30** and **3 April AD 33** the two most widely proposed dates.</div>

### The third day · Resurrection
The empty tomb and appearances to Mary Magdalene, the women, Peter, the Twelve and "more than five hundred brothers at one time" (1 Corinthians 15:3–8), and by the Sea of Galilee (John 21). → [St Mary Magdalene](/saints/mary-magdalene/)

### Forty days later · Ascension
From the Mount of Olives near Bethany, Jesus is taken up (Luke 24:50–53; Acts 1:9–11).

### Fifty days after Easter · Pentecost
The Spirit comes on the disciples in Jerusalem; Peter preaches and about three thousand are baptised (Acts 2). The Church's mission begins, and with it the roads this site follows. → [Footsteps of St Paul](/pilgrimage/st-paul/) · [St Peter](/saints/peter/)

</div>
