Verse a Day #21 · November 1, 2026

Mark 1:15

Alignment: Very close

Setting the scene

Galilee, around AD 28. John the Baptist has just been arrested by Herod Antipas, and Mark tells us that "after that John was put in prison, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God" (Mark 1:14). Mark is the shortest and fastest of the four Gospels: no birth story, no Sermon on the Mount yet. This verse is the very first thing Jesus says in Mark's Gospel, a one-line summary of his whole message, spoken in the fishing towns around the Sea of Galilee just before he calls Simon, Andrew, James and John from their nets.

Four witnesses

Greek · SBLGNT
καὶ λέγων ὅτι Πεπλήρωται ὁ καιρὸς καὶ ἤγγικεν ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ Θεοῦ· μετανοεῖτε καὶ πιστεύετε ἐν τῷ εὐαγγελίῳ.
kai legōn hoti Peplērōtai ho kairos kai ēngiken hē basileia tou Theou; metanoeite kai pisteuete en tō euangeliō.
Latin · Vulgate
et dicens: Quoniam impletum est tempus, et appropinquavit regnum Dei: pœnitemini, et credite Evangelio.
KJV
And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.
ESV
and saying, "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel."

Key words

  • peplērōtai ho kairos → impletum est tempus → the time is fulfilled. Perfect passive of plēroō, "to fill up." The time has been filled to the brim and stays full. Kairos is not clock time (chronos) but the appointed, decisive moment.
  • ēngiken → appropinquavit → is at hand. Another perfect tense, of engizō, "to come near." The kingdom has drawn near and is now standing close, within reach.
  • basileia tou Theou → regnum Dei → the kingdom of God. Mark's phrase for what Matthew usually calls "the kingdom of heaven": God's reign arriving in the person of Jesus.
  • metanoeite → pœnitemini → repent ye. Literally "change your mind," a turning of the whole person. A present imperative: keep turning.
  • pisteuete en tō euangeliō → credite Evangelio → believe the gospel. Euangelion means "good news." Only here in the Gospels does Jesus say to believe "in the gospel," and Jerome keeps it simply as "believe the Gospel."

Alignment

Very close. The Greek editions on Bible Hub (Nestle 1904, Westcott–Hort, the Byzantine Majority Text, both Textus Receptus editions) agree word for word, differing only in accents and punctuation; Tischendorf alone leaves out the opening "and saying." The Vulgate follows the Greek clause by clause. The KJV's "believe the gospel" follows the Latin and Tyndale's tradition, while the ESV's "believe in the gospel" is closer to the Greek "en."

The story

Compare this verse with Matthew 4:17, where the same Greek word metanoeite became "pænitentiam agite" ("do penance") in Jerome's Vulgate. Here in Mark, Jerome chose a different Latin word, "pœnitemini" ("repent," "be sorry"). That difference mattered 1,100 years later: Erasmus's new Latin translation and Martin Luther's first of the 95 Theses (1517) both argued that metanoia means an inward change of heart, not only an act of penance. Mark 1:15 shows that Jerome himself could translate it either way.

Dig Deeper: Roots in Hebrew & Jewish Sources

Every phrase in Jesus' first sentence in Mark would have rung bells for Jewish listeners. "The time is fulfilled" and "the kingdom" echo Daniel 7:22, where "the time came, and the holy ones took possession of the kingdom" (in Aramaic, "zimna meta," the time arrived). "Gospel" (euangelion) is rooted in Isaiah 52:7, the herald (mevasser) running across the mountains "announcing good news" and telling Zion, "Your God reigns!" Many scholars see Jesus taking up exactly that announcement. And "repent" carries the Hebrew idea of teshuvah, "turning" or "returning" to God, the call of all the prophets; the Hebrew translation of Mark on Bible Hub renders it "shuvu," "return." A copy of Isaiah 52 survives in the Great Isaiah Scroll from Qumran (1QIsaᵃ), a pre-Christian Dead Sea Scroll, so these words were being read and copied in Jesus' own century; it is described here but not linked, because its official online viewer couldn't be reached to check today. Much later, the Mishnah (about AD 200) keeps a rabbi's saying that "one hour in repentance and good deeds in this world" is more precious than all the life of the world to come. These links are background study tools, not Scripture, and including them is not an endorsement of any later theology. Keep the dates straight: Isaiah and Daniel are Hebrew Bible (Tanakh), written before Christ; the Isaiah Scroll is a pre-Christian copy; the Mishnah was compiled about 170 years after Jesus.

Where to dig

  1. Isaiah 52:7 in Hebrew and English, on Sefaria. The herald on the mountains bringing good news and announcing "Your God reigns!", the Old Testament root of "gospel" and "kingdom."
  2. Daniel 7:22 in Aramaic and English, on Sefaria. The vision where "the time came" and God's holy ones received the kingdom, close to Jesus' "the time is fulfilled."
  3. Pirkei Avot 4:17, on Sefaria. The Mishnah's later saying on the value of one hour of repentance (teshuvah) and good deeds in this world.
  4. metanoeō in the Blue Letter Bible Greek lexicon. The entry for Strong's 3340, "to change one's mind, to repent," the verb behind metanoeite in this verse.
