St John of GodFounder of the Hospitallers, patron of the sick

Saint John of God Carrying a Sick Man (c. 1672), Bartolomé Esteban Murillo · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1495–1550, Montemor-o-Novo–Granada
Feast day8 March
RecognisedCanonised 16 October 1690 (Alexander VIII)
PlaceMontemor-o-Novo / Granada, Portugal & Spain

His story

João Cidade was born in Montemor-o-Novo in Portugal and, as a boy, ended up in Spain, where he worked as a shepherd, served as a soldier, and later sold books and holy pictures in Granada.

In 1537 a sermon by the preacher St John of Ávila shook him so deeply that he ran through the streets, gave away his books and behaved so wildly that he was locked up in the Royal Hospital as a madman. There he saw how the mentally ill were treated, chained and whipped. Once released, he rented a house and began caring for the sick and homeless himself, begging at night with the cry, "Do good, brothers, to yourselves!" His followers became the Brothers Hospitallers of St John of God, now running hospitals in more than 50 countries. He died in Granada on his 55th birthday, after diving into a river to save a drowning boy.

Most of his life is well attested by an early biography (1585). Some details, such as the Child Jesus giving him the name "John of God" and the city of Granada as his destiny, come from pious tradition.

Path to sainthood

Beatified in 1630 and canonised by Pope Alexander VIII on 16 October 1690. Pope Leo XIII named him patron of hospitals and the sick (1886).

Patronages

Hospitals, the sick, nurses, heart patients, booksellers.

Where to visit

Basílica de San Juan de Dios, Granada (his tomb); his birthplace in Montemor-o-Novo, Alentejo

Trivia

  • His emblem is a pomegranate (granada) topped by a cross, recalling Granada.
  • The Fatebenefratelli hospital on Rome's Tiber Island takes its Italian nickname from his cry Fate bene, fratelli.
  • He is regarded as a pioneer of humane care for the mentally ill.
