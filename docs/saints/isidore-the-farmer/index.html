St Isidore the FarmerLabourer and patron saint of Madrid

Saint Isidore the Farmer, Cuzco School · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 1070–1130, Madrid
Feast day15 May
RecognisedCanonised 12 March 1622 (Gregory XV)
PlaceMadrid, Spain

His story

Isidore was a poor farm labourer who worked the fields of a landowner, Juan de Vargas, near Madrid, then a small town. He married María Toribia, also venerated as a saint (Santa María de la Cabeza). He went to Mass every morning before work, shared his meagre food with the poor and even with birds, and lived a simple, holy life. He died around 1130 and was buried in the cemetery of San Andrés.

Madrid's devotion grew after his body was moved into the church in the 12th century and was found incorrupt.

The angels who ploughed (legend): His fellow workers complained that he came late because of Mass. His master went to check and saw two angels ploughing beside Isidore, so that he did three men's work. Another story has him make a spring gush from the dry ground with his staff, and another says his son fell into a well and was saved when the water rose up to the brim. These come from a 13th-century life written long after his death.

Path to sainthood

Canonised by Pope Gregory XV on 12 March 1622, in the same ceremony as Ignatius of Loyola, Francis Xavier, Teresa of Ávila and Philip Neri. King Philip III, who believed he had been cured through Isidore's relics in 1619, pushed for the canonisation.

Patronages

Madrid, farmers, farm workers, rural communities.

Where to visit

Real Colegiata de San Isidro, Madrid (his tomb); the Ermita de San Isidro and its meadow (pradera) on 15 May

Trivia

  • Madrid's Fiestas de San Isidro on 15 May bring picnics on the meadow, chulapos in traditional dress and rosquillas (doughnuts) called tontas y listas.
  • The US Catholic Rural Life organisation and many farmers' cooperatives in Latin America take him as patron.
  • He is one of the few lay married men canonised in the Middle Ages.
