St Felipe de JesúsThe first Mexican saint, martyred in Nagasaki

Image of San Felipe de Jesús, Basilica of Zapopan (photo), Iqmann · CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1572–1597, Mexico City–Nagasaki
Feast day5 February
RecognisedCanonised 8 June 1862 (Pius IX)
PlaceMexico City, Mexico

His story

Felipe de las Casas was born in Mexico City, then the capital of New Spain, to Spanish parents. A restless youth, he tried the Franciscan life and left, and was sent by his father to Manila in the Philippines to trade. There he returned to the Franciscans and took the name Felipe de Jesús.

In 1596, sailing home to Mexico to be ordained, his ship, the San Felipe, was blown off course by storms and wrecked on the coast of Japan. The ruler Toyotomi Hideyoshi, suspicious of Spanish intentions, had the Franciscans and their Japanese converts arrested. On 5 February 1597, Felipe and 25 others, the 26 Martyrs of Japan, among them St Paul Miki, were crucified on Nishizaka Hill in Nagasaki and pierced with lances. Felipe, aged about 24, is traditionally said to have been the first of them to die.

The fig tree (legend): When Felipe was a wild boy, his family's maid is supposed to have said that Felipe would become a saint "when the dry fig tree blossoms". On the day he died, the story goes, the withered fig tree in his family's yard turned green.

Path to sainthood

Beatified in 1627 and canonised by Pope Pius IX on 8 June 1862, with the other 26 martyrs of Japan. He is the first saint born in Mexico.

Patronages

Mexico City.

Where to visit

Chapel of San Felipe de Jesús, Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral; the Twenty-Six Martyrs Monument and Museum, Nishizaka Hill, Nagasaki

Trivia

  • In the General Calendar the martyrs are celebrated on 6 February as St Paul Miki and Companions; Mexico keeps his feast on 5 February.
  • His baptismal font is kept in Mexico City Cathedral.
  • The San Felipe incident helped trigger Japan's later bans on Christianity.
