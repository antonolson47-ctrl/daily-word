St Wenceslaus"Good King Wenceslas", Duke of Bohemia

Saint Wenceslas at the Vintage, Attended by Angels and Putti by Franz Anton Maulbertsch, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 907–935, Bohemia
Feast day28 September
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Wenceslaus (Václav) was Duke of Bohemia in the early 10th century. He was brought up a Christian by his grandmother, St Ludmila, who was murdered in 921, probably on the orders of his pagan-leaning mother. As duke he favoured the Church, made peace with the German king Henry the Fowler, and was remembered for charity to the poor. On 28 September 935 (or 929) he was murdered on his way to Mass at Stará Boleslav by followers of his brother Boleslaus, who took the throne.

The full story

Patron of a nation

He was venerated as a martyr almost at once. His relics were taken to St Vitus's Cathedral in Prague, where the jewelled St Wenceslas Chapel holds his tomb, and he became the patron saint of the Czech lands. The equestrian statue in Wenceslas Square in Prague is where Czechs gathered in 1918, 1968 and 1989. A legend says he sleeps with an army of knights under Mount Blaník and will ride out when Bohemia is in its darkest hour.

The carol

The English carol "Good King Wenceslas" was written by John Mason Neale in 1853, set to a 13th-century spring tune. It tells how the king and his page go out on St Stephen's Day (26 December) to bring food and firewood to a poor man, and how the page walks in his master's footsteps, warmed by the saint's holiness. The story is Neale's invention, inspired by Wenceslaus's reputation for charity.

Legend and history. His murder and early cult are historical, recorded in Latin and Old Church Slavonic lives from the 10th century. The carol and the army under Blaník are legend. He was a duke, not a king; the Emperor Otto I is said to have granted him the royal title after his death.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint, venerated as a martyr from the 10th century.

Patronages

The Czech Republic, Bohemia, brewers.

Trivia

  • 28 September is a public holiday in the Czech Republic, Czech Statehood Day.
  • The St Wenceslas crown of the Bohemian kings is kept in his chapel and is shown only rarely.
