St AgathaVirgin martyr and patroness of Catania

Saint Agatha (c. 1630–33), Francisco de Zurbarán · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. 251, Catania (Sicily)
Feast day5 February
RecognisedPre-Congregation
PlaceCatania, Italy

Her story

Agatha was a young Christian woman of Catania, on Sicily's east coast, martyred during the persecution of Decius in 251. Her cult is very early: she is named in the Roman Canon of the Mass, and Pope Symmachus built a church in her honour in Rome around 500. That much is history.

Her Acts (legend): Her later Acts say the governor Quintianus wanted her, and when she refused he had her handed to a brothel, then tortured, and her breasts cut off. St Peter appeared to heal her in prison. She was then rolled over burning coals, and an earthquake shook the city before she died in prison. A year later, so the story goes, the people of Catania held up her veil against a lava flow from Mount Etna, and it stopped.

Her relics were taken to Constantinople in 1040 and brought back in 1126 by two soldiers, Gisliberto and Goselmo, who are said to have hidden them in quivers.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint, one of the most venerated virgin martyrs of the early Church.

Patronages

Catania, breast cancer patients, nurses, bell-founders, against fire and volcanoes.

Where to visit

Catania Cathedral (Badia di Sant'Agata and her relics); the Festa di Sant'Agata, 3–5 February

Trivia

  • Catania's Festa di Sant'Agata (3–5 February) is one of the largest religious festivals in the world; devotees in white robes pull her silver reliquary bust through the streets for nearly two days.
  • Sicilian pastry shops make small domed cakes called minne di Sant'Agata ("St Agatha's breasts") for her feast.
  • Painters, like Zurbarán above, show her carrying her breasts on a dish; they were sometimes mistaken for bells or loaves, which is why bell-founders and bakers took her as patron.
