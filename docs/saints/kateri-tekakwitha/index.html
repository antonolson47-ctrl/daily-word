St Kateri Tekakwitha"Lily of the Mohawks", first Native American saint

Kateri Tekakwitha (c. 1690), Fr Claude Chauchetière · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1656–1680, Ossernenon–Kahnawake
Feast day14 July (USA); 17 April (Canada)
RecognisedCanonised 21 October 2012 (Benedict XVI)

Her story

Tekakwitha was born at Ossernenon, a Mohawk village in what is now New York State, to a Mohawk father and an Algonquin Christian mother. Smallpox killed her parents and left her face scarred and her eyesight weak. Drawn to the faith by Jesuit missionaries, she was baptised at 20 and took the name Kateri (Catherine). Facing hostility at home, she fled to the Christian mission of Kahnawake near Montreal, where she lived a life of prayer, penance and care for others before dying at 24.

Path to sainthood

Beatified on 22 June 1980 by Pope John Paul II and canonised by Pope Benedict XVI on 21 October 2012, the first Indigenous North American saint.

Patronages

Ecology and the environment, Indigenous peoples.

Trivia

  • Witnesses said her smallpox scars disappeared shortly after her death; this is reported by the Jesuits who knew her.
  • The earliest portrait of her, by Fr Claude Chauchetière, was painted from memory around 1690.
  • Her shrine is at the St Francis Xavier church in Kahnawake, Quebec.
