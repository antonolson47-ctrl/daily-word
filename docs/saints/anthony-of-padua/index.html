St Anthony of PaduaFranciscan preacher, finder of lost things

Saint Anthony of Padua with the Child, Bartolomé Esteban Murillo · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 1195–1231, Lisbon–Padua
Feast day13 June
RecognisedCanonised 1232 (Gregory IX); Doctor of the Church 1946

His story

Born Fernando in Lisbon, he became an Augustinian canon, but the sight of the relics of the first Franciscan martyrs, killed in Morocco, made him join the Franciscans, taking the name Anthony. He sailed for Morocco, fell ill, and a storm drove his ship to Sicily. Called on unexpectedly to preach at an ordination, he astonished everyone; from then on he was a celebrated preacher in Italy and southern France, admired by Francis of Assisi himself. He died near Padua at about 36.

Path to sainthood

Canonised by Pope Gregory IX on 30 May 1232, less than a year after his death, one of the fastest canonisations in history. Pope Pius XII declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1946.

Patronages

Lost things, the poor, Portugal, Padua.

Trivia

  • He is invoked for lost items because, according to a story, a novice who borrowed his psalter without permission returned it after Anthony prayed.
  • A legend tells of him preaching to the fish at Rimini when the people would not listen.
  • When his tomb was opened in 1263, his tongue was reportedly found incorrupt; it is displayed in the Basilica of St Anthony in Padua.
