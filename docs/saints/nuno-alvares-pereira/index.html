St Nuno Álvares PereiraPortugal's Holy Constable, soldier turned Carmelite

King John I visits Nuno Álvares Pereira at the Carmo Convent in 1425 (1883), Caetano Moreira da Costa Lima · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1360–1431, Cernache do Bonjardim–Lisbon
Feast day6 November
RecognisedCanonised 26 April 2009 (Benedict XVI)
PlaceLisbon, Portugal

His story

Nuno Álvares Pereira was a nobleman who, still in his twenties, became the Constable (commander-in-chief) of Portugal during the crisis of 1383–85, when Castile tried to take the Portuguese throne. Backing John of Aviz, he won a string of victories, above all the Battle of Aljubarrota on 14 August 1385, where a small Portuguese army with English archers defeated a much larger Castilian force and secured Portugal's independence. The victory was commemorated by the great monastery at Batalha.

He was known as a soldier who prayed before battle, forbade looting and treated the defeated with mercy. In 1389 he founded the Carmelite convent of Carmo in Lisbon. After his wife and only daughter died, he gave away his vast estates and in 1423 entered the convent as a humble lay brother, Brother Nuno of Saint Mary, begging in the streets for the poor. He died there on 1 April 1431, Easter Sunday.

The history here is well documented by contemporary chroniclers such as Fernão Lopes. Later popular tradition added miracles at Aljubarrota, such as a spring that appeared for his thirsty troops.

Path to sainthood

Venerated as a saint in Portugal for centuries; beatified by Benedict XV in 1918 and canonised by Pope Benedict XVI on 26 April 2009.

Patronages

Portuguese army (popularly), Carmelite lay brothers.

Where to visit

Convento do Carmo ruins, Lisbon; the Aljubarrota battlefield and Batalha Monastery

Trivia

  • The Carmo church was ruined in the 1755 Lisbon earthquake and left roofless; its Gothic arches now frame an archaeological museum.
  • He is called o Santo Condestável, "the Holy Constable".
  • Through his daughter Beatriz he is an ancestor of the House of Braganza, Portugal's later royal family.
