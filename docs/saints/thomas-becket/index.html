St Thomas BecketArchbishop murdered in his cathedral

Thomas Becket Murder by Unknown authorUnknown author, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1120–1170, London and Canterbury
Feast day29 December
Canonised1173, Alexander III

The story

Thomas Becket, son of a London merchant, became chancellor and close friend of King Henry II, living in great splendour. In 1162 Henry made him Archbishop of Canterbury, expecting an ally. Instead Thomas changed completely, lived austerely, and defended the rights of the Church against the king.

The full story

"Will no one rid me...?"

After six years of exile in France, Thomas returned in December 1170 and excommunicated bishops who had sided with the king. Henry, in Normandy, is said to have exclaimed in anger something like "Will no one rid me of this turbulent priest?" Four knights took him at his word. On 29 December 1170 they found Thomas in Canterbury Cathedral at Vespers and killed him with their swords near the altar. His last words: "For the name of Jesus and the protection of the Church, I am ready to embrace death."

The pilgrimage

Miracles were reported almost at once. He was canonised in 1173, and in 1174 Henry did public penance at his tomb, walking barefoot and being whipped by the monks. Canterbury became one of the great pilgrimage sites of Europe, the goal of Chaucer's Canterbury Tales. Henry VIII destroyed the shrine in 1538. (See our Via Francigena page.)

History. His murder was witnessed by Edward Grim, a monk who was wounded trying to protect him, and is very well documented. The king's exact words are uncertain.

Path to sainthood

Canonised 21 February 1173 by Alexander III, just over two years after his death.

Patronages

Clergy, Portsmouth, the secular clergy of England.

Trivia

  • A candle burns on the spot of the old shrine in Trinity Chapel, Canterbury.
  • T. S. Eliot's play Murder in the Cathedral (1935) was first performed in Canterbury Cathedral's chapter house.
