St Brigid of KildareAbbess, patron of Ireland, and the cloak that covered a kingdom

Saint Bride title QS:P1476,en:"Saint Bride "label QS:Len,"Saint Bride "label QS: by John Duncan, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 451–525, Ireland
Feast day1 February
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Brigid is, with Patrick and Columba, one of the three patron saints of Ireland. Tradition says she was born in Faughart, near Dundalk, to a chieftain and a slave woman, was famous for giving everything away, and founded a double monastery for women and men at Kildare (Cill Dara, "church of the oak"), about 480. Her first Life, by the monk Cogitosus, was written about 650.

The full story

The cloak

The best-known story says Brigid asked the King of Leinster for land to build her monastery. He laughed and said she could have as much as her cloak would cover. She spread it on the ground, and it grew until it covered the whole Curragh plain. Other stories tell of butter that never ran out, a lake of beer for visiting clergy, and her cross.

Brigid's cross

She is said to have woven a cross out of rushes while sitting with a dying pagan chieftain, explaining the faith to him; he was baptised before he died. Irish households still weave St Brigid's crosses on 1 February and hang them over the door. Her monastery kept a perpetual fire, tended by nuns, until the Reformation; the flame was relit in Kildare in 1993.

History or legend? Historians agree Brigid existed and founded Kildare, but we know little for certain. Her feast falls on Imbolc, the old Celtic festival of spring, and she shares a name with a pagan goddess, so some customs may be older traditions given a Christian meaning.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint.

Patronages

Ireland, dairy workers, midwives, newborns, poets.

Trivia

  • Since 2023 Ireland has a public holiday on or near 1 February in her honour, the first named after a woman.
  • Her head is said to be kept in a church in Lumiar, Lisbon.
