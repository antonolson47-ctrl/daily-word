The Blessed Virgin MaryMother of Jesus, Mother of God (Theotokos)

Lived1st century BC – 1st century AD, Nazareth
Feast days1 Jan, 25 Mar, 31 May, 15 Aug, 8 Dec (and more)
RecognisedPre-Congregation (biblical)
PlaceNazareth, Bethlehem, Jerusalem, Ephesus

Her story

Mary of Nazareth was a young Jewish woman betrothed to Joseph, a carpenter of David's line, when the angel Gabriel announced that she would conceive a son by the Holy Spirit (Luke 1:26-38). Her answer, "be it unto me according to thy word", is remembered in every Angelus. She visited her cousin Elizabeth and sang the **Magnificat**, gave birth in Bethlehem, presented Jesus in the Temple, searched for him when he was twelve, prompted his first sign at **Cana** ("Whatsoever he saith unto you, do it", John 2:5), stood at the **Cross**, and prayed with the apostles before Pentecost (Acts 1:14).

Tradition: The 2nd-century Protoevangelium of James names her parents Joachim and Anne. Ancient tradition places her last years either in Jerusalem or at Ephesus with St John, to whom Jesus entrusted her from the Cross.

What the Church teaches

  • Mother of God (Theotokos), defined at the Council of Ephesus (431): because Jesus is one divine Person, his mother is truly the Mother of God. Luther kept this title and preached on the Magnificat (1521).
  • Perpetual virginity, taught from the early centuries.
  • Immaculate Conception (Pius IX, 1854): preserved from original sin from the first moment of her conception. Feast 8 December.
  • Assumption (Pius XII, 1950): taken body and soul into heavenly glory. Feast 15 August.

Lutherans honour Mary as the mother of the Lord and the model of faith, keeping the Annunciation, the Visitation and 15 August (as "Mary, Mother of Our Lord"), while not holding the 1854 and 1950 definitions.

Feasts through the Church year

1 January (Mary, Mother of God) · 2 February (Presentation) · 25 March (Annunciation; in 2027 moved to 5 April) · 31 May (Visitation) · 15 August (Assumption) · 8 September (Nativity of Mary) · 8 December (Immaculate Conception) · 12 December (Our Lady of Guadalupe; see St Juan Diego). See the Feast days page.

Where to visit

Basilica of the Annunciation, Nazareth; Church of the Visitation, Ein Kerem; House of the Virgin Mary near Ephesus; Santa Maria Maggiore, Rome; and the Marian shrines of Lourdes, Fátima and Guadalupe.

Trivia

  • The Hail Mary joins Gabriel's greeting and Elizabeth's words (Luke 1:28, 42); the second half was fixed in the 16th century.
  • Mary speaks only seven times in the Gospels.
  • The Magnificat is sung every evening at Vespers and is the Lutheran canticle for Evening Prayer.
