St John of the CrossPoet of the Dark Night, Teresa's companion

Christ Appears to St John of the Cross (19th century), Robert von Langer, Bavarian State Painting Collections · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1542–1591, Fontiveros–Úbeda
Feast day14 December
RecognisedCanonised 27 December 1726 (Benedict XIII); Doctor of the Church 1926
PlaceSegovia / Úbeda, Spain

His story

Juan de Yepes grew up in poverty in Castile; his father died when he was small. He became a Carmelite friar and was ordained in 1567, the year he met St Teresa of Ávila, who persuaded the young friar to join her reform. In 1568 he helped found the first friary of the Discalced (barefoot) Carmelites at Duruelo, taking the name John of the Cross.

The reform caused bitter conflict. In December 1577 friars opposed to it kidnapped John and locked him in a tiny cell in their priory at Toledo for nine months, flogging him regularly. In that darkness he composed much of his poem the Spiritual Canticle. In August 1578 he escaped by night down a rope of knotted strips of blanket. He went on to lead and found friaries in Andalusia, and wrote the Dark Night of the Soul, the Ascent of Mount Carmel and the Living Flame of Love, which are masterpieces of Spanish poetry and mystical theology. Sidelined again at the end of his life, he died at Úbeda on 14 December 1591.

His life is well documented. Pious tradition adds details such as Mary helping him escape from Toledo.

Path to sainthood

Beatified in 1675 and canonised by Pope Benedict XIII on 27 December 1726. Pius XI declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1926.

Patronages

Mystics, contemplatives, Spanish poets.

Where to visit

Convento de los Carmelitas Descalzos, Segovia (his tomb); Úbeda, where he died; Fontiveros, his birthplace

Trivia

  • A small sketch he drew of Christ on the cross, seen from above, inspired Salvador Dalí's painting Christ of Saint John of the Cross (1951).
  • The phrase "dark night of the soul" comes from his poem.
  • He was barely five feet tall; Teresa joked that she had found "half a friar" for her reform.
