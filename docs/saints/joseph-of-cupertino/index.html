St Joseph of CupertinoThe flying friar

Frari (Venice) nave right - Altar of san Giuseppe da Copertino - The Miracle of by Didier Descouens, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1603–1663, Apulia and the Marches (Italy)
Feast day18 September
Canonised1767, Clement XIII

The story

Giuseppe Desa was born in a shed in Copertino, near Lecce, in 1603, because his father's debts had cost the family their house. He was absent-minded, slow at school and so clumsy that the Capuchins dismissed him as a lay brother. The Conventual Franciscans took him in to look after their mule, and in 1628 he was ordained a priest, by most accounts after passing his examinations only because the bishop happened to ask the one question he could answer. He became patron of students and exam-takers.

The full story

Raptures and flights

From the 1630s Joseph fell into ecstasies at the names of Jesus and Mary, at church music, or at the sight of a picture of the Virgin. Witnesses, including friars, cardinals, a Spanish ambassador and the Lutheran Duke of Brunswick (who became a Catholic afterwards), testified that he rose into the air and floated to the altar or into the branches of a tree. More than seventy such episodes are recorded in the process for his canonisation. Pope Urban VIII is said to have seen one himself.

Hidden away

The crowds became a problem. The Inquisition examined him at Naples in 1638 and found nothing wrong, but he was moved from friary to friary and finally kept almost in seclusion at Osimo, where he died on 18 September 1663. His body lies in the Basilica of San Giuseppe da Copertino in Osimo.

History or legend? The flights are among the best documented "miracles" of the 17th century, in sworn testimony gathered within a few years of his death; the Church examined them in his cause. Historians differ on how to explain them. What is certain is the number and rank of the witnesses.

Path to sainthood

Beatified 1753 by Benedict XIV (who as Prosper Lambertini had been the sceptical "devil's advocate" in his cause), canonised 1767 by Clement XIII.

Patronages

Students and examinations, air travellers and pilots, astronauts.

Trivia

  • He is a patron of air travellers and pilots.
  • Benedict XIV, the great expert on canonisation procedure, personally reviewed the evidence for his flights.
