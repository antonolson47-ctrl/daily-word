St MatthewApostle and Evangelist, the tax collector who followed

Artistic illustration in a Renaissance fresco style, created for The Daily Word; not a historical likeness. Matthew writing his Gospel, guided by an angel (his symbol).

Lived1st century, Capernaum
Feast day21 September
RecognisedPre-Congregation (apostle)
PlaceCapernaum; Salerno, Italy

His story

Matthew (also called **Levi**) was a tax collector at Capernaum, working for Herod Antipas: a man most of his neighbours would have regarded as a collaborator. Jesus saw him at the tax booth and said "Follow me", "and he arose, and followed him" (Matthew 9:9). He then threw a dinner for Jesus with other tax collectors and sinners, prompting the line "I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

Tradition: Papias of Hierapolis (early 2nd century) says Matthew "compiled the sayings in the Hebrew language", and the Church has always attached his name to the first Gospel. Later traditions send him to preach in Ethiopia or Persia and tell of his martyrdom; they differ.

His Gospel in the Church year

Matthew is the Gospel of Lectionary Year A, the most quoted in the liturgy: the Sermon on the Mount, the Lord's Prayer as Christians pray it, the Magi, and the Great Commission. Several Daily Word verses so far (Matthew 6:9–13, 16:18, 4:17) come from it. His symbol is a winged man or angel, because his Gospel begins with Jesus' human genealogy.

Patronages

Accountants, bankers, tax collectors, customs officers; Salerno.

Where to visit

Capernaum on the Sea of Galilee; Salerno Cathedral, Italy, where his relics have been kept since 954; Caravaggio's Calling of St Matthew in San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome.
