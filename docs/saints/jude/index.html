St JudeApostle of desperate causes, not Iscariot

St Jude Thaddeus (c. 1610–14), El Greco · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1st century
Feast day28 October (with St Simon)
RecognisedPre-Congregation (apostle)
PlaceRome

His story

The lists of the Twelve include a Judas who is not the traitor. Luke and Acts call him Judas of James. Matthew and Mark call a companion of Simon Thaddaeus (some manuscripts, Lebbaeus). The Church in the West has long taken these as one man: Jude, also Thaddaeus, also the author of the short Epistle of Jude, which opens "Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James". At the Last Supper, John has "Judas (not Iscariot)" ask, "Lord, how is it that you will manifest yourself to us, and not to the world?" (John 14:22). It is his only sentence in the Gospel story.

"Brother of James" pulled him into a further argument. The James is usually taken to be James the Less, and the "brothers of the Lord" in the Gospels were then read, in Catholic tradition, as relatives rather than sons of Mary. Hegesippus, quoted by Eusebius, says the grandsons of Jude were brought before Domitian as descendants of David and dismissed as harmless peasants. Scholars still debate which Judas is which. The feast the Church keeps does not wait on the end of that debate: Simon and Jude, apostles, 28 October.

The later legend sends him with Simon the Zealot into Syria and Mesopotamia, and has them martyred together, Jude with a club or a halberd. El Greco paints him with the halberd, old, looking out of the canvas as if he would rather not be asked for anything.

The patronage of lost and desperate causes is devotional, and the usual explanation is a sad one. People confused him with Judas Iscariot and were afraid to pray to him. Those who did, the devotion says, found him willing, as if grateful to be asked. By the nineteenth century, especially through the Claretian and other popular missions, St Jude had become the saint you turned to when a case was already called hopeless. His altar with Simon is in the left transept of St Peter's.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation, as an apostle. The joint feast with St Simon on 28 October is on the General Roman Calendar.

Patronages

Desperate and lost causes, hospitals, and the police in some countries. Also, because of the epistle's warning against false teachers, he is sometimes asked for clarity when a situation is confused.

Trivia

  • Classified ads in twentieth-century newspapers used to thank "St Jude for favours granted", often with no other detail. The thank-you was part of the devotion.
  • He is shown with a medallion of the face of Christ on his chest, to keep him distinct from Judas Iscariot, and with a club, an axe or a halberd.
  • The Epistle of Jude is twenty-five verses. It quotes the non-biblical Book of Enoch, and it ends in one of the great doxologies of the New Testament.
