St Josemaría EscriváFounder of Opus Dei

Torreciudad Sanctuary (photo), Preyes · CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1902–1975, Barbastro–Rome
Feast day26 June
RecognisedCanonised 6 October 2002 (John Paul II)
PlaceBarbastro / Torreciudad, Spain

His story

Josemaría Escrivá was born in Barbastro, Aragon. His family's business failed when he was a boy, and they moved to Logroño, where, he said, seeing footprints of a barefoot friar in the snow moved him to the priesthood. He was ordained in Zaragoza in 1925 and moved to Madrid.

On 2 October 1928, during a retreat, he said he "saw" what God wanted of him: Opus Dei ("Work of God"), an institution teaching that ordinary people can become saints through their daily work and family life. During the Spanish Civil War, when priests were being killed, he hid in Madrid and escaped across the Pyrenees in 1937. He published The Way (Camino), a book of short spiritual points, in 1939; it has sold millions of copies. He moved to Rome in 1946 and died there on 26 June 1975. By then Opus Dei had tens of thousands of members worldwide.

His life is recent and documented. His canonisation was controversial: critics of Opus Dei objected to the speed of the process and questioned his personality and the organisation's secrecy, while supporters point to the many testimonies of holiness. We note both views.

Path to sainthood

Beatified on 17 May 1992 and canonised by Pope John Paul II on 6 October 2002, just 27 years after his death.

Patronages

Ordinary work (popularly), Opus Dei.

Where to visit

Sanctuary of Torreciudad (Huesca), near his birthplace Barbastro; his tomb in the prelatic church of Santa Maria della Pace, Rome

Trivia

  • The Torreciudad shrine in the Pyrenean foothills, which he promoted, is near the hermitage where, as a sick child, he was taken by his mother to pray to Our Lady.
  • In 2022 Pope Francis reorganised Opus Dei's status within the Church.
  • The Way has been translated into more than 40 languages.
