St CeciliaThe singing martyr, found incorrupt in 1599

RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Cecilia was a Roman martyr buried in the Catacomb of St Callixtus, and her name is in the Roman Canon of the Mass. According to her 5th-century Acts, she was a noble Christian girl married against her will to Valerian. On her wedding night she told him an angel guarded her virginity; he converted, with his brother Tiburtius, and both were martyred.

The full story

Singing in her heart

Her Acts say that as the wedding music played, "Cecilia sang in her heart to the Lord alone". From this line, misread as playing an organ, she became the patron of music. Condemned, she was shut in the steam bath of her house to suffocate, but survived. An executioner struck her neck three times and failed to cut off her head; she lived three days, giving her goods to the poor and asking that her house become a church.

1599

Her body was moved to Santa Cecilia in Trastevere in the 9th century. When the tomb was opened in 1599, it was reported to be incorrupt, lying on its side, with the face turned down. The sculptor Stefano Maderno carved the beautiful marble statue of her in exactly that position, now under the altar.

History or legend? Her early cult and burial are historical. Her Acts are a later romance; the discovery of 1599 is well recorded, though no one now can verify its details.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint.

Patronages

Musicians, singers, composers, poets.

Trivia

  • Purcell, Handel and Britten (born on her feast) all wrote odes or hymns for St Cecilia's Day.
  • The Accademia di Santa Cecilia in Rome is one of the oldest music academies in the world.
