St Michael the ArchangelPrince of the heavenly host

The Archangel Michael Defeating Satan (1636), Guido Reni · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

LivedAn angel: not a human life
Feast day29 September (Michaelmas)
RecognisedScripture & ancient tradition

Who he is

Michael (Hebrew Mi-ka-El, "Who is like God?") appears in Daniel 10:13, 21 and 12:1 as a great prince who protects God's people, in Jude 9 disputing with the devil, and in Revelation 12:7–9 leading the angels against the dragon. Christians, Jews and Muslims all honour him.

Path to sainthood

Michael is not a canonised human but one of the three archangels named in the Catholic canon (with Gabriel and Raphael), venerated since the earliest centuries. Since the 1969 calendar reform the three share the feast of 29 September.

Patronages

Soldiers, police officers, paramedics, the sick, and a good death.

Trivia

  • Pope Leo XIII added the short Prayer to St Michael in 1886 to the prayers he had ordered said after Low Mass in 1884. The famous story that he wrote it after a vision of Satan during Mass first appeared in the 1930s, with conflicting details and no contemporary evidence, so treat it as legend.
  • Michaelmas was one of the four "quarter days" when rents were due in England, and it gave its name to Oxford and Cambridge's autumn Michaelmas term.
  • English folklore: don't pick blackberries after Michaelmas, because the devil, cast out of heaven, landed in a bramble bush and spoiled them.
