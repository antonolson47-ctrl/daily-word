St John BoscoDon Bosco, juggler for God, and Grigio the guardian dog

Verona, chiesa di San Giovanni Bosco - Pala d'altare by Syrio, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1815–1888, Piedmont (Italy)
Feast day31 January
Canonised1934, Pius XI

The story

John Bosco was born to a poor farming family at Becchi, near Turin. At nine he had a dream in which wild animals turned into lambs, and a lady told him he must win over unruly boys "not with blows but with gentleness". As a boy he learned to juggle, do magic tricks and walk a tightrope to draw a crowd, then repeated the Sunday sermon to them.

The full story

The Oratory

Ordained in 1841, he found Turin full of homeless boys working in factories, sleeping rough or in prison. He gathered them in an oratory in Valdocco, gave them food, schooling and trades, and played with them. In 1859 he founded the Salesians (named after St Francis de Sales) and, with St Mary Mazzarello, the Salesian Sisters. His "preventive system" of education rested on reason, religion and loving kindness.

Grigio

Don Bosco had enemies, and was attacked several times on the dark roads near Valdocco. From about 1852, a large grey dog, which he called Grigio ("Greyish"), appeared out of nowhere when he was in danger, drove off attackers, would not let him leave the house when danger waited outside, and then vanished. It never ate, and no one knew where it came from. Bosco wrote about it in his Memoirs and said it was strange, but true.

History and legend. His work and the Salesians are fully historical. The Grigio stories come from Bosco himself and early witnesses; many believers read the dog as a guardian sent by Providence.

Path to sainthood

Beatified 1929; canonised on Easter Sunday 1934 by Pius XI, who had met him.

Patronages

Young people, students, schoolchildren, editors, magicians and stage illusionists.

Trivia

  • The Salesians are now one of the largest religious orders in the world, with schools in over 130 countries.
  • His body lies in the Basilica of Mary Help of Christians, Turin.
