St Francis of PaolaThe hermit who sailed across the Strait of Messina on his cloak

Ulisse giocchi, San Francesco di Paola attraversa lo stretto di Messina sul suo by Sailko, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1416–1507, Calabria and France
Feast day2 April
Canonised1519, Leo X

The story

Francis was born in Paola, Calabria, and named after St Francis of Assisi, to whom his parents had prayed for a child. At 15 he became a hermit in a cave by the sea. Followers joined him, and he founded the Order of Minims ("the least"), who keep a perpetual Lenten fast, abstaining from meat, eggs and dairy.

The full story

The cloak

The best-known story says that in 1464 Francis wanted to cross to Sicily, but a boatman refused to take him because he could not pay. Francis spread his cloak on the water, tied one end to his staff as a sail, and sailed across the Strait of Messina with two companions. The scene is painted in many southern Italian churches, and Pius XII named him patron of Italian seafarers.

The king's hermit

His fame as a healer reached Louis XI of France, who was dying and begged the Pope to send him. Francis went reluctantly in 1483, did not cure the king but helped him die well, and stayed in France as adviser to Charles VIII and Louis XII. He died at Plessis-lès-Tours on Good Friday 1507, aged 91.

History and legend. His life, his order and his time in France are historical, well documented in his canonisation process (begun 1512). The cloak crossing is legend and miracle tradition.

Path to sainthood

Canonised 1519 by Leo X, only 12 years after his death.

Patronages

Seafarers and navy, Calabria, boatmen.

Trivia

  • Huguenots burned his body in 1562; some relics survive and were returned to Paola.
  • The Sanctuary of San Francesco di Paola is the biggest pilgrimage site in Calabria.
