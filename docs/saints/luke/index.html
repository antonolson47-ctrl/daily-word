St LukeEvangelist, physician and historian of the early Church

Artistic illustration in a Renaissance fresco style, created for The Daily Word; not a historical likeness. Luke painting the Madonna (a medieval legend), with the winged ox, his symbol.

Lived1st century, Antioch (tradition)
Feast day18 October
RecognisedPre-Congregation (evangelist)
PlaceAntioch; Padua, Italy

His story

Luke wrote the third Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles, together about a quarter of the New Testament. St Paul calls him "Luke, the beloved physician" (Colossians 4:14) and his loyal companion ("Only Luke is with me", 2 Timothy 4:11). The "we" passages of Acts (16:10 onward) suggest he travelled with Paul. He was probably a Gentile; he opens his Gospel like a Greek historian, having "traced the course of all things accurately from the first" (Luke 1:3).

Tradition: Early writers say he came from Antioch and died at 84 in Boeotia, Greece. A medieval legend says he painted the first icon of the Virgin Mary; several famous icons, such as the Salus Populi Romani in Santa Maria Maggiore, were attributed to him.

His Gospel in the Church year

Without Luke there would be no Christmas story as we tell it: the Annunciation, the Visitation, the Magnificat, the Benedictus, the shepherds, the Gloria in excelsis (Luke 2:14), Simeon's Nunc dimittis. All three canticles of the Church's daily prayer come from his first two chapters. Luke is the Gospel of Lectionary Year C, and his Acts is read throughout Easter. His symbol is the ox, the sacrificial animal, because his Gospel begins in the Temple.

Patronages

Physicians, surgeons, artists and painters (the Guild of St Luke), students.

Where to visit

Basilica of Santa Giustina, Padua (his tomb); Santa Maria Maggiore, Rome.
