St Juan DiegoWitness of Our Lady of Guadalupe

The image of Our Lady of Guadalupe on Juan Diego's tilma (1531), Basilica of Guadalupe · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1474–1548, Mexico
Feast day9 December
RecognisedCanonised 31 July 2002 (John Paul II)

His story

Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin was an Indigenous (Nahua) convert in central Mexico. According to the tradition first written down in the 16th century in the Nahuatl Nican Mopohua, in December 1531 the Virgin Mary appeared to him on the hill of Tepeyac, asking that a church be built there. When the bishop asked for a sign, Juan Diego gathered roses out of season in his cloak (tilma); when he opened it before the bishop, the image of Our Lady of Guadalupe was found on it.

Path to sainthood

Beatified on 6 May 1990 and canonised by Pope John Paul II in Mexico City on 31 July 2002, the first Indigenous saint of the Americas. Some historians questioned whether he existed; the Church concluded its investigation in favour of his historicity.

Patronages

Indigenous peoples (popularly).

Trivia

  • His tilma with the image hangs in the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe in Mexico City, one of the most visited Catholic shrines in the world.
  • Our Lady of Guadalupe's feast is 12 December, three days after his.
  • The image shows Mary with dark skin and Indigenous features, a powerful symbol for Mexico.
