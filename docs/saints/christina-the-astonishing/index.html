St Christina the AstonishingThe woman who rose from her coffin

Artistic illustration in a Renaissance fresco style, created for The Daily Word; not a historical likeness. Christina rising from her coffin at her own funeral.

Livedc. 1150–1224, Brustem and Sint-Truiden (Belgium)
Feast day24 July (local)
RecognisedVenerated locally; never formally canonised

The story

Christina was an orphaned peasant girl from Brustem, near Sint-Truiden in today's Belgium. Almost everything we know comes from a Life written about 1232 by the Dominican Thomas of Cantimpré, who said he had spoken with people who knew her. Cardinal Jacques de Vitry also mentions her.

The full story

The funeral

According to Thomas, Christina died of a seizure at about 22. During her funeral Mass, in the open coffin, she sat up and rose to the rafters of the church. The congregation fled. The priest coaxed her down. She said she had seen hell, purgatory and heaven, and had been offered the choice of staying in heaven or returning to suffer for the souls in purgatory. She chose to return.

A strange life

She could not bear the smell of human beings, Thomas says, and climbed trees, church towers and rocks to escape it. She threw herself into ovens and freezing rivers, prayed balanced on fence posts, and was chained up by her family, who thought her mad. Later in life she grew calmer and was sought out for advice, including by Count Louis of Looz. She died at the convent of St Catherine in Sint-Truiden in 1224.

Legend. Thomas's Life is a work of medieval devotion, written to teach about purgatory. Christina was a real person and her cult is old, but the miracles cannot be checked, and many historians think she suffered from an illness that her age understood in religious terms.

Path to sainthood

Never formally canonised; venerated locally in the diocese of Liège and in the Roman Martyrology tradition of the Low Countries.

Patronages

Mental illness, therapists, psychiatrists (popular patronage).

Trivia

  • Nick Cave wrote a song, "Christina the Astonishing" (1996), about her.
  • Her relics are at the Redemptorist church in Sint-Truiden.

Sources

  • Thomas of Cantimpré, Vita Christinae Mirabilis (c. 1232), in Acta Sanctorum, July, vol. 5.
