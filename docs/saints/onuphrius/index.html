St OnuphriusThe desert hermit covered only by his beard and a loincloth of leaves

Saint Onuphrius Emmanuel Tzanes · Emmanuel Tzanes · Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. c. 400, Upper Egypt (traditional)
Feast day12 June
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Onuphrius (Humphrey in English mouths) is known from one text: Paphnutius' account of journeying into the Egyptian desert and finding an old man hairy as a beast, naked except for leaves, who had lived alone some sixty or seventy years.

The full story

The angel's bread

Onuphrius told Paphnutius he had left a monastery for the utter desert, where an angel guided him, a date palm and spring sustained him, and an angel brought him Communion on Sundays. Paphnutius stayed; the old man died as they prayed, lions (in the beloved iconography) helped dig his grave with their paws.

Icon of the desert

No saint looks like Onuphrius: Orthodox and Coptic icons show the white torrent of beard and hair, the leaf skirt, the palm. He became the type of the solitary — venerated from Egypt to Sicily (where Palermo honours Sant'Onofrio) to the hermitages of Spain.

Legend. The Paphnutius narrative is edifying desert literature, possibly built around a real solitary. Read it as the tradition read it: a parable of total dependence on God, with lions for emphasis.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation; cult East and West, strong in Coptic, Byzantine and Sicilian devotion.

Patronages

Hermits, weavers (in some lists), Palermo devotion as Sant'Onofrio; invoked for a peaceful death (popular).

Trivia

  • In icons the two grave-digging lions are his signature — nature serving the man who served God alone.
  • His name travelled: Humphrey is the English form of Onuphrius.
