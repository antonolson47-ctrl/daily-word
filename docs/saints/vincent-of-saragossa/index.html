St Vincent of SaragossaDeacon martyr, patron of Lisbon and Valencia

The Apotheosis of Saint Vincent of Saragossa, Carlo Innocenzo Carlone · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. 304, Valencia
Feast day22 January
RecognisedPre-Congregation
PlaceLisbon / Valencia, Portugal & Spain

His story

Vincent was a deacon of the church of Saragossa (Zaragoza) under Bishop Valerius, who had a speech impediment and let Vincent preach for him. In the persecution of Diocletian, both were arrested and taken to Valencia. Valerius was exiled; Vincent was tortured on the rack and a gridiron and died in prison in 304. The poet Prudentius wrote a hymn to him around 400, and St Augustine preached several sermons on his feast, so his martyrdom is early and well attested.

His later fame owes much to legend. His body, thrown to the wild animals, was said to be guarded by a raven; thrown into the sea, it washed ashore. Christians later took it to the southwestern tip of Portugal, which is still called Cape St Vincent.

The ravens of Lisbon (legend): In 1173 King Afonso Henriques had the relics brought by ship from the cape to Lisbon, and two ravens are said to have kept watch on the ship the whole way. A ship with two ravens has been Lisbon's coat of arms ever since, and you can see it on the city's lamp-posts and bins.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint, one of the most celebrated martyrs of the early Church in the West.

Patronages

Lisbon, Valencia, wine-growers (vintners), deacons.

Where to visit

Lisbon Cathedral (Sé) and the Monastery of São Vicente de Fora; Cape St Vincent (Cabo de São Vicente), Algarve

Trivia

  • Ravens were kept at Lisbon Cathedral for centuries in his honour.
  • France's wine-growers celebrate la Saint-Vincent on 22 January, partly because his name sounds like vin (wine).
  • The Monastery of São Vicente de Fora in Lisbon holds the tombs of the Braganza kings.
