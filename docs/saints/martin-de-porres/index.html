St Martin de PorresLima's barber-surgeon, friend of the poor and of mice

San Martín de Porres - Oratorio San Felipe Neri by Abraham Menahem, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1579–1639, Lima (Peru)
Feast day3 November
Canonised1962, John XXIII

The story

Martin was born in Lima in 1579, the son of a Spanish nobleman and Ana Velázquez, a freed woman of African and Native descent. His father at first did not acknowledge him. As a mixed-race child he faced constant prejudice. He trained as a barber-surgeon and at 15 entered the Dominican Convent of the Holy Rosary as a servant; only in 1603 were the rules waived so he could make vows as a lay brother.

The full story

Brother broom

Martin swept, cooked, cut hair and ran the infirmary. He cared for the sick of the whole city, whatever their race, founded an orphanage and gave out food to the poor every day. He called himself "a poor mulatto" and carried his broom as a sign of humility.

Mice, dogs and bilocation

Many stories gathered round him. He is said to have made peace between a dog, a cat and a mouse who ate from the same bowl, and to have talked the convent's mice into leaving for the garden, where he fed them. Witnesses in his process said he was seen in Mexico, Africa, China and Japan while never leaving Lima, and that he passed through locked doors to reach the sick.

History and legend. His life of service is well documented in testimony gathered from 1660. The animal stories and bilocations are pious tradition, though they come from that testimony. He was a close friend of St Rose of Lima and St John Macías.

Path to sainthood

Beatified 1837 by Gregory XVI; canonised 6 May 1962 by John XXIII, who called him "Martin of Charity".

Patronages

People of mixed race, barbers, hairdressers, public health workers, social justice, Peru.

Trivia

  • He was the first Black saint of the Americas.
  • He is shown with a broom, and often with a dog, cat and mouse at his feet.
