St Elizabeth of HungaryThe princess whose bread turned to roses

Saint Elisabeth of Hungary memorial, Miracle of the Roses, 2018 Pesterzsébet by Globetrotter19, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1207–1231, Hungary and Thuringia (Germany)
Feast day17 November
Canonised1235, Gregory IX

The story

Elizabeth was the daughter of King Andrew II of Hungary. At four she was sent to the court of Thuringia at the Wartburg castle, and at 14 she married Ludwig IV, Landgrave of Thuringia. It was a loving marriage, and Ludwig supported her charity. She built a hospital below the castle and fed hundreds of poor people daily during the famine of 1226.

The full story

The roses

The best-known legend says Elizabeth was carrying bread in her cloak to the poor when she met her husband (or her disapproving in-laws), who asked what she was hiding. When she opened her cloak, there were only roses, in the middle of winter. The same story is told of her great-niece, St Elizabeth of Portugal.

Widowhood

Ludwig died on crusade in 1227. Elizabeth, at 20, left the Wartburg with her children, became a Franciscan tertiary, and gave her dowry to found a hospital at Marburg, where she nursed the sick, including lepers, with her own hands, under the harsh direction of her confessor, Conrad of Marburg. She died on 17 November 1231, aged 24.

History and legend. Her life is well documented in the testimony of her handmaids for her canonisation. The miracle of the roses is a later legend.

Path to sainthood

Canonised 1235 by Gregory IX, four years after her death.

Patronages

Bakers, the homeless, nurses, Franciscan tertiaries, Catholic charities.

Trivia

  • The Elisabethkirche in Marburg, built over her tomb, is one of the first purely Gothic churches in Germany.
  • Liszt wrote an oratorio, The Legend of St Elizabeth (1862).
