St Gregory the GreatThe monk who was sent to be pope

Saint Gregory the Great (c. 1630), Jacopo Vignali · public domain, Walters Art Museum, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 540–604, Rome
Feast day3 September
RecognisedPre-Congregation; Doctor of the Church
PlaceRome

His story

Gregory was born in Rome about 540, into a senatorial family that had already given the Church two popes. He had the education, and by about thirty he was prefect of the city, the highest civil post Rome still had, in a century of plague, Lombard armies and a Senate that no longer mattered much. Then he resigned, turned the family house on the Caelian Hill into a monastery under the patronage of St Andrew, and became a monk. The house is San Gregorio al Celio. He founded six other monasteries on the family estates in Sicily.

Pope Pelagius II pulled him out and sent him to Constantinople as the papal representative. He went with monks, lived with them, and wrote there the long commentary on Job, the Moralia. In 590, with Rome flooding and the plague in the streets, Pelagius died and the city elected Gregory. He wrote to the emperor begging to be excused. He was consecrated on 3 September anyway.

As pope he fed the city from the farms of the Church, ransomed captives from the Lombards, and wrote the Pastoral Rule, a short book on what a bishop is for that King Alfred would later translate into English. He called himself servus servorum Dei, servant of the servants of God. In 596 he sent a prior of his own monastery, Augustine, with a company of monks, to the English. They landed in Kent in 597. Augustine became the first archbishop of Canterbury. Gregory never saw England. A story told later, that he saw English boys in the Roman slave market and said they were not Angles but angels, is the kind of pun his legend loved. The mission itself is in his letters.

He also wrote the Dialogues, including the Life of St Benedict that is still the main early source for Benedict and for Scholastica. He died on 12 March 604 and was buried in St Peter's.

During the plague of 590 he ordered processions through the city. A telling famous by the thirteenth century says that as the procession passed the mausoleum of Hadrian, Michael the Archangel appeared on its summit and sheathed a bloody sword, and the plague stopped. The building has been Castel Sant'Angelo, the castle of the angel, ever since. The procession is Gregory's. The angel is the story the castle has kept. Painters also set a dove at his ear. Gregorian chant is named after him, and he did care how the Roman liturgy was sung. The repertoire that bears his name grew over later centuries.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation. One of the four great Latin Doctors, with Ambrose, Augustine and Jerome. His memorial is 3 September, the day of his election, because 12 March, the day he died, falls in Lent.

Patronages

Musicians, singers, teachers, students, and the missions, especially the English mission he began. Popes have kept his title, servant of the servants of God.

Trivia

  • Vignali's painting gives him the papal tiara, the dove, and a book. Painters used the dove to mean the chant, or the inspiration of the Dialogues.
  • He was the first monk to become pope.
  • His letters, more than 800 of them, are the best record of what a pope actually did at the end of the sixth century: grain prices, a bishop's quarrel, a lute sent as a gift, the English mission in among them.
