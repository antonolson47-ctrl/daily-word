St John the ApostleSon of Zebedee, apostle and evangelist

St John the Evangelist on Patmos (c. 1618), Diego Velázquez · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. c. AD 100 (tradition), Ephesus
Feast day27 December
RecognisedPre-Congregation (apostle)

His story

John and his brother James, fishermen and sons of Zebedee, left their nets to follow Jesus (Mark 1:19–20); Jesus nicknamed them Boanerges, "sons of thunder". With Peter and James he witnessed the Transfiguration and the agony in Gethsemane. Christian tradition identifies him with the "disciple whom Jesus loved", to whom Jesus entrusted his mother from the cross (John 19:26–27). Tradition also attributes to him the fourth Gospel, three letters and Revelation (written on the island of Patmos); modern scholars debate these attributions. According to Irenaeus, he lived to old age at Ephesus.

Path to sainthood

An apostle, venerated from the beginning. No formal canonisation (pre-Congregation).

Patronages

Writers, theologians, friendship.

Trivia

  • By tradition he is the only one of the Twelve who was not martyred, dying of old age.
  • A legend says he was handed a poisoned cup and blessed it, and the poison left as a serpent; that's why he is often painted holding a chalice with a snake.
  • His symbol among the four evangelists is the eagle, because his Gospel soars into theology from its first line, "In the beginning was the Word".
