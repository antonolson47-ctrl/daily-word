St Rafael Guízar y ValenciaBishop of Veracruz who ministered in disguise

Portrait of St Rafael Guízar y Valencia (photo of a painting), Nheyob · CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1878–1938, Cotija–Mexico City
Feast day6 June
RecognisedCanonised 15 October 2006 (Benedict XVI)
PlaceXalapa, Mexico

His story

Rafael Guízar y Valencia was born into a wealthy family in Cotija, Michoacán, and ordained in 1901. He was a tireless missionary preacher. During the Mexican Revolution, when priests were hunted, he moved among the soldiers and the wounded in disguise (as a junk dealer, a doctor, a musician) to hear confessions and give the last rites. Sentenced to death, he escaped to the United States, Guatemala and Cuba.

Named bishop of Veracruz in 1919, he arrived to find the region devastated by an earthquake and threw himself into relief work. When persecution closed the seminaries, he kept his own running in secret in Mexico City for 15 years, training hundreds of priests. Repeatedly exiled and threatened, he died in Mexico City on 6 June 1938, worn out and poor, having given away everything.

His life is well documented. Stories of his narrow escapes, told by those who knew him, have grown in the retelling.

Path to sainthood

Beatified by John Paul II in 1995 and canonised by Pope Benedict XVI on 15 October 2006. He is often described as the first Latin American-born bishop to be canonised.

Patronages

Bishops (popularly), Veracruz.

Where to visit

Xalapa Cathedral, Veracruz (his tomb); his birthplace, Cotija (Michoacán)

Trivia

  • When his body was exhumed in 1950, it was reported to be incorrupt.
  • Once, it's said, he went in person to a government office to hand himself in, so that the officials would stop persecuting his priests.
  • Because of the persecution, he was consecrated bishop in exile, in Havana, Cuba.
