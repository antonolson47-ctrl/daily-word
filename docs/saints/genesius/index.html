St GenesiusThe actor converted on stage

Artistic illustration in a Renaissance fresco style, created for The Daily Word; not a historical likeness. Genesius converting mid-performance before the emperor.

Livedd. c. 286–303, Rome
Feast day25 August
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Genesius is said to have been a comic actor in Rome, the leader of a troupe that performed before the Emperor Diocletian.

The full story

The play

The legend says the troupe put on a farce mocking Christian baptism. Genesius played a sick man asking to be baptised. When the "priest" and "exorcist" poured water over him, he was suddenly converted. Still on stage, he declared to the emperor that he had seen angels and now believed in Christ. Diocletian had him tortured and beheaded.

Theatre and art

The story inspired plays: Lope de Vega's Lo fingido verdadero (1608) and Jean Rotrou's Le Véritable Saint Genest (1646). His relics are claimed by the church of San Giovanni della Pigna and by Santa Susanna in Rome.

Legend. The Acts of Genesius date from the 5th–6th century and are thought to be legendary; the story is also told of other actor-martyrs. A Genesius of Arles, a notary martyred about 303, was a real saint and may have been the origin of the Roman tale.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint.

Patronages

Actors, comedians, clowns, musicians, dancers, converts.

Trivia

  • The St Genesius chapel in many theatres and the "Actors' Church" (St Paul's, Covent Garden) keep his memory.
  • Pope Benedict XVI said he was "a saint for our time" in a message to artists.
