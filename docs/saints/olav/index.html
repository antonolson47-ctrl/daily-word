St OlavViking king and patron of Norway

Norwegian: Olav den helliges fall i slaget på Stiklestadtitle QS:P1476,no:"Olav by Peter Nicolai Arbo, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived995–1030, Norway
Feast day29 July
RecognisedPre-Congregation; declared a saint 1031

The story

Olav Haraldsson was a Viking raider in England, France and the Baltic who was baptised in Rouen about 1013. He became King of Norway in 1015 and set about making it a Christian kingdom, building churches and introducing Christian laws, often by force. Driven out by Cnut the Great in 1028, he returned in 1030 and was killed at the battle of Stiklestad on 29 July 1030.

The full story

The saint king

A year later, in 1031, his body was dug up and found incorrupt, with hair and nails grown. Bishop Grimkell declared him a saint. Miracles were reported at his grave in Nidaros (Trondheim), where Nidaros Cathedral was built over it, and pilgrims came from all over the North along the routes now revived as St Olav's Ways (see our St Olav's Way pages).

Legends

Norwegian folklore is full of Olav: he is said to have fought trolls, made springs gush where he struck the ground, and carved paths through mountains. He is shown with an axe, the weapon of his death, standing on a dragon or troll.

History and legend. His reign, conversion policy and death are historical, known from skaldic poems and Snorri Sturluson's sagas. The troll stories are folklore.

Path to sainthood

Declared a saint by local bishop in 1031; venerated across Christendom; still honoured by the Lutheran Church of Norway.

Patronages

Norway, the Faroe Islands, carvers.

Trivia

  • His name is on the Norwegian coat of arms indirectly: the lion holds St Olav's axe.
  • St Olave's Church in London's Hart Street was dedicated to him in memory of his help against the Danes in 1014.
