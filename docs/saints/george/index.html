St GeorgeSoldier martyr and dragon-slayer of legend

Saint George and the Dragon (c. 1506), Raphael · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. c. AD 303, Lydda (Palestine)
Feast day23 April
RecognisedPre-Congregation

His story

George was a soldier who, according to early tradition, was martyred at Lydda (modern Lod, Israel) around 303, during the persecution under Diocletian. His cult spread widely and early, but the details of his life are uncertain; already in 494 a list attributed to Pope Gelasius placed him among saints "whose names are justly reverenced among men, but whose acts are known only to God". The famous dragon story appears only centuries later and was popularised by the 13th-century Golden Legend: George kills a dragon that terrorises a town and rescues a princess, and the town is baptised.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint, venerated in both East and West. He remains on the General Roman Calendar.

Patronages

England, Georgia, Catalonia, soldiers, scouts.

Trivia

  • The red cross on a white field of England's flag is St George's cross.
  • In Catalonia, 23 April (Sant Jordi) is a day for giving roses and books.
  • The dragon legend is medieval; the historical George, if we knew more, would have been a Roman soldier, not a knight.
