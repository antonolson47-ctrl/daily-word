St Kevin of GlendaloughThe hermit and the blackbird

Condado de Wicklow - Glendalough - 20170827115227 by Rafesmar, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 498–618, Wicklow (Ireland)
Feast day3 June
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Kevin (Cóemgen, "fair-born") came from a noble Leinster family and became a hermit in the remote valley of Glendalough, "the valley of two lakes", in the Wicklow Mountains. He lived for some years in a cave above the upper lake, now called St Kevin's Bed. Disciples gathered, and the monastery he founded became one of the great monastic cities of Ireland, with churches, a round tower and a school.

The full story

The blackbird

The best-loved legend says that Kevin was praying with arms outstretched when a blackbird landed on his open palm and laid an egg. He stayed still, arms out, until the eggs had hatched and the chicks had flown. The Nobel poet Seamus Heaney retold it in his poem "St Kevin and the Blackbird".

An old saint

His Lives say he lived to 120, and were written centuries later. They tell of an otter that brought him salmon and returned his psalter when he dropped it in the lake.

History or legend? Kevin and his monastery are historical. The animal stories and his great age are legend, written to show his gentleness and holiness.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint; canonised by tradition, and listed as a saint by Pope Pius X in 1903.

Patronages

Blackbirds, Dublin (co-patron), the archdiocese of Dublin.

Trivia

  • The monastic ruins at Glendalough, with the 30 m round tower and "St Kevin's Kitchen", are among the most visited sites in Ireland.
  • The Wicklow Way and St Kevin's Way pilgrim path both lead to Glendalough.
