St MatthiasChosen by lot to take Judas's place

St Matthias, Anthony van Dyck · public domain, Yale University Art Gallery, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1st century, Jerusalem
Feast day14 May
RecognisedPre-Congregation (biblical)

His story

After the Ascension, and before Pentecost, Peter stood up among about a hundred and twenty believers and said that Judas's place in the Twelve should be filled. The condition was narrow: a man who had been with them the whole time the Lord went in and out, from the baptism of John until the day he was taken up, a witness of the resurrection (Acts 1:21–22). Two men were put forward. One was Joseph, called Barsabbas, and surnamed Justus. The other was Matthias. They prayed, "Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all men, shew whether of these two thou hast chosen," and they cast lots. The lot fell on Matthias, and he was numbered with the eleven (Acts 1:23–26).

That is the whole of what the New Testament says about him. He does not speak. He does not travel in the later chapters of Acts. Paul, who will call himself an apostle because he has seen the risen Lord, is not in this election. The church in that upper room believed the Twelve were a sign of the renewed Israel, and that a vacant chair should not stay empty. They also believed the choice was God's, which is why they used the lot and not a vote they could steer.

Later lists send him to Judea, or to Cappadocia, or to "Ethiopia", a name ancient writers used loosely for lands to the south and not always for the country of that name today. They do not agree. His death is told as stoning, or beheading, or the axe, which is why painters put an axe or a halberd in his hand. Van Dyck's picture uses that attribute. It is not a likeness from life. Relics are claimed at Trier, with a story that Helena brought them, and also in Rome and at Santa Giustina in Padua. Those are the shrines' own tellings. Acts itself stops at the lot.

The calendar

The older Western date was 24 February, pushed to the 25th in leap years because it sat next to the old leap day. The Roman calendar of 1969 moved him to 14 May, where he is kept now, still in Eastertide, which fits a man chosen between Ascension and Pentecost. He is a feast of the apostles, not a memorial.

Path to sainthood

An apostle by the choice recorded in Acts. Pre-Congregation.

Patronages

Carpenters and tailors in some medieval lists, from the tools of the martyrdom legends; people who have been chosen for a work they did not campaign for.

Trivia

  • The other candidate, Joseph Barsabbas Justus, disappears from the story. The church remembered the man the lot did not choose by name, and then no further.
  • Casting lots was already a Jewish way of leaving a decision with God (Proverbs 16:33). It is not a command for later church elections.
  • He is the only apostle in the New Testament whose call we watch happening after Easter.
