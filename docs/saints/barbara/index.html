St BarbaraLocked in a tower; patron of artillery and miners

Saint Barbara by Master Bolfgangus, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived3rd century, traditionally Nicomedia or Heliopolis
Feast day4 December
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Barbara was, in legend, the beautiful daughter of a rich pagan, Dioscorus, who locked her in a tower to keep her from suitors. While he was away she became a Christian and had a third window put in the bath house he was building, in honour of the Holy Trinity.

The full story

The lightning

When her father discovered her faith, he denounced her to the governor. She was tortured, and her father himself beheaded her on a mountain. On the way down he was struck by lightning and burned to ashes. So she became patron of everyone who works with sudden fire and explosions: artillerymen, miners, firefighters and explosives workers. The powder magazine on a Spanish or Italian warship is still called the santabárbara.

Barbara branches

In Germany and Central Europe, cherry branches cut on her feast, 4 December, and put in water are expected to blossom by Christmas, the "Barbara branches" (Barbarazweige), a sign of new life in winter.

Legend. Her story is late and legendary, and there is no early evidence of her life. Her feast was removed from the General Roman Calendar in 1969, but she remains in the Martyrology and is very popular in the Eastern Churches and in mining regions.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint; feast removed from the universal calendar in 1969.

Patronages

Artillery, miners, firefighters, mathematicians, architects, sudden death.

Trivia

  • She is one of the Fourteen Holy Helpers.
  • Paintings show her with a three-windowed tower.
  • Santa Barbara, California, is named after her.
