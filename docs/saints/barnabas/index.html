St BarnabasSon of encouragement, companion of Paul, apostle to Cyprus

Paul and Barnabas at Lystra, Jacob Jordaens · public domain, Hermitage Museum, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1st century, Cyprus and the Pauline mission
Feast day11 June
RecognisedPre-Congregation (biblical)

His story

Luke introduces him before he introduces the work: Joseph, a Levite of Cyprus, whom the apostles surnamed Barnabas, "which is, being interpreted, The son of consolation" (Acts 4:36). He sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet. When Saul came back from Damascus and the Jerusalem church was afraid of him, Barnabas took him, brought him to the apostles, and told them how Saul had seen the Lord (Acts 9:26–27). That introduction is the reason there is a Paul the churches could trust.

The church at Antioch was growing, and Jerusalem sent Barnabas. He was glad, Luke says, and he went to Tarsus to look for Saul and brought him back. For a whole year they taught together, and in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians (Acts 11:22–26). When a famine was coming, Antioch sent relief to Judea by the hands of Barnabas and Saul (Acts 11:27–30).

Then the Holy Spirit said, "Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them" (Acts 13:2). They sailed to Cyprus, Barnabas's own island. At Paphos the proconsul Sergius Paulus believed, and a magician named Elymas was struck blind. From there the narrative starts to say "Paul and his company". At Lystra the crowd tried to sacrifice to them, calling Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, "because he was the chief speaker" (Acts 14:12). Jordaens's painting is that moment: a bull, garlands, and the two men tearing their clothes to stop the worship. Paul was then stoned and left for dead, and he got up and went on. They appointed elders and returned to Antioch.

At the council in Jerusalem both men told what God had done among the Gentiles (Acts 15). Afterwards they planned another journey. Barnabas wanted to take John Mark, his cousin (Colossians 4:10), who had left them once. Paul refused. "The contention was so sharp between them, that they departed asunder" (Acts 15:39). Barnabas took Mark and sailed to Cyprus. Paul took Silas. Luke follows Paul. He does not say the friendship was finished. Paul still speaks of Barnabas as a fellow worker (1 Corinthians 9:6; Galatians 2:1, 9), and Mark is back with Paul in the later letters.

Luke calls him an apostle alongside Paul (Acts 14:14), though he was not one of the Twelve. The quarrel is told in full. The so-called Epistle of Barnabas is not his. It is a second-century treatise, probably from Alexandria, which borrows his famous name. A fifth-century story, long loved in Cyprus, says the bishop Anthemius of Salamis found Barnabas's body, with a copy of Matthew on his chest, and that the find helped the Church of Cyprus claim its independence. The Monastery of St Barnabas near Salamis still marks the tomb the island keeps for him. It stands in the north of Cyprus. Check current travel advice before planning a visit.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation, from the apostolic age. Feast 11 June.

Patronages

Cyprus, and people who make peace between a newcomer and a suspicious community. Invoked against hail in some rural lists, a local custom rather than a Roman decree.

Trivia

  • "Son of encouragement" (or consolation) is Luke's translation of the Aramaic name. The Greek is huios paraklēseōs.
  • At Lystra, Barnabas is taken for Zeus, the senior god, and Paul for Hermes, the spokesman. Luke's detail is exact, and a little wry.
  • Mark's later restoration to Paul is the quiet end of the quarrel. Barnabas's stubbornness about the younger man was not wasted.
