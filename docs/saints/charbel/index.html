St CharbelThe hermit of Annaya

Charbel Makhlouf, photograph before 1898, unknown photographer · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1828–1898, Bekaa Kafra–Annaya
Feast day24 July
RecognisedCanonised 9 October 1977 (Paul VI)
PlaceAnnaya, Lebanon

His story

Youssef Antoun Makhlouf was born on 8 May 1828 at Bekaa Kafra, the highest village in the mountains of northern Lebanon. His father died when the boy was three. He grew up in a Maronite family, tended animals, and from early on wanted the monastery. He did not get there quickly. He entered the Lebanese Maronite Order in 1851, at twenty-three, and took the name Charbel, after a second-century martyr of the Antiochene church. He was ordained on 23 July 1859.

For fifteen years he lived the common life at the monastery of St Maron at Annaya: the liturgy, the fields, the guests, very few words. In 1875 his superiors let him do what he had asked for since the novitiate. He moved to the hermitage of Sts Peter and Paul, a short walk up the hill, with two other monks and a rule that was mostly silence. He stayed twenty-three years. People who came for confession or for a blessing said he barely looked at them. He celebrated the Maronite liturgy every day, slowly. The photograph that survives shows a face already turned inward, eyes down, the hood up.

On 16 December 1898, during the liturgy, he suffered a stroke. He died eight days later, on the night of 24 December. The Roman Martyrology notes the day: he had gone up from the monastery at Annaya into the hermitage, and served God there in fasting and prayer, and rested in the Lord on 24 December.

After the burial, the monastery reported a light at the tomb and a liquid seeping from it. Months later the tomb was opened. The body, the monks said, was incorrupt, flexible, and warm, and a fluid like blood and water continued to come from it. Pilgrims came from the villages around, Christian and Muslim and Druze, which in Lebanon is its own kind of evidence: the road to Annaya was never a single community's road. Paul VI beatified him on 5 December 1965, at the close of the Second Vatican Council, and canonised him on 9 October 1977. He was the first modern Lebanese saint to be canonised. The body was long venerated as incorrupt; the tomb at the monastery of St Maron is still the pilgrimage.

Path to sainthood

Beatified by Paul VI on 5 December 1965 and canonised by him on 9 October 1977. The memorial in the Roman Martyrology, and the feast kept in Lebanon and in many Latin calendars, is 24 July. Maronites also remember him on the third Sunday of July, and on 24 December, the day he died.

Patronages

Lebanon, and people seeking a way through a divided family or a divided town. Invoked for healing, which is why the monastery keeps ex-votos.

Trivia

  • Annaya is above the town of Jbeil (Byblos), at about 1,200 metres. The hermitage is a short walk above the monastery, and it is still furnished as a monk's room.
  • His name is also spelled Sharbel, and the surname Makhlouf or Makhluf. The Martyrology latinises him as Sarbelius.
  • The old photograph, taken before 1898, is the face on the holy cards. There is no painted legend to compete with it.
