Sts Francisco and Jacinta MartoThe child seers of Fátima

Francisco and Jacinta Marto (photograph, 1917), unknown photographer · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

LivedFrancisco 1908–1919; Jacinta 1910–1920
Feast day20 February
RecognisedCanonised 13 May 2017 (Francis)
PlaceFátima, Portugal

Their story

Francisco and Jacinta Marto were a brother and sister from the hamlet of Aljustrel, near Fátima in central Portugal. With their cousin Lúcia dos Santos they looked after their families' sheep.

Between 13 May and 13 October 1917, the three children reported six apparitions of a Lady "brighter than the sun" at the Cova da Iria, asking for prayer, especially the rosary, and penance for sinners. Crowds grew month by month despite official hostility; the children were even jailed and threatened for a time. On 13 October, tens of thousands of people reported seeing the sun spin and plunge, the "Miracle of the Sun", reported in the Portuguese secular press.

Both children caught the Spanish flu in the 1918 pandemic. Francisco, quiet and contemplative, died at home on 4 April 1919, aged ten; Jacinta, who offered many sacrifices for sinners, died alone in a Lisbon hospital on 20 February 1920, aged nine.

The apparitions are approved by the Church as "worthy of belief" (1930), which means Catholics may believe them, not that they must. The solar phenomenon is documented by many witnesses, but there is no scientific explanation and it was not seen by observatories.

Path to sainthood

Beatified by John Paul II at Fátima on 13 May 2000 and canonised by Pope Francis at Fátima on 13 May 2017, the centenary of the first apparition. They are the youngest non-martyrs ever canonised.

Patronages

Sick children (popularly), Portugal.

Where to visit

Sanctuary of Fátima: Chapel of the Apparitions and the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary (their tombs); their family home in Aljustrel

Trivia

  • The miracle approved for their canonisation was the healing of a Brazilian boy who had fallen from a window in 2013.
  • Their cousin Lúcia became a Carmelite nun and died in 2005, aged 97; her own cause for sainthood is under way.
  • Fátima draws several million pilgrims a year; the big pilgrimages are on 12–13 May and 12–13 October.
