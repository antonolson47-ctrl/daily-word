St CuthbertWonder-worker of the North, warmed by otters

Cuthbert discovers piece of timber - Life of St. Cuthbert (late 12th C), f.45v - by Bede, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 634–687, Northumbria
Feast day20 March
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Cuthbert was a shepherd boy who, one night in 651, saw a light in the sky like angels carrying a soul to heaven; it was the night St Aidan died. He became a monk at Melrose, then prior of Lindisfarne, then a hermit on the Inner Farne, and finally, unwillingly, bishop of Lindisfarne. He died on Inner Farne on 20 March 687. (See our St Cuthbert's Way pages.)

The full story

The otters

St Bede tells that while staying at Coldingham, Cuthbert went down to the sea at night and stood in the cold water up to his neck, praying until dawn. A monk who followed him secretly saw that when he came out, two otters bounded up, warmed his feet with their breath and dried them with their fur, then slipped back into the sea. Cuthbert, guessing he had been seen, asked the monk to tell no one until after his death.

The incorrupt body

When his tomb was opened in 698, eleven years after his death, his body was found incorrupt. Fleeing the Vikings, the monks carried it around the North for over a century, until it came to rest at Durham in 995, where the great cathedral was built over it. His coffin, pectoral cross and the St Cuthbert Gospel (now in the British Library) survive.

History and legend. Cuthbert is well documented by an anonymous Lindisfarne monk and by Bede. The otter story is told by Bede, who said he had it from a witness, but it is best read as a miracle story showing harmony between the saint and creation.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint.

Patronages

Northumbria, Durham, shepherds, sailors.

Trivia

  • Eider ducks on the Farne Islands are still called "Cuddy's ducks"; Cuthbert made rules protecting them in 676, one of the earliest bird-protection laws.
  • His shrine is behind the high altar of Durham Cathedral.
