St AthanasiusAthanasius against the world

Icon of St Athanasius of Alexandria · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 296–373, Alexandria
Feast day2 May
RecognisedPre-Congregation; Doctor of the Church
PlaceAlexandria

His story

Athanasius was an Alexandrian, born about 296, and still a deacon when he went to Nicaea in 325 as secretary to his bishop, Alexander. The council was meeting because a priest of Alexandria, Arius, had been teaching that the Son was not eternal: there was, Arius said, when the Son was not. Nicaea answered with a word Arius would not say. The Son is homoousios, of one being with the Father. Athanasius spent the rest of his life refusing to let that word be bargained away.

He became bishop of Alexandria in 328. He was driven out five times. One exile was in Trier, in the Latin West. Another was in Rome, where Pope Julius received him. He hid among the monks of Egypt, the people he knew from his friendship with Anthony the Abbot, whose Life he wrote. That book, more than any decree, carried the monastic life out of the desert and into the Latin world: Augustine would later be turned by a page of it. When a visitor told him "the world is against you", the later tradition put a Latin sentence on his answer: Athanasius contra mundum, Athanasius against the world. He did not coin the slogan. He lived the fact.

He was not a comfortable man. His enemies accused him of violence, of magic, even of murdering a bishop named Arsenius, and then produced a hand they said was the dead man's. Arsenius was found alive, with both his hands. The court politics were real, and Athanasius could be fierce. What he would not do was sign a creed that made the Son a creature. In his Festal Letter of 367 he set down the twenty-seven books of the New Testament as the Church in Egypt read them, the list the wider Church came to share. His treatise On the Incarnation puts the whole argument in one line he repeats in different words: the Word became human so that we might share the life of God.

He died in his own city, in peace, on 2 May 373. Gregory Nazianzen, preaching soon after, called him the pillar of the Church. Bernini later set his statue, with Ambrose, Chrysostom and Augustine, around the Chair of Peter in the apse of St Peter's.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation. Honoured as a Doctor of the Church in East and West. His memorial on 2 May is on the General Roman Calendar.

Patronages

Theologians, and people who have to hold a true thing when the room disagrees. Relics are claimed at the Coptic cathedral of St Mark in Alexandria, at San Zaccaria in Venice, and at Sant'Atanasio in Rome.

Trivia

  • Five exiles in about forty-five years as bishop. The count is the usual one; the exact itinerary is argued.
  • The phrase "Athanasius contra mundum" became English shorthand, used by Newman and others, for a person who will not move with the majority.
  • He was small. Julian the Apostate, who hated him, is said to have called him "hardly a man, only a little fellow". The stories of his size are later, and fond.
