St FaustinaThe sister who was told to paint mercy

St Faustina Kowalska (1931), unknown photographer · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1905–1938, Głogowiec–Kraków
Feast day5 October
RecognisedCanonised 30 April 2000 (John Paul II)
PlaceKraków-Łagiewniki, Poland

Her story

Helena Kowalska was born on 25 August 1905 at Głogowiec, a village in central Poland, the third of ten children of a farming family. She left school after a few years and went into service in other people's houses so there would be money at home. At twenty, against her parents' first answer, she entered the Sisters of Our Lady of Mercy in Warsaw and took the name Maria Faustina. She worked as cook, gardener and porter. Her superiors did not think of her as remarkable. She was often ill.

On 22 February 1931, in her cell at Płock, she wrote that she saw Jesus in a white robe, one hand raised, the other touching his chest, with two rays coming from it, one red and one pale. She recorded the words she understood: paint an image of this, with the sentence Jesus, I trust in you, and that the soul who honoured it would not perish. A painter in Vilnius, Eugeniusz Kazimirowski, made the first image in 1934 under her direction and her confessor's. A later painting by Adolf Hyła, still in Kraków, is the one most people know. With the image came a chaplet, an hour of prayer at three in the afternoon, and a request for a feast of Divine Mercy on the Sunday after Easter. She wrote it all down. The Diary runs to hundreds of pages. She called herself, because she said Christ had, the secretary of his mercy.

She coughed blood. Tuberculosis killed her at the convent in Kraków-Łagiewniki on 5 October 1938. She was thirty-three.

Then the devotion stalled. In 1959 the Holy Office stopped the circulation of the images and writings as they were then being spread. A faulty translation of the Diary had made some of the language look doctrinally wild, and the cause was put aside. A critical Polish edition was made. Karol Wojtyła, archbishop of Kraków, took the case up in the 1960s. As John Paul II he beatified her on 18 April 1993 and canonised her on 30 April 2000, and on that same morning he made Divine Mercy Sunday a feast of the whole Latin Church. He died on 2 April 2005, the eve of that Sunday.

The shrine at Łagiewniki, built out from her convent, holds her tomb under the image. It is one of the main pilgrimage places in Poland.

Path to sainthood

Beatified in 1993 and canonised on 30 April 2000 by John Paul II, who also instituted Divine Mercy Sunday that day. Her optional memorial is 5 October, the day she died. The 1959 notification is part of the story, not a footnote to hide: the devotion the Church now keeps went through a real correction of its texts.

Patronages

Mercy, and people who do not think they can be forgiven. World Youth Day in 2002 and the Jubilee of Mercy in 2016 both leaned on her diary. She was not a founder and not a scholar. The message was the work.

Trivia

  • The two rays, she wrote, are blood and water, the red and the pale that John saw from the side of Christ.
  • "Jesus, I trust in you" is Jezu, ufam Tobie on the Polish images. The sentence is the devotion in five words.
  • The 1931 photograph shows a young sister in the habit of her congregation, a small crucifix on her chest, not yet ill enough for the later pictures.
