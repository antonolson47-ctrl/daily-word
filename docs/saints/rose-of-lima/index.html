St Rose of LimaFirst saint of the Americas

Spanish: Santa Rosa de Limalabel QS:Les,"Santa Rosa de Lima" by Claudio Coello, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1586–1617, Lima (Peru)
Feast day23 August
Canonised1671, Clement X

The story

Isabel Flores de Oliva was born in Lima in 1586. A family servant said her face looked like a rose, and at her confirmation she took the name Rose. Very beautiful, she refused marriage, cut her hair and rubbed pepper on her face to avoid admirers. She became a Dominican tertiary at 20, modelling her life on St Catherine of Siena, and lived in a tiny hut she built in her family's garden.

The full story

Prayer and service

She grew flowers and did embroidery to support her family, and turned a room in the house into an infirmary for the poor, the sick, Native people and enslaved Africans. Her penances were extreme, including a silver crown with spikes hidden under a wreath of roses. She died on 24 August 1617, aged 31, and the whole city came to her funeral.

The pirates

In 1615 the Dutch pirate fleet of Joris van Spilbergen appeared off Callao. Rose gathered the women in church to pray before the Blessed Sacrament, ready to defend it with her life. The fleet sailed away, and Lima credited her prayers.

History. Rose's life is well documented in the testimony for her beatification. Her penances, admired in her time, were extreme and were not offered as an example to copy.

Path to sainthood

Beatified 1668 by Clement IX; canonised 1671 by Clement X, the first saint born in the Americas.

Patronages

The Americas, the Philippines, Peru, gardeners, florists, embroiderers.

Trivia

  • Her face appears on the Peruvian 200-sol banknote.
  • Her feast, 30 August, is a national holiday in Peru (in the General Roman Calendar it is 23 August).
