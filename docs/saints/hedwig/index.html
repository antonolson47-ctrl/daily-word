St Hedwig of SilesiaThe duchess who walked barefoot in the snow, carrying her shoes

Legend of Saint Hedwig of Silesia Triptych (left panel) · Unknown authorUnknown author · Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1174–1243, Andechs (Bavaria) – Trzebnica (Silesia)
Feast day16 October
RecognisedCanonised 26 March 1267 (Clement IV)

The story

Born at Andechs to Bavarian counts (and aunt of St Elizabeth of Hungary), Jadwiga — Hedwig — was married at about twelve to Henry the Bearded, duke of Silesia. In a frontier duchy being rebuilt with towns, mines and monasteries, she became its conscience: founding hospitals, feeding the poor from the ducal kitchen, and ransoming captives.

The full story

Barefoot duchess

Her Vita describes a deliberate downward mobility: plain dress, barefoot walks even in Silesian winter (carrying shoes in her hand to satisfy critics, which rather misses — and makes — the point), a hairshirt late in life, and long prayer before a small ivory Madonna she carried everywhere. With Henry she founded the Cistercian convent at Trzebnica (1202), the first women's religious house in Silesia.

Widow among ruins

Henry died in 1238; their son Henry the Pious fell against the Mongols at Legnica in 1241, where Hedwig is said to have identified his body among the dead. She retired to Trzebnica among the nuns without taking vows, died in 1243, and pilgrims were at her tomb within weeks.

History vs legend. The 13th-century Vita maior is close to living memory and her foundations are documented in charters. Miracles at the tomb and prophecies were added in the devotional telling; the charity, foundations and barefoot austerity are the historical core.

Path to sainthood

Canonised by Pope Clement IV in 1267; her relics at Trzebnica drew Silesian, Polish and German pilgrims for centuries.

Patronages

Silesia, Poland and Germany, brides, widows, and those who have lost children.

Trivia

  • She is depicted with a church in one hand (her foundations) and her shoes in the other — the famous compromise with her confessor.
  • Aunt of St Elizabeth of Hungary and great-aunt of St Elizabeth of Portugal: sanctity ran in this family network.
