St Juliana of NicomediaShe refused a pagan marriage and, in legend, wrestled a demon in prison

Menologion of Basil II - Juliana of Nicomedia · Authors of Menologion of Basil II (circa 985 AC, Constantinople), Byzantine manu · Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. c. 304, Nicomedia / Cumae (traditional)
Feast day16 February
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Juliana's Passio — early enough to put her name in the oldest martyrologies, embroidered enough to read like a novel — makes her the Christian daughter of a pagan official of Nicomedia, betrothed to the prefect Eleusius. She refused marriage unless he converted and renounced idols; he had her arrested in the Diocletian persecution.

The full story

The demon in the cell

In prison, the devil appeared as an angel of light urging her to sacrifice and save herself. Juliana prayed, seized the demon, beat him (art shows her leading him by a chain or horn, exasperated as a housewife with a pest) and forced him to confess his tricks — then was wheeled, burned and finally beheaded, tradition says, with Eleusius watching his power fail.

From Nicomedia to Naples

Her relics' travels (Cumae, then Naples, then Brussels and Spain in fragments) spread a cult beloved in the Netherlands and England — Cynewulf wrote an Old English poem on her demon-fight, one of four signed poems we have from early England.

Legend. A martyr Juliana at Nicomedia under Diocletian is early attested; the marriage plot, demon wrestling and torture catalogue are romance. The chained devil is theology in picture form: discernment defeats the counterfeit angel.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation; in the Hieronymian Martyrology and medieval calendars East and West.

Patronages

Childbirth and sickness (medieval invocation), Cumae and Juliana churches.

Trivia

  • An Old English poet signed his name in runes into his poem about her — literary celebrity, 8th/9th century style.
  • Art loves her annoyed expression dragging the demon: sanctity with no patience for nonsense.
