Sts Joachim and AnneNamed in tradition as the parents of Mary

The meeting at the Golden Gate, Giotto, Scrovegni Chapel, Padua (c. 1305) · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1st century BC, if the tradition is followed
Feast day26 July
RecognisedPre-Congregation (tradition, not Scripture)

Their story

The New Testament never names Mary's parents. What the Church has long told about them comes from the Protoevangelium of James, an infancy gospel usually dated to the second century. It is not Scripture. It is the book that gave them names: Joachim and Anne (Hannah), and a story shaped like the biblical stories of childless couples who are heard.

Joachim is a rich and righteous man who goes up to the Temple and is turned away, the tale says, because he has no child. He goes into the wilderness to fast. Anne stays home and grieves, and sees a sparrow's nest, and prays. An angel speaks to each of them. They hurry toward each other and meet at the Golden Gate of Jerusalem. Giotto painted that meeting on the walls of the Scrovegni Chapel around 1305: two people embracing in a gateway, the whole of the legend in one gesture. Mary is born. Her parents present her in the Temple. The rest of the book is about Mary's childhood and the birth of Jesus, and it is full of details the Gospels do not have.

Legend, and where it stops. The names, the rebuke at the Temple, the wilderness fast, the Golden Gate, and the presentation of the child Mary are from the Protoevangelium and from later tellings that repeat it. They are not in Matthew or Luke. The Church has loved the story without putting the book in the Bible. Mary's page on this site says the same thing in one sentence. This page is the longer saying of it.

What can still be said. Mary of Nazareth had parents and grandparents. Their names are not recorded by the evangelists. A devotion to her mother is very old in the East: by the sixth century a church of St Anne stood in Constantinople. In the West Anne's cult grew strong in the later Middle Ages, when the "holy kinship" became a favourite subject for altarpieces. Joachim was slower to receive his own feast. For a long time Anne was kept on 26 July and Joachim on 16 August. The Roman calendar of 1969 joined them on 26 July, which is the date this site uses.

Two cults, one legend

Anne became the grandmother people prayed to: for a child, for a safe birth, for a marriage, for the household. Joachim remained the quieter figure, the grandfather at the gate. Shrines claimed Anne's relics, notably at Apt in Provence, with a story that they had come from the East. That claim is a shrine legend. In 1625, at Auray in Brittany, a farmer named Yvon Nicolazic said St Anne had told him where a statue of her was buried. Ste-Anne-d'Auray grew from that report. It is a seventeenth-century apparition tradition. It is not part of the second-century book, and it is not evidence for the Golden Gate.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation, by long public devotion, not by a modern process and not by a verse of Scripture that names them. The Roman calendar remembers them together on 26 July as the parents of the Blessed Virgin Mary.

Patronages

Anne: grandmothers, mothers, women in labour, and homemakers. Joachim: grandfathers and fathers. Together they are the saints people ask to watch a family line.

Trivia

  • "Anne" is the Greek form of Hannah, the mother of Samuel, and the Protoevangelium wants the reader to hear that echo.
  • Giotto's cycle in Padua gives Joachim several scenes before Mary is born. The Golden Gate is the one Europe remembered.
  • A picture of Anne teaching Mary to read is a favourite of the later Middle Ages. It is devotion, not a scene from the Gospels.
