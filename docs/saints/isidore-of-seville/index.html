St Isidore of SevilleEncyclopedist, bishop and Doctor of the Church

Saint Isidore of Seville (1655), Bartolomé Esteban Murillo · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedc. 560–636, Cartagena?–Seville
Feast day4 April
RecognisedPre-Congregation; Doctor of the Church 1722
PlaceSeville / León, Spain

His story

Isidore came from a family of saints: his brothers Leander and Fulgentius were bishops, and his sister Florentina an abbess. Raised by Leander after their parents died, he succeeded him as bishop of Seville around 600 and led the Church in Visigothic Spain for over three decades. He presided over the Fourth Council of Toledo (633), which set out church discipline and urged a school attached to every cathedral.

He is remembered above all for the Etymologies, a 20-book encyclopedia that tried to gather all knowledge, from grammar and medicine to geography and theology, organised around the origins of words. It was one of the most-copied books of the Middle Ages and passed classical learning on to medieval Europe. He also wrote histories of the Goths, Vandals and Suevi, and works of theology.

His life is well documented. A pleasant legend says that as a baby a swarm of bees settled on his mouth, foretelling his eloquence (the same story is told of St Ambrose).

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint, venerated soon after his death. Pope Innocent XIII declared him a Doctor of the Church in 1722.

Patronages

Students, (unofficially) the internet and computer users.

Where to visit

Real Colegiata de San Isidoro, León (his tomb and the Royal Pantheon); Seville Cathedral

Trivia

  • His body was moved from Seville to León in 1063, to the basilica that now bears his name, with its magnificent Romanesque Royal Pantheon.
  • He is sometimes called "the last scholar of the ancient world".
  • The idea of him as patron of the internet is popular, but the Vatican has never officially declared it.
