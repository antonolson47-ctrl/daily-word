St Louis IXThe king who built a chapel for the Crown of Thorns

Saint Louis, King of France, El Greco · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Lived1214–1270, Poissy–Tunis
Feast day25 August
RecognisedCanonised 11 August 1297 (Boniface VIII)
PlaceParis, France

His story

Louis was twelve when his father, Louis VIII, died in 1226, and the crown of France came to him with a barons' revolt attached. His mother, Blanche of Castile, held the kingdom for him and did not pretend the work was gentle. He grew into a king who heard cases himself, sitting under an oak at Vincennes so that anyone could walk up, and who tried to stop the private wars of his nobles. He is the French king of the justice-tales: the man who would rather lose a lawsuit than keep a doubtful right. Joinville, who went on crusade with him and wrote his life, is the witness. Joinville admired him and also argued with him, which makes the portrait usable.

In 1239 Louis bought the Crown of Thorns from Baldwin II, the Latin emperor of Constantinople, who was selling relics to pay his debts. Other passion relics followed. To hold them he built the Sainte-Chapelle in the palace on the Île de la Cité, a reliquary the size of a church, glass almost from floor to vault. It was consecrated on 26 April 1248. That August he left for the Seventh Crusade.

The crusade was a disaster told honestly by the same Joinville. The army landed in Egypt, took Damietta, stalled, and was cut to pieces in the canals around Mansurah. Louis was taken prisoner in 1250, sick, and ransomed for a sum that hurt France. He stayed in the Holy Land four more years, rebuilding defences, and came home in 1254. He did not treat the failure as someone else's. He lived more austerely after it, fed lepers, and founded the abbey of Royaumont and, for his sister Isabelle, the Poor Clare house at Longchamp.

In 1270 he sailed again, for Tunis, hoping to begin a new passage east. Dysentery went through the camp. He died at Tunis on 25 August 1270, on a bed of ashes, the legend of his death says, with the words of the psalm. His bones were carried home. Most of the royal tombs at Saint-Denis were wrecked in the Revolution; a few relics of Louis survived. The Crown of Thorns stayed at Notre-Dame for centuries. It was carried out of the burning cathedral on the night of 15 April 2019.

Path to sainthood

Boniface VIII canonised him on 11 August 1297, less than thirty years after his death. The politics were real: a French king, a pope who needed France, a canonisation that also said something about the throne. The inquiry still collected miracles and the memory of a man who had tried to be just. His optional memorial is 25 August, the day he died.

Patronages

France (with others), the Third Order of St Francis, which claims him as a tertiary, masons, and difficult marriages. The city of St Louis, Missouri, is named for him, which is why the American city has a king of France for a patron.

Trivia

  • El Greco's portrait puts him in gold armour with a boy page holding a fleur-de-lis. It was painted in Toledo, three centuries after Louis died. It is a king's face, not a death mask.
  • The Sainte-Chapelle was the palace chapel, upstairs for the king and the relics, downstairs for the household. The upper chapel is the one visitors mean.
  • Joinville refused to go on the second crusade. He told the king, in effect, that his duty was now at home. Louis went anyway.
