St AndrewThe first-called apostle, brother of Peter

The Martyrdom of St Andrew (c. 1675–82), Bartolomé Esteban Murillo · public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. c. AD 60 (tradition), Patras
Feast day30 November
RecognisedPre-Congregation (apostle)

His story

Andrew was a fisherman from Bethsaida and Simon Peter's brother. John's Gospel says he was first a disciple of John the Baptist, followed Jesus, and then brought his brother to him: "We have found the Messiah" (John 1:40–42). The Eastern Church calls him the Protoklētos, the "First-called". He appears at the feeding of the five thousand, pointing out the boy with five loaves and two fish (John 6:8–9). Tradition says he preached around the Black Sea and Greece and was crucified at Patras.

Path to sainthood

An apostle, venerated from the beginning. No formal canonisation (pre-Congregation).

Patronages

Scotland, Greece, Russia, Ukraine and Romania; fishermen.

Trivia

  • The X-shaped saltire cross associated with him is a medieval tradition; earlier depictions show an ordinary cross.
  • The saltire on Scotland's flag, and the town of St Andrews, come from the tradition that some of his relics were brought there.
  • Most of his relics are venerated in the Cathedral of Amalfi, Italy, where they were brought from Constantinople in 1208.
