St Margaret of AntiochThe girl who burst out of a dragon

German: Hl. Margarete Saint Margaret and the Dragontitle QS:P1476,de:"Hl. Margar by Raphael, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Livedd. c. 304, Antioch in Pisidia
Feast day20 July
RecognisedPre-Congregation

The story

Margaret (Marina in the East) was, according to legend, the daughter of a pagan priest at Antioch in Pisidia (today Turkey). Driven out for being a Christian, she kept sheep with her nurse. The Roman prefect Olybrius wanted to marry her; when she refused and declared herself a Christian, he had her tortured and imprisoned.

The full story

The dragon

In prison the devil came to her as a huge dragon and swallowed her. But the cross she was holding irritated the dragon's insides (or grew larger), and it burst open, and Margaret stepped out unharmed. Because she came out of the dragon safe and sound, she became the patron of childbirth. She was later beheaded.

A voice for Joan

Margaret was one of the most popular saints of the Middle Ages and one of the Fourteen Holy Helpers. She was one of the three saints whose voices St Joan of Arc said she heard.

Legend. Even the medieval Golden Legend called the dragon story "apocryphal and not to be believed". Because there is no historical evidence for her life, her feast was removed from the General Roman Calendar in 1969, although she remains in the Roman Martyrology.

Path to sainthood

Pre-Congregation saint; feast removed from the universal calendar in 1969, still in the Martyrology.

Patronages

Pregnant women, childbirth, nurses, peasants.

Trivia

  • Over 250 English churches were dedicated to her, including St Margaret's, Westminster, beside the Abbey.
  • Raphael painted her stepping calmly past the dragon (Vienna, Louvre).
