Everything began on the road to Damascus, one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. Saul was riding north from Jerusalem with letters from the high priest to arrest followers of "the Way" when a light from heaven flashed around him, and he heard a voice: "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?" (Acts 9:4, ESV). Blinded, he was led into the city to a house on the street called Straight.

Travel warning. Syria remains subject to serious travel warnings from most governments. Check the latest advice (for example the US State Department) before you consider going. The sites below are described for readers and for future travel.

Good to know

  • The Old City of Damascus is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1979); its Christian quarter, Bab Touma, is at the east end of Straight Street.
  • The three Pauline sites are within a 15-minute walk: Straight Street, the Chapel of St Ananias, and St Paul's Chapel at Bab Kisan.
  • Most visitors who go now travel with a licensed local guide.

Deep Dive

What the Bible says

Luke tells the story three times (Acts 9, 22 and 26). The Lord sent a disciple named Ananias: "Rise and go to the street called Straight, and at the house of Judas look for a man of Tarsus named Saul" (Acts 9:11, ESV). Ananias laid hands on him, "something like scales fell from his eyes," and Saul was baptized. When the Jews of Damascus plotted to kill him, the disciples lowered him through an opening in the wall in a basket (Acts 9:25). Paul himself tells the same escape, adding that the governor under King Aretas was guarding the city (2 Corinthians 11:32–33), and that after his call he went away into Arabia before returning to Damascus (Galatians 1:17).

Archaeology and what to see

History. Straight Street (Via Recta, today Medhat Pasha and Bab Sharqi streets) is the Roman decumanus maximus, the main east–west street of the Roman grid, about 1.5 km long. It still runs straight across the Old City, and at its east end stands the Roman gate Bab Sharqi and remains of a Roman arch. Paul really did walk this street; the grid is that old.

  • The Chapel of St Ananias: a small underground chapel reached by steps in the Christian quarter, venerated since at least the Middle Ages as the house of Ananias. Excavations found remains of an earlier Byzantine church.
  • St Paul's Chapel, Bab Kisan: a church built into the old gate of Kisan in the city wall, marking by tradition where Paul was let down in the basket. The gate itself was rebuilt in the 14th century.
  • The Umayyad Mosque: built from 705 on the site of the Cathedral of St John the Baptist (and before that a temple of Jupiter); inside is a shrine venerated by Christians and Muslims as holding the head of John the Baptist.

Tradition. Kawkab, a hill about 15 km south-west of the city, has long been pointed out as the place of the conversion, and a Greek Orthodox monastery stands there. The Bible names no exact spot, saying only that it happened "as he approached Damascus."
