PhilippiThe first church in Europe: Lydia, the jailer, and the letter of joy

Photo: Philippi - 1 by MrPanyGoff, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

CountryGreece
NearKavala
Acts16:9–40; 20:6
UNESCO2016

At Troas Paul had a vision in the night: a man of Macedonia urging him, "Come over to Macedonia and help us" (Acts 16:9, ESV). He sailed across, landed at Neapolis (today's Kavala) and walked up the Via Egnatia to Philippi, a proud Roman colony. Here, around AD 49–50, he founded the first Christian community in Europe, the church he would later thank in his warmest letter.

Good to know

  • Getting there: Kavala airport (KVA) is 35 km away; Philippi is 16 km from Kavala on the road to Drama, with local buses.
  • The archaeological site (paid entry, closed some holidays) straddles the main road: the theatre, the acropolis slope and Basilica A on one side; the forum, Basilica B and the Octagon on the other.
  • Lydia's baptistery is a 5-minute drive west at the river, in the village of Lydia.
  • Kavala has a monument and church of St Paul by the harbour and a mosaic of his landing.

Deep Dive

What Acts tells

History in Acts. On the Sabbath, Paul, Silas, Timothy and Luke (the "we" passages begin here) went outside the gate to a place of prayer by the river. There they met Lydia, a dealer in purple cloth from Thyatira: "The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what was said by Paul" (Acts 16:14, ESV). She and her household were baptized, Europe's first recorded converts, and her house became the church's home. After Paul freed a slave girl from a spirit of divination, her owners had Paul and Silas beaten and thrown in prison. At midnight, as they sang hymns, an earthquake opened the doors; the jailer, about to kill himself, asked what he must do to be saved, and was baptized with his family that night. The next day, Paul revealed he was a Roman citizen, and the frightened magistrates came to apologize.

Philippi was a Roman colonia refounded after the Battle of Philippi in 42 BC, where Mark Antony and Octavian defeated Brutus and Cassius; its veterans and Latin inscriptions explain why Acts calls it "a Roman colony." Paul's later Letter to the Philippians, written from prison, is a letter of joy and friendship, with the great hymn of Christ who "emptied himself" (Philippians 2) and the line "For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (Philippians 1:21, ESV).

Archaeology and what to see

  • The forum of the 2nd century AD, laid out over the 1st-century square Paul knew.
  • The "prison of Paul": a small crypt-like room near Basilica A, venerated since early Christian times as the jail of Acts 16. Archaeologists think it was originally a Roman cistern; it was turned into a chapel and decorated with paintings.
  • Basilica A (late 5th century) and Basilica B (6th century), whose dome collapsed during construction, and the Octagon, the cathedral dedicated to St Paul, built over a 4th-century church whose mosaic inscription names Bishop Porphyrios.
  • The theatre, first built by Philip II of Macedon in the 4th century BC and later used for gladiator shows.
  • The Via Egnatia: the Roman highway's paving stones still cross the site.
  • Lydia's baptistery: a modern open-air baptistery and church by the Zygaktis stream, at the traditional place of Lydia's baptism, where pilgrims renew their baptismal promises.
