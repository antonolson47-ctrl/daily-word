AntiochWhere the disciples were first called Christians, and where every journey began

RebuildingAfter 2023 quake

Antioch on the Orontes was the third city of the Roman Empire after Rome and Alexandria, a capital of perhaps a quarter of a million people. Here the Gospel was first preached widely to non-Jews, and "in Antioch the disciples were first called Christians" (Acts 11:26, ESV). It was Paul's home church: each of his three journeys started here.

After the earthquakes. The earthquakes of 6 February 2023 devastated Antakya, killing tens of thousands across the region and destroying much of the old city. The cave Church of St Peter on the mountainside survived with damage. Check the current situation and opening times locally before you travel.

Good to know

  • Getting there: Hatay airport (HTY), about 20 km north; buses from Adana (about 3 hours).
  • The Church of St Peter (St. Pierre Kilisesi) is a museum site on the slope of Mount Stauris, about 2 km north-east of the centre. Christians gather there each year on 29 June.
  • The Hatay Archaeology Museum holds one of the world's great collections of Roman mosaics from Antioch and its suburb Daphne.

Deep Dive

A church of Jews and Gentiles

History. Believers scattered after Stephen's martyrdom reached Antioch and began to speak "to the Hellenists also" (Acts 11:20). The Jerusalem church sent Barnabas, who went to Tarsus to find Saul, and the two taught there for a whole year. The church sent famine relief to Judea, and in Acts 13 the Holy Spirit said, "Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul" while the prophets and teachers were worshipping and fasting; they were sent off from the port of Seleucia Pieria. It was also at Antioch that Paul confronted Peter over eating with Gentiles (Galatians 2:11–14), one of the most candid scenes in the New Testament.

Antioch went on to become one of the great patriarchates of the Church: Ignatius of Antioch was its bishop (martyred in Rome around 110), and John Chrysostom preached here before going to Constantinople.

What to see

  • The Church of St Peter: a cave on the mountainside that tradition says was used by the first Christians of Antioch, perhaps even by Peter himself. The stone façade was built by the Crusaders (12th–13th century) and restored in 1863; inside are traces of mosaic flooring, an altar and a tunnel used, it's said, for escape in times of persecution.
  • The Hatay Archaeology Museum: mosaics from the villas of Antioch and Daphne (2nd–6th centuries).
  • The Orontes river and the old quarter, where churches, mosques and the synagogue stood side by side until 2023.
  • Seleucia Pieria (near Samandağ, 30 km): the port from which Paul sailed, with the huge Titus tunnel, a Roman channel cut through rock.

Tradition. Antioch's Christians traditionally held that Luke the Evangelist was a native of Antioch (Eusebius records this), and that Peter was the city's first bishop before going to Rome, which is why the Church once kept a feast of the "Chair of Peter at Antioch."

Not to be confused with Pisidian Antioch

On his first journey Paul preached his first recorded sermon (Acts 13:16–41) at a different Antioch, Pisidian Antioch, near Yalvaç in central Türkiye, where the ruins of a Roman colony, its aqueduct and a 4th-century church traditionally linked to Paul can be visited.
