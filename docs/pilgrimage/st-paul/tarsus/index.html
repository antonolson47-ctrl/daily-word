Tarsus"A citizen of no obscure city": Paul's birthplace in Cilicia

"I am a Jew, from Tarsus in Cilicia, a citizen of no obscure city" (Acts 21:39, ESV). Paul was proud of his hometown, and rightly: Tarsus was a rich trading city at the foot of the Taurus Mountains, on the river Cydnus, famed for its schools of philosophy. Today it's a busy Turkish town of about 350,000, with a few precious traces of the city Paul knew.

Good to know

  • Getting there: Tarsus is 40 km from Adana airport and 30 km from Mersin, with frequent trains and buses.
  • St Paul's Church is a state museum; Christian groups may celebrate Mass with permission arranged in advance through the local Catholic church. The Catholic Apostolic Vicariate of Anatolia keeps a small presence here.
  • Feast: pilgrims gather on and around 29 June, the feast of Sts Peter and Paul.

Deep Dive

Paul's city

History. Tarsus was capital of the Roman province of Cilicia. Here, in 41 BC, Cleopatra sailed up the Cydnus in her gilded barge to meet Mark Antony. The geographer Strabo, a near-contemporary of Paul, wrote that the people of Tarsus were so devoted to learning that they surpassed even Athens and Alexandria. A city of Greek culture, Roman government and a strong Jewish community: no wonder Paul could quote Greek poets and speak to Romans, Greeks and Jews alike. The city was also famous for cilicium, a cloth woven from goat hair, used for tents, which fits Paul's trade.

After his conversion, the believers in Jerusalem sent Paul "off to Tarsus" (Acts 9:30), where he spent perhaps ten "hidden years," until Barnabas came to fetch him to Antioch (Acts 11:25–26).

What to see

  • St Paul's Church: an Orthodox church built in 1862 on an older site, now a museum, with ceiling frescoes of the four Evangelists. The Holy See has long asked that it be returned to use as a church.
  • St Paul's Well: in the old quarter, a well said to be at the site of Paul's family house. Pilgrims draw water from it. The well itself is Roman-period at best; the house identification is tradition.
  • The ancient Roman road: a well-preserved stretch of basalt-paved street with a colonnade, excavated in the town centre (Antik Yol), from the time of the Roman city.
  • Cleopatra's Gate (Kancık Kapı): a Roman-period city gate, heavily restored. It has nothing to do with Cleopatra, but it's the city's emblem.
  • The Tarsus Waterfall on the Cydnus (Berdan) river at the edge of town.

Tradition. Local Christians have long pointed to the well and the nearby ruins as the house where Paul was born. The tradition can't be proven, but the site has been visited by pilgrims, and during the Pauline Year (2008–09) the Turkish authorities allowed pilgrim groups to celebrate Mass in St Paul's Church.
