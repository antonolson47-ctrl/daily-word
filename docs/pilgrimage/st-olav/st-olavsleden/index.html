St. OlavsledenSelånger to Trondheim: from the Baltic to the Atlantic in Olav's last footsteps

Photo: Selångers kyrkoruin 04 by Hans Lindqvist, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Distance564 km
Days~30
StartSelånger, Sundsvall (SE)
ViaÖstersund · Åre · Stiklestad

St. Olavsleden is one of the world's northernmost pilgrim paths, running 564 km from the Baltic coast of Sweden to the Atlantic fjords of Norway. It follows the way Olav himself took in 1030, back from exile in Russia, across Sweden and over the mountains to his death at Stiklestad. It's the only St Olav Way that crosses a national border.

Good to know

  • Start: the ruined medieval church of Selånger, near Sundsvall, where Olav is said to have landed from the Baltic.
  • Two finishes: at Munkeby near Levanger the route splits into an inner way (Stiklestad–Trondheim, about 139 km, 7 days) and an outer way by the fjord to the island of Tautra (Munkeby–Tautra about 60 km, 3 days).
  • Cycling: Åre to Trondheim (inner way) is about 252 km, 5 days, by bike.
  • Getting there: fly or take the train to Sundsvall; the Mittbanan railway follows much of the route through Jämtland.
  • St Olavsleden has its own official site, run by the Swedish and Norwegian partners: stolavsleden.com.

Deep Dive

History

History. In the sagas Olav returned from Kievan Rus in 1030 by ship to Sweden, gathered men and crossed Jämtland and the mountains into Trøndelag. After his death, pilgrims from Sweden, Finland and the Baltic used this east–west road to reach his shrine for five centuries. Frösön, an island in Lake Storsjön by Östersund, has Sweden's northernmost runestone, raised by Östmaðr Guðfastarson, who "had Jämtland made Christian." The route was waymarked and opened in its modern form in 2013.

Notable stops

  • Selånger church ruins (12th century), the start, by the Selångersån river.
  • Borgsjö and the forests of Medelpad, the longest wilderness stretch.
  • Östersund and Frösön, with the runestone, by Storsjön, Sweden's lake of legend.
  • Åre old church (13th century), a stone church in the ski resort valley under Åreskutan.
  • Storlien and the border, crossing the watershed into Norway.
  • Stiklestad, the battlefield and the church over the place where Olav fell.
  • Mære church, a medieval church on the site of a pagan temple, one of the most important cult sites of pre-Christian Trøndelag.
  • Tautra Mariakloster, a community of Cistercian (Trappist) nuns on Tautra island since 1999, near the ruins of a medieval Cistercian abbey: a rare living Catholic monastery on the way.
