GudbrandsdalsledenOslo to Trondheim: the medieval main road over Dovrefjell

Photo: PermaLiv Gudbrandsdalen gårder 18-04-22 by Øyvind Holmstad, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Distance643 km
Days~32
StartSt Hallvard's ruins, Oslo
High point~1,300 m (Dovrefjell)

The most popular of the St Olav Ways follows the medieval king's road north from Oslo: along Norway's largest lake, Mjøsa, up the long valley of Gudbrandsdalen, over the bare high plateau of Dovrefjell, and down through the forests of Trøndelag to Nidaros. It's 643 km of well-marked trail, about four to five weeks on foot at 20 km a day, and trains run beside nearly all of it, so it's easy to walk a section.

Good to know

  • Start: the ruins of St Hallvard's Cathedral in Oslo's Middelalderparken. Soon after, the route splits: west of Mjøsa via Bærum, Hadeland, Granavollen and Gjøvik, or east via Eidsvoll, Stange and Hamar. They meet again at Lillehammer.
  • Dovrefjell: the route is "technically closed" over the mountain until about 10 June, when bridges are laid over the rivers Hondyrju and Vesle Hondyrju; snow and meltwater can last longer. If in doubt, take the train over the mountain.
  • Pilgrim centres in Oslo, Granavollen, Hamar, Dale-Gudbrand (Hundorp), Dovrefjell and Trondheim give advice and stamps.
  • Book beds a few days ahead: many hostels are small farms. About one pilgrim in five walks alone.
  • Combine with a boat: the paddle steamer Skibladner (1856), the oldest paddle steamer in regular service in the world, crosses Mjøsa between Gjøvik and Hamar in summer.

Deep Dive

History

History. This was the main pilgrim road of medieval Norway. King Øystein (died 1123) is credited with building mountain shelters (sælehus) on Dovrefjell for travellers, and some of those sites, such as Kongsvoll and Hjerkinn, are still lodges today. At Sygard Grytting in Gudbrandsdalen you can sleep in a building that has housed pilgrims since the 14th century. After the Reformation the road was forgotten as a pilgrimage; it was re-marked and opened in 1997.

Notable stops

  • Oslo: St Hallvard's Cathedral ruins in the Middelalderparken, where Oslo's medieval cathedral stood over the tomb of Oslo's own patron saint.
  • Bønsnes and Granavollen (west branch): the district where Olav grew up, and the Sister Churches of Granavollen, two medieval stone churches side by side.
  • Hamar (east branch): the only inland town of medieval Norway, with its cathedral ruins under a dramatic glass shelter (1998) at Domkirkeodden.
  • Lillehammer, where the branches meet.
  • Ringebu stave church (around 1220), one of Norway's largest surviving stave churches.
  • Hundorp, Dale-Gudbrand's farm: by Snorri's saga, the chieftain Dale-Gudbrand was converted here in 1021 when Olav's men smashed the image of Thor and mice and snakes ran out. The story is saga legend; the farm is a real pilgrim centre.
  • Dovrefjell: the high, open plateau with musk oxen and wild reindeer. See Dovrefjell.
  • Buvika: south of Trondheim, where pilgrims see the fjord for the first time since Oslo.
  • Trondheim: the "zero stone" milestone outside the west front of Nidaros Cathedral.

Legend. Gudbrandsdalen is full of Olav stories: his horse's hoofprint in rock, springs that rose for him, and the curse he is said to have laid on a farm that refused him hospitality. Like the Olav springs everywhere in Norway, these are folk traditions, mostly written down centuries later.
