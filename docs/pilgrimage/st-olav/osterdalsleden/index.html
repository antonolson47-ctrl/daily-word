ØsterdalsledenTrysil or Rena to Trondheim: forests, mountain huts and wild reindeer

Photo: Forollhogna Skarvdalen Vangrøftdalen Dalsbygda Os Østerdalen Innlandet Norway (2 by Geir Hval (www.MacWhale.eu), CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Distance375 km (from Rena)
Days~21
StartsTrysil (Lutnes) or Rena
ForExperienced hikers

If Gudbrandsdalsleden is the main road, Østerdalsleden is the quiet one: 375 km from Rena (379 km from the Swedish border at Trysil) through the great forests and valleys of eastern Norway, over the mountains of Forollhogna National Park and down the river Nid into Trondheim. The official route page calls it the way to "find silence and solitude." It is wilder, with cabins, shelters and tents, and is meant for experienced hikers.

Good to know

  • Two official starts: Lutnes in Trysil on the Swedish border (the milestone says 379 km to Trondheim) or Nesvangen at Rena in Åmot (375 km). They meet at the medieval pilgrim stone at Åkrestrømmen in Rendalen.
  • Forollhogna (about 1,000 m) can hold snow until late June; don't cross it while there's snow. Take the train from Tynset or Tolga to Singsås instead.
  • Wild reindeer calve here in May and early June: stay on marked paths, keep dogs on a leash, and don't fly drones.
  • You need map-and-GPS skills, a sleeping bag, and food planning: some stretches are days between shops, and mobile coverage is patchy. The Rørosbanen railway runs near the route from Rena to Singsås.
  • The season can stretch into September.

Deep Dive

History

History. In the late Middle Ages Østerdalen carried heavy pilgrim traffic between Olav's shrine in Nidaros and Vadstena in Sweden, home of St Birgitta (Bridget) of Sweden. Pilgrims came over the border through Trysil and up from Värmland along the river Glomma, the two strands that make up today's route. In Trysil the trail follows the Munkeveien (Monks' Road) past Munkbetsetra, a resting place for pilgrims, who were often called "monks."

Notable stops

  • Lia Gård, a retreat centre with an active pilgrim house, in the forest of Stor-Elvdal.
  • The pilgrim stone at Åkrestrømmen, from the Middle Ages, where the Trysil and Rena strands join.
  • Fonnåsfjellet, a climb with a wide view over Rendalen.
  • Tylldalen, a valley with its own Olav stories, and the old church at Tynset.
  • Forollhogna National Park, with traces of ancient travel routes and one of Norway's 24 wild reindeer areas.
  • Øyvindstjønna on Vassfjellet, where you first see the spire of Nidaros Cathedral; then a peaceful walk along the Nid into Trondheim.
  • Røros, the UNESCO-listed copper-mining town, is a worthwhile side trip near Tolga.
