Pilgrimage
Marian ShrinesFátima, Lourdes, Guadalupe and Częstochowa

Photo: Torchlight Procession Rosary Square Lourdes by Laurence Goff, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

"For behold, from now on all generations will call me blessed" (Luke 1:48, ESV). From the first centuries Christians have honoured Mary, the Mother of the Lord, and gone on pilgrimage to her shrines, not to put her in the place of Christ, but because she always points to him: "Do whatever he tells you" (John 2:5, ESV). Four shrines stand out for the crowds they draw and the place they hold in the life of the Church: Guadalupe in Mexico, Częstochowa in Poland, Lourdes in France and Fátima in Portugal.

Good to know

  • Apparitions and the Church: the Church never obliges Catholics to believe in a private revelation, even an approved one. It judges whether an apparition is "worthy of belief" and whether the devotion is sound; Lourdes, Fátima and Guadalupe have been approved by the local bishops and honoured by many popes. Częstochowa is an icon shrine, not an apparition.
  • Great days: 12 December (Guadalupe), 11 February (Lourdes, first apparition; World Day of the Sick), 13 May and 13 October (Fátima), 26 August and 15 August (Częstochowa, with the great walking pilgrimages).
  • What to expect: Mass in many languages, confession, the Rosary, candle processions, crowds on feast days and quiet in the off-season. Book rooms early for the big days.
  • Accessibility: Lourdes in particular is organized around the sick and disabled; all four shrines have assistance for pilgrims with reduced mobility.

Deep Dive

Four kinds of shrine

History. Częstochowa is the oldest of the four as a shrine: the Pauline Fathers have kept the icon of the Black Madonna at Jasna Góra since 1382. Guadalupe goes back to 1531, ten years after the fall of Tenochtitlan, and its image is on the cloak (tilma) of St Juan Diego. Lourdes (1858) and Fátima (1917) belong to the great modern apparitions, both to poor children: Bernadette Soubirous, and Lúcia, Francisco and Jacinta.

What the messages have in common

The message of each is simple and evangelical: prayer (above all the Rosary), penance and conversion, care for the poor and the sick, and trust in God's mercy. At Lourdes Mary called herself the Immaculate Conception; at Fátima she asked for prayer for peace and for sinners; at Guadalupe she spoke in Nahuatl to an Indigenous man and asked for a church where she could show her love to all who came.

Popular traditions. Each shrine has its own devotions: the water of Lourdes, the candle processions of Lourdes and Fátima, pilgrims crossing Fátima's esplanade on their knees, the dances of the concheros at Guadalupe, and the great walking pilgrimages to Częstochowa, some, like Warsaw's, kept every year since 1711.
