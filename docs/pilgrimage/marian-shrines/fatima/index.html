FátimaOur Lady of the Rosary and the three shepherd children, 1917

Photo: Fátima, Sanctuary of Our Lady of Fátima (6172325245) by Arian Zwegers from Brussels, Belgium, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

WhereCentral Portugal, 125 km north of Lisbon
Apparitions13 May–13 October 1917
SeersLúcia, Francisco, Jacinta
Great days12–13 May, 12–13 October

"Pray the Rosary every day, in order to obtain peace for the world." In 1917, while the First World War raged, Our Lady appeared six times to three shepherd children in the Cova da Iria, a hollow of pasture near the village of Fátima. Today the Shrine of Our Lady of the Rosary of Fátima is one of the largest Marian sanctuaries in the world, and two of the children, Francisco and Jacinta Marto, are saints, the youngest non-martyrs ever canonized.

Good to know

  • The heart of the shrine is the small Chapel of the Apparitions (Capelinha), built on the spot of the apparitions; the Rosary is prayed there several times a day in different languages, broadcast online.
  • Basilicas: the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary (1953), with the tombs of the three seers, and the large round Basilica of the Most Holy Trinity (2007).
  • Candlelight procession every evening from spring to autumn after the evening Rosary, and the farewell procession on the 13th of the great months.
  • Pilgrimage anniversaries: 12–13 May and 12–13 October, with hundreds of thousands present, and on the 13th of each month from June to September.
  • Aljustrel and Valinhos: walk 2 km to the seers' family houses at Aljustrel and the Way of the Cross through the olive groves to Valinhos, where the Angel and Our Lady appeared.
  • Getting there: buses from Lisbon take about 1½ hours; Fátima has no railway station in town. Many Portuguese walk to Fátima, and marked Caminhos de Fátima link it to Lisbon and to the Camino de Santiago.

Deep Dive

The apparitions

History. In 1916 the children, Lúcia dos Santos (10), and her cousins Francisco (9) and Jacinta Marto (7), said an angel, the "Angel of Peace", appeared to them three times. On 13 May 1917 they saw "a Lady brighter than the sun" above a small holm oak. She came back on the 13th of each month until October (in August the children were held by the local administrator, and she appeared on the 19th at Valinhos). She asked for prayer of the Rosary, penance and reparation, and on 13 October said she was Our Lady of the Rosary. Francisco died in 1919 and Jacinta in 1920, victims of the Spanish flu. Lúcia became a Carmelite nun and died in 2005. The Bishop of Leiria declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1930.

The miracle of the sun

What was reported. On 13 October 1917 a crowd of perhaps 50,000–70,000 people, including sceptical journalists, gathered in the rain. Many reported that the sun "danced", spun and seemed to plunge toward the earth, and that their wet clothes were suddenly dry. The secular Lisbon paper O Século reported it. Not everyone present saw it, and no observatory recorded anything; the Church holds it as a sign but does not make belief in it a matter of faith.

The "secret"

History. On 13 July 1917 the children received a three-part "secret": a vision of hell, a call for the consecration of Russia to the Immaculate Heart of Mary and the devotion of the First Saturdays, and a vision of a "bishop dressed in white" falling under gunfire among martyrs. Lúcia wrote down the third part in 1944; it was published by the Vatican in 2000, with a commentary by Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger. St John Paul II, shot in St Peter's Square on 13 May 1981, believed that Our Lady of Fátima had saved his life, and had the bullet set in her crown.

"Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God" (Matthew 5:8, ESV). Pope Francis canonized Francisco and Jacinta at Fátima on 13 May 2017, the centenary of the first apparition. The cause of Lúcia is open.
