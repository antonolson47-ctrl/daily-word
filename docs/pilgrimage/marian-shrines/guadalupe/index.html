WhereTepeyac, northern Mexico City
Apparitions9–12 December 1531
Feast12 December
Opendaily 6:00–21:00

"¿No estoy yo aquí, que soy tu madre?", "Am I not here, I who am your mother?" These words of Our Lady to St Juan Diego, an Indigenous convert, at the hill of Tepeyac in December 1531, are at the heart of the most visited Marian shrine in the world. Around 12 December alone, Mexico City counted some 13 million visitors in 2025. Pilgrims come to see the image of the Virgin on Juan Diego's cloak, the tilma, kept above the high altar of the Basilica of Our Lady of Guadalupe.

Good to know

  • Hours: the Basilica is open daily 6:00–21:00, with Masses at the high altar almost every hour from 6:00 to 20:00 (official timetable).
  • The tilma hangs behind and above the altar; to see it close up, take the moving walkways that pass under it behind the sanctuary.
  • The precinct (Recinto Guadalupano): the Old Basilica (1709, now the Expiatory Temple of Christ the King), the Capuchin church, the Chapel of the Well (del Pocito), and the steps and gardens up Tepeyac to the Capilla del Cerrito, built where the roses were gathered.
  • 12 December: the feast begins at midnight with the Mañanitas, songs to the Virgin, followed by Masses through the day; parishes, guilds and dance groups walk in from all over Mexico for weeks before. Expect very large crowds and road closures.
  • Getting there: Metro La Villa-Basílica (line 6) or Metrobús line 7 from the city centre.

Deep Dive

The apparitions

History. According to the Nican Mopohua, a Nahuatl account attributed to the scholar Antonio Valeriano and published in 1649, Our Lady appeared four times to Juan Diego Cuauhtlatoatzin, a widower of about 57, between 9 and 12 December 1531, and once to his dying uncle Juan Bernardino, who was healed. She asked for a church on Tepeyac. When Bishop Juan de Zumárraga asked for a sign, she sent Juan Diego to gather Castilian roses on the frozen hilltop in December. When he opened his tilma before the bishop, the roses fell and the image of the Virgin was seen on the cloth.

Debates and traditions. Some historians note that early Spanish sources say little about the apparitions, and the cause of Juan Diego met objections; others point to Indigenous annals and the very early chapel on Tepeyac (by the 1550s). The Church, after study, beatified Juan Diego in 1990 and canonized him in 2002. Many traditions surround the image: that the cloth of agave fibre has lasted almost 500 years, that it survived a bomb hidden in flowers in 1921, and that the stars on her mantle match the sky of December 1531.

A Mother for the Americas

The image speaks the language of the Aztec world: a young woman with dark skin (la Morenita), standing before the sun and on the moon, wearing the black sash of a pregnant woman, her hands joined in prayer. "A great sign appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet" (Revelation 12:1, ESV). In the following decades millions of the Indigenous peoples of Mexico were baptized. Pius XII called her Empress of the Americas in 1945, and in 1999 St John Paul II declared her Patroness of all America; he canonized Juan Diego in the basilica on 31 July 2002.

Stop by stop

The New Basilica

Built in 1974–76, the round basilica seats 10,000. The tilma of St Juan Diego hangs above the altar; moving walkways below it keep the crowds flowing.

The Old Basilica

Completed in 1709, it sank unevenly into the soft lake bed and leans visibly; it has been restored and reopened as the Templo Expiatorio a Cristo Rey.

Capilla del Cerrito

Stairs climb Tepeyac hill to the chapel where tradition places the first apparition and where Juan Diego gathered roses in December.

Tradition. The roses, Castilian and out of season, filled his tilma; when he opened it before Bishop Zumárraga the image appeared. The tilma's survival is fact; its origin is a matter of faith and continuing study.

12 December

On the feast, millions of pilgrims, many on foot or on their knees, arrive overnight; it is among the largest pilgrimages in the world.
