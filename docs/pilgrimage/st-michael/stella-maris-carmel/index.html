History. Mount Carmel is where the prophet Elijah challenged the prophets of Baal (1 Kings 18). In the 12th century hermits imitating Elijah settled on the mountain and became the Carmelite Order. Stella Maris ("Star of the Sea") is the mother house of the Discalced Carmelites. The Carmelites returned here in 1631, and the present church opened in 1836 and became a minor basilica in 1839. A grotto under the high altar is honoured as a cave of Elijah (Wikipedia; Carmelite leaflet).

Why it's on the "line." Stella Maris is dedicated to Our Lady and Elijah, not to St Michael. It is included as the eastern end of the modern Sacred Line story, not because of any ancient Michael shrine here.

Stella Maris in depth

Founding stories: legend and history

History or legend? Elijah is a biblical figure. His stories in 1 Kings 17–19 and 2 Kings 1–2 are scripture, not datable records, and the tradition that he lived in the cave under the altar is a pious tradition. The Carmelite Order's beginnings with hermits on Mount Carmel around 1200 are documented. So are their expulsion in 1291, their return in 1631, the massacre of 1799 and the rebuilding in 1827–36. Some older Carmelite claims, that the order goes back without a break to Elijah himself or that Mary visited the hermits, are legend. The order no longer teaches them as history.

Elijah on Carmel. The Bible tells how the prophet Elijah challenged the 450 prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel to call down fire on a sacrifice (1 Kings 18). Baal sent nothing. Elijah prayed, and fire fell from heaven and burned the offering, the wood, the stones and the water in the trench. After this, during a long drought, Elijah sent his servant to look out to sea seven times. On the seventh, he saw "a little cloud like a man's hand" rising from the sea, and the rain came. Carmelites later saw the little cloud as a sign of Mary, and that is one root of the title Stella Maris ("Star of the Sea").

Where it happened. The traditional place of the contest is El-Muhraqa ("the place of burning"), on the south-east ridge of Carmel, about 25 km from Haifa, where the Carmelites have a small monastery. Stella Maris, at the north-west end of the ridge above the sea, is linked instead with Elijah's dwelling and with the "school of the prophets". A cave under the high altar is honoured as a place where Elijah lived. A larger cave at the foot of the mountain, also called Elijah's Cave, is a separate holy site, honoured by Jews, Christians, Muslims and Druze.

The Carmelites. Around 1200, after the Crusades, Latin hermits settled near the spring of Elijah in the Wadi es-Siah, a valley on Carmel. Between 1206 and 1214 Albert, Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem, gave them a short Rule of life addressed to "Brother B." and the hermits. It is still the base of Carmelite life today (O.Carm.). The ruins of their first monastery can still be visited in the wadi. When the crusader kingdom fell in 1291, the hermits were killed or fled to Europe, where the order spread widely.

The return to Stella Maris. In 1631 the Discalced Carmelite Fr Prosper of the Holy Spirit obtained permission to settle on the headland, near the cave. The community suffered many times: - In 1799 Napoleon's army used the monastery as a hospital during the siege of Acre. When the French withdrew, the wounded soldiers left behind were killed. A pyramid in front of the church is their memorial, inscribed "How are the mighty fallen in the midst of the battle!" (Carmel Holy Land). - In 1821 the Ottoman governor Abdullah Pasha destroyed the monastery. - The Carmelite brother Giovanni Battista of Frascati raised money across Europe and rebuilt it in 1827–36. That is the present church and monastery. It is the mother house of the Discalced Carmelites.

Architecture and special features

  • The church. It has a Latin cross plan with a central dome, and inside it is lined with gleaming white marble panels. They are so bright and well placed that visitors sometimes think the walls are painted.
  • The dome. It was painted by the Maltese Carmelite Luigi Poggi in 1924–28. It shows scenes from the Bible. The most striking is Elijah carried up to heaven in a chariot of fire.
  • Four reliefs on the pillars show four Carmelite saints: Teresa of Avila, John of the Cross, Edith Stein (Teresa Benedicta of the Cross), and Mariam Baouardy (Mary of Jesus Crucified, from Galilee). Edith Stein was a philosopher who died at Auschwitz. Mariam Baouardy was an Arab Melkite girl from Galilee who became a Carmelite and founded the Carmel in Bethlehem.
  • The lighthouse. Opposite the church stands the old lighthouse. It was once part of the monastery and is not open to visitors. Next to it is a pillar with a statue of the Immaculate Conception, a gift from Chile, whose patron is Our Lady of Mount Carmel.

Relics and sacred objects

  • The statue of Our Lady of Mount Carmel stands above the high altar, on a pedestal carved from a cedar of Lebanon. It is a much-loved image among the Christians of Haifa and the Galilee. The Virgin is shown holding the Child and the brown scapular.
  • Elijah's grotto lies under the altar. A few steps lead down into a small rock cave. Inside is an altar cut into the rock with a small statue of the prophet.
  • The brown scapular. The scapular is two small pieces of brown cloth on cords, worn over the shoulders. It is the best-known Carmelite devotion. The tradition that Mary gave it to the English Carmelite St Simon Stock in a vision in 1251 is legend, not history, but the devotion itself is very old and widespread. Scapulars are sold in the monastery shop.
  • Byzantine fragments. A small display shows remains of a Byzantine church found on the site, and there is a year-round Nativity scene.

Notable pilgrims through history

  • St Louis IX of France. Tradition says he met the Carmelite hermits during his crusade and in 1254 brought some of them back to France. They settled in Paris.
  • Napoleon's soldiers, sick and wounded during the siege of Acre in 1799.
  • Carmelite saints. Teresa of Avila and John of the Cross never came here, but the place was the spiritual "home" of their reform. Mariam Baouardy, born in Galilee in 1846, belonged to this Carmelite world in the Holy Land.
  • Pilgrims from Latin America and the Philippines, where Our Lady of Mount Carmel is a national patron. Chile's statue and the mass pilgrimages around 16 July, the feast of Our Lady of Mount Carmel, show this.

The surrounding area

The city. Haifa is Israel's third city and its main port. It is a mixed city of Jews and Arabs, Christians and Muslims, rising in terraces up Mount Carmel from the bay. Stella Maris stands at the north-west tip of the mountain, with a view over the Mediterranean, the bay and, on a clear day, Akko and the hills of Lebanon.

Nearby sights. - The Bahá'í Gardens and the Shrine of the Báb, with 19 terraces running down the slope. They are part of the UNESCO site Bahá'í Holy Places in Haifa and the Western Galilee (UNESCO). Free guided tours run on set days. - The German Colony, founded by the German Templers in 1869, with restaurants. - Wadi Nisnas, the Arab quarter, with its market. - Elijah's Cave at the foot of the mountain. - The cable car from Bat Galim beach up to Stella Maris. - El-Muhraqa on the ridge. - The Wadi es-Siah ruins. - Ein Hod, an artists' village. - The Crusader city of Akko (Acre), a UNESCO site.

Local food. Haifa's Arab bakeries and restaurants are famous: hummus and falafel, knafeh (a cheese pastry in syrup), sfiha (meat pies) and fish by the port. Wadi Nisnas and the market in the lower city are the places to eat well and cheaply.

Getting there. Fly into Ben Gurion Airport (Tel Aviv). A direct train runs to Haifa in about 1–1.5 hours. From the station, take a bus or taxi up the mountain, or the cable car from Bat Galim. Before travelling, check the latest travel advisory and security situation (see the advisory at the top of this page). Opening hours and access can change at short notice.

Best time to visit. Spring (March–May) and autumn (October–November) are mild. Summers are hot and humid. The great feast is 16 July (Our Lady of Mount Carmel). Haifa's Christian community marks it with processions and crowded Masses.

Visiting

  • Church hours: daily 6:00–12:00 and 15:00–18:00 (per the Carmelite custody site; call +972 4 833 7758 to confirm).
  • Don't confuse it with the separate, larger Elijah's Cave on Allenby Road below the mountain.
