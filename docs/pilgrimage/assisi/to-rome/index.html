On to RomeFrom the Rieti valley through the Sabine hills to St John Lateran and St Peter's

Photo: Rome, Italy, Sunlight and basilica of St. John Lateran by Vyacheslav Argenberg, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Rieti – Romeabout 100 km
Days5 to 6
Rule approved1209/10, Innocent III
Testimonium in RomePercorso Oranti, St Peter's

Francis came to Rome in 1209 or 1210 with eleven brothers to ask the Pope's approval for their way of life. The last stretch of the Via di Francesco retraces that journey through the Sabine hills and the Tiber valley to the Lateran, the Pope's cathedral, and on to St Peter's.

Good to know

  • From the Rieti valley the way runs by Poggio San Lorenzo, Ponticelli, Monterotondo and Monte Sacro into the city, in about 5 to 6 days.
  • In Rome, St John Lateran recalls the approval of the Rule. San Francesco a Ripa in Trastevere keeps the cell where Francis stayed.
  • Pilgrims arriving at St Peter's can also receive the Testimonium given at the end of the Via Francigena, through the Percorso Oranti; see our Rome section.

Deep Dive

The approval of the Rule

History. Francis and his first companions brought a short rule made of Gospel sayings to Innocent III. With the help of Bishop Guido of Assisi and Cardinal John of St Paul, the Pope gave verbal approval and permission to preach penance. The final Rule was confirmed by Honorius III with the bull Solet annuere on 29 November 1223.

Legend. St Bonaventure tells that the Pope had dreamed of the Lateran basilica tottering and a small, poor man holding it up on his shoulder, and recognised that man in Francis. The scene is painted by Giotto in the Upper Basilica.
