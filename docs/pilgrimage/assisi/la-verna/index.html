La VernaThe mountain of the stigmata, where the way begins

Photo: Chiusi della verna, santuario francescano, chiesa maggiore, esterno 01 by Sailko, CC BY 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Height1,128 m
Given to Francis1213
StigmataSeptember 1224
Feast of the Stigmata17 September

The sanctuary of La Verna stands on a crag of beech and fir above the Casentino valley. Here, in September 1224, two years before his death, Francis received the wounds of Christ. For most pilgrims it is the starting point of the Via di Francesco; others walk here first from Florence in about five days.

Good to know

  • The Franciscan friars run the sanctuary and a guesthouse; pilgrims can sleep there.
  • The friars process every afternoon from the basilica to the Chapel of the Stigmata, a custom kept without a break for centuries.
  • See the Chiesa Maggiore with the great glazed terracottas of Andrea della Robbia, the Chapel of the Stigmata, the Sasso Spicco (the overhanging rock where Francis prayed) and the bed of stone in the cave.
  • From La Verna, the first stage drops to Pieve Santo Stefano and the second to Sansepolcro.

Deep Dive

The mountain

History. Count Orlando of Chiusi gave the mountain to Francis in 1213 as a place of solitude. Francis came there many times to keep the "Lent of St Michael" (15 August – 29 September). In 1224, around the feast of the Holy Cross (14 September), while he was praying, Brother Leo and the early biographers record that he saw a crucified seraph, and afterwards bore wounds in his hands, feet and side. Thomas of Celano and St Bonaventure describe them; Leo kept the parchment on which Francis wrote him a blessing, now in Assisi. The Feast of the Stigmata is kept on 17 September.

Legends. The Fioretti tell that the rocks of La Verna were split at the moment of Christ's death, and that a falcon woke Francis for prayer each night. They are pious stories rather than history.
