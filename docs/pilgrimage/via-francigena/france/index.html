Pilgrimage › Via Francigena
Through FranceArras, Reims, Langres and the Jura, about 1,000 km

Distanceabout 1,000 km
Timeabout 6–7 weeks
HighlightsArras, Laon, Reims, Langres
Leaves Francenear Pontarlier

The French section is the longest and the quietest part of the Via Francigena: about a thousand kilometres of wheat fields, Champagne vineyards, forests and limestone plateaux, with great Gothic cathedrals at long intervals. Few pilgrims walk it, which makes it a place of real solitude.

Good to know

  • Route: Calais – Wissant – Guînes – Thérouanne – Arras – Bapaume – Péronne – Laon – Reims – Châlons-en-Champagne – Bar-sur-Aube – Langres – Champlitte – Besançon – Ornans – Pontarlier – the Swiss border at Les Fourgs/Jougne.
  • Accommodation is the hard part: pilgrim hostels are rare, so book gîtes, chambres d'hôtes and hotels ahead. Local Via Francigena associations and parish offices sometimes help.
  • Signs: waymarks are good in most regions; some sections also follow French GR trails (GR 145 for much of the route), marked red and white.
  • Sundays: shops in small villages close; carry food.

Deep Dive

Reims, cathedral of kings

History. Around 496 (the date is debated) St Remigius (Remi), bishop of Reims, baptized Clovis, king of the Franks, and the Franks turned to Catholic Christianity. "Bow your head, proud Sicambrian; adore what you have burned, burn what you have adored", Gregory of Tours reports him saying. For centuries the kings of France were crowned in Reims, and St Joan of Arc stood beside Charles VII at his coronation here in 1429. The present cathedral of Notre-Dame was begun in 1211. It was badly burned by German shelling in 1914 and restored over decades; the World Heritage site includes the Basilica of Saint-Remi, which holds the saint's tomb.

Legend. The Holy Ampulla (Sainte Ampoule), the vial of oil used to anoint French kings, was said to have been brought by a dove from heaven for the baptism of Clovis. It was smashed during the French Revolution in 1793; a fragment is said to have been saved and is kept at Saint-Remi.

Laon and Langres

Laon stands on a ridge above the plain, its cathedral of Notre-Dame (12th century) one of the first great Gothic churches, famous for the stone oxen on its towers, remembering the beasts that hauled the stone up the hill. Langres, a hilltop town ringed by ramparts, has the cathedral of Saint-Mammès and marks the watershed between the North Sea, the Atlantic and the Mediterranean.

Into the Jura

After Besançon, with its cathedral of Saint-Jean and Vauban's citadel, the way climbs the valley of the Loue to Pontarlier and the Jura heights, and crosses into Switzerland.
