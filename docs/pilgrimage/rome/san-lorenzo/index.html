San Lorenzo fuori le muraStation 6 · St Lawrence Outside the Walls, the deacon martyr's tomb

EntryFree

St Lawrence was one of the seven deacons of Rome, keeper of the Church's goods and its care for the poor, martyred in August 258 under Valerian. His tomb on the Via Tiburtina became one of the most visited shrines in Rome. The basilica over it is really two churches joined end to end, and in 1943 it became the only papal basilica struck in World War II.

Good to know

  • Entry is free; the Romanesque cloister is opened by the Capuchin friars at set times.
  • Getting there: tram 2, 3, 8 or 19, or bus 71 and 492, to Verano. On the walk it is 2 km from Santa Croce and 2.5 km to Santa Maria Maggiore.
  • Feast: 10 August. Romans say the August shooting stars (the Perseids) are "Lawrence's tears."
  • Next door is the Verano, Rome's monumental 19th-century cemetery, worth a slow walk.

Deep Dive

History: two basilicas made one

History. Constantine built a large funerary basilica near the catacomb of Cyriaca, where Lawrence was buried. Pope Pelagius II (579–590) built a smaller church right over the tomb, cut into the hillside; its triumphal arch mosaic shows Pelagius offering the church to Christ. In the early 13th century Honorius III (1216–1227) built a new nave on the west and joined it to Pelagius's church, which became the raised chancel. In 1855–64 Pius IX restored it. On 19 July 1943 Allied bombs aimed at the nearby San Lorenzo rail yards hit the basilica, destroying the façade and much of the nave; Pius XII came at once to stand among the dead in the neighbourhood. It was carefully rebuilt with the original stones and reopened in 1949.

Tomb and relics

  • The confessio under the high altar holds the tombs of St Lawrence, St Stephen, the first martyr (see our St Stephen page), and St Justin. Stephen's relics were brought from Constantinople, by tradition, under Pelagius II.
  • The stone of St Lawrence: in the crypt behind the confessio is a marble slab shown as the one on which the martyr's body was laid, with dark stains venerated as marks of his burns.
  • The gridiron: the most famous relic of Lawrence's martyrdom, a part of the gridiron, is not here but in the church of San Lorenzo in Lucina in the city centre.
  • The tomb of Blessed Pius IX in a mosaic-covered chapel below the chancel, and that of Alcide De Gasperi, a founder of postwar Italy and of the European project, whose cause for beatification is open.

Legend. In the account repeated by St Ambrose, the prefect demanded the Church's treasures; Lawrence brought him the poor and the sick and said, "These are the treasures of the Church." Sentenced to be roasted on a gridiron, he reportedly said, "Turn me over, I'm done on this side." Many historians think he was beheaded, like Pope Sixtus II four days earlier, and that the gridiron came from a scribe's slip (passus est, "he suffered," read as assus est, "he was roasted"). The gridiron story is tradition; that he died for Christ in 258 is history.

Art and architecture

  • The Cosmatesque pavement, ambos and the papal throne (1254) of the chancel, among the finest in Rome.
  • The portico with 13th-century frescoes of the lives of Lawrence and Stephen, and two ancient sarcophagi.
  • The Romanesque cloister (12th century), with fragments of inscriptions from the catacomb.

Next on the walk

West along Via Tiburtina and past Termini station, it's 2.5 km to the last station, Santa Maria Maggiore.
