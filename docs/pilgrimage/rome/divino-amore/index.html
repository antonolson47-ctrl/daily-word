Madonna del Divino AmoreJubilee 2000 alternative to San Sebastiano · Our Lady of Divine Love, Castel di Leva

Out in the Roman Campagna, about 12 km south of the centre, is the shrine Romans have called their own since the 18th century: Our Lady of Divine Love. For the Great Jubilee of 2000, it took San Sebastiano's place among the seven Jubilee churches, a sign of how much this sanctuary means to modern Rome.

Good to know

  • Hours (official site): the old sanctuary is open every day 6:45–21:00; the new sanctuary on weekdays 8:00–13:00 and all day on Saturday evening, Sundays and holidays.
  • Getting there (official directions): Metro B to Laurentina then bus 702, or Metro A to San Giovanni then bus 218, to the Santuario Divino Amore stop.
  • The night pilgrimage: from Easter to October, on Saturday nights, Romans walk about 14 km from Piazza di Porta Capena to the shrine, praying the rosary, arriving for a dawn Mass. It's one of the great living pilgrim walks of Rome.

Deep Dive

History and the miracle of 1740

The founding story. In the spring of 1740 a pilgrim on his way to Rome lost his way near the castle of Leva and was attacked by a pack of sheepdogs. Seeing an image of Mary painted on the castle tower, he cried out to her for help, and the dogs fell back. The story spread, and pilgrims began to come. The image was moved to a new church, consecrated in 1750.

The fresco on the tower, a 13th- or 14th-century painting of Mary enthroned with the Child, with the Holy Spirit as a dove above, gave the shrine its name: Divine Love is the Holy Spirit.

Don Umberto Terenzi and the vow of 1944

In 1930 Don Umberto Terenzi (1900–1974) came as rector and transformed the shrine with a new parish, works of charity, and the Daughters and Oblates of Our Lady of Divine Love. In January 1944, with the Germans fighting in the hills around, the image was brought for safety into Rome, to churches including Sant'Ignazio. As the Allied front approached, Romans made a vow on 4 June 1944: if Rome was spared, they would change their lives, renew the shrine and build a new church and a work of charity. That same evening the Germans withdrew without a battle, and Rome was liberated. Pius XII, who came to venerate the image, called Mary the Salvatrice dell'Urbe, "Saviour of the City."

History. The vow took more than fifty years to fulfil. The new sanctuary, a large modern church partly sunk into the hillside, was consecrated by St John Paul II on 4 July 1999, just in time for the Jubilee. In 2000 the shrine became one of the seven Jubilee churches of Rome. John Paul II, Benedict XVI and Francis all visited.

What to see

  • The old sanctuary (1745–50), with the miraculous image above the altar.
  • The tower of Castel di Leva, where the image was painted.
  • The new sanctuary (1999), its glass walls looking out over the countryside, and the tomb of Don Umberto Terenzi, whose cause for beatification is open.
