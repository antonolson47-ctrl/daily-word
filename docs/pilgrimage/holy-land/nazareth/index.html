NazarethThe Basilica of the Annunciation and the hidden life of the Holy Family

"Behold, I am the servant of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word" (Luke 1:38, ESV). In this hill town of Galilee the angel Gabriel came to Mary, and Jesus grew up "in wisdom and in stature" as the son of the carpenter. Today Nazareth is the largest Arab city in Israel and a home of Christians of many rites; above the old town rises the dome of the Basilica of the Annunciation.

Good to know

  • Hours (CIC, September 2026): Upper Basilica, Grotto and St Joseph's Church 8:00–18:00 (last entry 15 minutes before); silent prayer daily 18:00–21:00; Rosary on Tuesday and Adoration on Thursday at 20:30; candlelight procession every Saturday at 20:30. Closed Good Friday.

Deep Dive

The Basilica and the grotto

History. Excavations by the Franciscan archaeologist Bellarmino Bagatti in the 1950s found a small 1st-century village of cave-houses, Jewish-Christian graffiti (including XE MAPIA, "Hail Mary") and the remains of a pre-Byzantine place of worship, a Byzantine church and a Crusader cathedral. The present basilica by Giovanni Muzio was consecrated in 1969. It is built on two levels: the lower church frames the grotto, the upper church is the parish church. Its walls and courtyard are covered with images of Our Lady sent from Catholic countries around the world.

Next door, the Church of St Joseph (1914) stands over caves venerated as the workshop or home of Joseph.

Legends. The Greek Orthodox tradition, from the apocryphal Protoevangelium of James, says Gabriel first greeted Mary as she drew water at the spring; the Church of St Gabriel is built over it. A famous Western legend says that the house of Mary was carried by angels from Nazareth in 1291 and set down at Loreto in Italy; historians think its stones came by ship, possibly with the help of a family called Angeli.
