The Jesus Trail65 km on foot from Nazareth to Capernaum

Photo: Walking the Jesus Trail by Zeromancer44, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Length65 km
Time4 days
StartNazareth
EndCapernaum

"Leaving Nazareth he went and lived in Capernaum by the sea" (Matthew 4:13, ESV). The Jesus Trail is a waymarked 65 km footpath that follows the move of Jesus from his home town to the lake shore where his ministry began. It is the best way to feel the scale of Galilee: the distances Jesus walked, the hills, fields and springs of the parables.

Good to know

  • Stages (4 days): Nazareth – Cana (via Sepphoris), Cana – Kibbutz Lavi, Lavi – Moshav Arbel (via the Horns of Hattin and Nebi Shu'eib), Arbel – Capernaum (down the cliffs of Mount Arbel, through Magdala, Tabgha and the Mount of Beatitudes).
  • Waymarks: orange-and-white blazes (some sections share the Israel National Trail).
  • Season: March–May and October–November. Summer is very hot; carry 3 litres of water. Winter rain makes the Arbel descent slippery; it has fixed handholds and is steep.
  • The first day is mostly through the urban edge of Nazareth; many start from Sepphoris or Cana.
  • Accommodation: guesthouses in Nazareth's old city, B&Bs in Cana and Arbel, the kibbutz hotel at Lavi. The trail's official site lists them and sells guided and self-guided packages.

Deep Dive

Sepphoris

History. Sepphoris (Zippori), 6 km from Nazareth, was rebuilt as Herod Antipas' capital in Jesus' boyhood. Some scholars think Joseph and Jesus, as builders (tektōn), may have worked there. Later it was a centre of Jewish learning where the Mishnah was compiled. Its mosaics, including the "Mona Lisa of Galilee", are in a national park. Christian tradition places the home of Joachim and Anne, the parents of Mary, here, at the Crusader church of St Anne.

Cana

At Kafr Kanna, the Franciscan Wedding Church marks the first sign of Jesus, water made wine (John 2:1–11). Married couples come to renew their vows. Some scholars prefer Khirbet Qana, a ruin a few miles north, as the Gospel village.

Hattin and Arbel

The twin hilltop of the Horns of Hattin saw the defeat of the Crusader army by Saladin in 1187, which lost Jerusalem for the Latins. Below, Druze tradition honours the tomb of Nebi Shu'eib (Jethro). From the clifftop of Mount Arbel the whole lake opens out; the path drops past cliff caves to the Valley of the Doves (Wadi Hamam), and on to Magdala.

Tradition. A medieval tradition put the Sermon on the Mount on the Horns of Hattin (the "Mount of the Beatitudes" of Crusader pilgrims), before it moved to the hill above Tabgha. The Gospel of Matthew simply says "the mountain".
