Pilgrimage
The Holy LandJerusalem, Bethlehem, Nazareth and Galilee, the land of the Gospels

Photo: 2013-Aerial-Mount of Olives by Godot13, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Every Christian pilgrimage points back here. In Bethlehem the Word became flesh; in Nazareth he grew up; by the Sea of Galilee he called his disciples, taught and healed; in Jerusalem he died and rose again. "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14, ESV). Since at least the 4th century, when St Helena came and Egeria wrote her diary of the liturgy in Jerusalem, Christians have travelled to see and pray in the places of the Gospel. They call the land the Fifth Gospel.

Check the situation before you book. As of 16 October 2026 the UK Foreign Office advises against all travel to Gaza and to parts of the north and of the West Bank, and against all but essential travel to the rest of the West Bank except East Jerusalem, the Bethlehem governorate, Ramallah and Jericho; the US State Department rates Israel and the West Bank Level 3, "Reconsider travel". Group pilgrimages are running, and the Latin Patriarch and the Custos of the Holy Land have asked pilgrims to return, since local Christians depend on them. Read your government's advice the week you travel, go with a recognised pilgrimage organiser or local Christian guide, and expect last-minute changes, including at Holy Week.

Good to know

  • Who cares for the shrines: since 1342 the Franciscans of the Custody of the Holy Land (Custodia Terrae Sanctae) have kept the Catholic holy places. The Holy Sepulchre and the Nativity are shared by several Churches (Greek Orthodox, Catholic, Armenian and others) under the Status Quo of 1852.
  • Entry: most visitors need an electronic travel authorization (ETA-IL) before flying to Israel; Bethlehem and Jericho are reached through Israeli checkpoints (Checkpoint 300 for Bethlehem). Carry your passport.
  • Time: a classic pilgrimage is 8–10 days: 3–4 in Jerusalem and Bethlehem, 3–4 in Galilee, with Nazareth, Mount Tabor and the Jordan in between.
  • Mass at the holy places: priests leading groups book Masses through the Franciscan Pilgrims' Office (FPO) in Jerusalem.
  • Opening hours change often: the Christian Information Centre at Jaffa Gate publishes the current hours of every shrine (last updated 15 September 2026).
  • Dress: shoulders and knees covered in churches; modest dress also in Orthodox Jewish and Muslim areas. Many shrines close at midday and on some feasts.

Deep Dive

The first pilgrims

History. After Constantine legalized Christianity, his mother Helena visited the Holy Land around 326–28, and imperial basilicas rose over the cave of the Nativity in Bethlehem, the tomb of Christ in Jerusalem and the Mount of Olives. Eusebius of Caesarea describes the work. The Bordeaux Pilgrim (333) left the first itinerary, and the nun Egeria (about 381–84) described the Holy Week liturgy in Jerusalem, the origin of the stations and processions still kept today. St Jerome settled in Bethlehem to translate the Bible.

Legend. Tradition says St Helena found the True Cross in a cistern near Calvary, identifying it by a miracle of healing. The story is told from the end of the 4th century by St Ambrose and others; the chapel of the Finding of the Cross is still shown beneath the Holy Sepulchre.

Crusades, Franciscans and the Status Quo

History. The Persians burned the churches in 614, and in 1009 the caliph al-Hakim destroyed the Holy Sepulchre. The Crusaders held Jerusalem from 1099 to 1187 and rebuilt much of what pilgrims see today. St Francis himself came to the East in 1219; in 1342 Pope Clement VI entrusted the holy places to his friars. Rivalry among the Churches over the shrines was frozen by the Ottoman Status Quo decree of 1852, still in force.

Walking with the living Church

The Holy Land is not a museum. There are Arab Christian parishes in Bethlehem, Beit Sahour, Nazareth and Jerusalem, Hebrew-speaking Catholic communities, and monasteries of every rite. Pilgrims are asked to meet them, buy from Christian craftsmen and pray for the peace of Jerusalem: "Pray for the peace of Jerusalem! May they be secure who love you!" (Psalm 122:6, ESV).
