The Via DolorosaThe Way of the Cross through the Old City of Jerusalem

Photo: Jerusalem Via dolorosa BW 2010-09-20 09-56-43 by Berthold Werner, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Lengthabout 600 m
Stations14
StartsUmariya school, near Lions' Gate
Franciscan Via CrucisFridays, 15:00 winter / 16:00 summer

"And he went out, bearing his own cross, to the place called The Place of a Skull, which in Aramaic is called Golgotha" (John 19:17, ESV). The Via Dolorosa, "the Way of Sorrow", runs about 600 metres through the Muslim and Christian Quarters from the site of the Antonia fortress to Calvary, through busy market streets. Nine stations are marked in the streets; the last five are inside the Holy Sepulchre.

Good to know

  • The Franciscan Way of the Cross leaves every Friday from the courtyard of the Umariya school, opposite the Flagellation monastery, at 15:00 in winter / 16:00 in summer (CIC, September 2026). Anyone may join; it ends at the Holy Sepulchre.
  • Walking alone: carry a booklet with the stations, and expect shops, crowds, steps and narrow lanes. Mornings are quieter.
  • On the way: the Flagellation and Condemnation chapels and the Terra Sancta Museum (7:00–18:00, winter 17:00); the Ecce Homo basilica and Lithostrotos of the Sisters of Sion (8:00–17:00); the Austrian Hospice with its roof terrace.
  • Good Friday: thousands walk the route; check the Custody's Holy Week programme each year (in 2026 some public celebrations were cancelled for security reasons).

Deep Dive

The stations

  1. Jesus is condemned to death (Umariya school, on the site of the Antonia)
  2. Jesus takes up his cross (Chapel of the Condemnation)
  3. Jesus falls the first time
  4. Jesus meets his Mother
  5. Simon of Cyrene helps Jesus carry the cross
  6. Veronica wipes the face of Jesus
  7. Jesus falls the second time (the "Gate of Judgment")
  8. Jesus speaks to the women of Jerusalem (Luke 23:28)
  9. Jesus falls the third time (by the Coptic Patriarchate) 10–14. In the Holy Sepulchre: Jesus is stripped, nailed to the cross, dies; is taken down; and is laid in the tomb

How old is the route?

History. The Gospels do not record a route. In Byzantine times processions went from Gethsemane to Calvary; the medieval routes varied. The present line was fixed by the Franciscans from the 14th century and settled in the 18th–19th, with numbered stations borrowed from the European devotion of the Stations of the Cross, itself spread by the Franciscans. Many scholars now think Pilate judged Jesus at Herod's palace near Jaffa Gate, where Roman governors usually stayed, rather than at the Antonia. The Ecce Homo arch belongs to a 2nd-century Roman triumphal arch of Hadrian, and the paving of the Lithostrotos below the Sisters of Sion convent is also Hadrianic, later than Jesus.

Legends. Veronica, whose name was later read as vera icon, "true image", is not in the Gospels; the story of her veil, which kept the imprint of Christ's face, dates from the Middle Ages. The three falls of Jesus and the meeting with his Mother are also traditional, not Gospel, stations, which is why John Paul II introduced in 1991 a "Scriptural Way of the Cross" using only Gospel scenes.

What matters is not the precise paving, but walking with Christ: the stations call each pilgrim to "take up his cross daily and follow me" (Luke 9:23).

Stop by stop

Lions' Gate

Also called St Stephen's Gate, where tradition places the first martyr's stoning. The route's Franciscan procession, every Friday at 3 pm, starts nearby.

Church of the Flagellation

Stations I–II. The Franciscan Studium Biblicum has its home here; the chapel windows show the scourging. The paving stones in the nearby Ecce Homo convent were once taken for the Lithostrotos of John 19:13.

History. Archaeologists date that paving to Hadrian's Aelia Capitolina (2nd century), after Jesus' time.

Ecce Homo Arch

The arch crossing the street was named for Pilate's words, "Behold the man" (John 19:5). It is in fact part of a 2nd-century triple gateway built by Hadrian.

Ninth Station and the Ethiopian monastery

Station IX is reached by stairs to the roof of the Holy Sepulchre, where Ethiopian monks live in tiny cells of Deir es-Sultan. From here you descend through their chapels into the church.
