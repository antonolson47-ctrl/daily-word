WhenFifty days, Easter Sunday to Pentecost. 2027: 28 March to 16 May. Easter is the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after 21 March.

History

Easter is the oldest Christian feast, the Christian Pascha, kept from the beginning as the Passover of Christ. In the 2nd century a sharp dispute (the "Quartodeciman" controversy) asked whether to keep it on 14 Nisan or on the following Sunday; St Polycarp and Pope Anicetus disagreed in peace. The Council of Nicaea (325) settled on a Sunday after the spring full moon, independent of the Jewish calendar. The Eastern Orthodox still use the Julian calendar for the calculation, so their Easter often falls later (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Easter").

Why it is observed

"If Christ be not raised, your faith is vain" (1 Corinthians 15:17, KJV). The Resurrection is the heart of the Gospel, and the Church celebrates it for fifty days as one "great Sunday" (St Athanasius). The readings come from Acts, showing the Risen Lord's life in the first church.

Colours and symbols

The Paschal candle lit from the new fire, the Alleluia restored, lilies, eggs (the sealed tomb broken open; once forbidden in Lent), holy water sprinkled in memory of baptism, the greeting Christ is risen! He is risen indeed!

Catholic practice

Easter Sunday is followed by an octave ending on Divine Mercy Sunday. Ascension falls forty days after Easter (Thursday 6 May 2027; moved to Sunday 9 May in most US dioceses). The Regina Caeli replaces the Angelus. Every Catholic is obliged to receive Communion during Easter time.

Lutheran practice

Lutherans call the Sundays "of Easter" (not "after Easter") to keep the fifty days as one feast. Ascension Day is kept on the Thursday or the following Sunday. Many congregations keep the old Easter Vigil and sunrise services.
