Christians have marked time by the life of Christ since the first centuries: a weekly Sunday for the Resurrection, a yearly Pascha, and, by the fifth century, a cycle of preparation, feast and ordinary time. The Catechism calls it the year in which "the whole mystery of Christ" unfolds (§1171). Catholics, Lutherans, Anglicans and many other churches share the same shape and, through the lectionary, many of the same Sunday readings.

Lectionary years: readings run on a three-year cycle: A (Matthew), B (Mark), C (Luke), with John in all three. The year that begins on 29 November 2026 is Year B, the year of Mark, for both the Catholic Lectionary and the Revised Common Lectionary.

The seasons

🕯️ Advent
Four Sundays before Christmas. 2026: Sunday 29 November to Thursday 24 December.⭐ Christmas
25 December to the Baptism of the Lord (Catholic; 10 January 2027) or to 5 January, the Twelve Days (Lutheran).✨ Epiphany
Feast on 6 January (in the US Catholic calendar, the Sunday between 2 and 8 January: 3 January 2027). Lutheran "Time after Epiphany" runs to Transfiguration Sunday (7 February 2027).✝️ Lent
Ash Wednesday to Holy Thursday. 2027: 10 February to 25 March.🌿 Holy Week & Triduum
Palm Sunday to Holy Saturday. 2027: 21–27 March; the Triduum runs from Holy Thursday evening (25 March) to Easter Sunday evening.🌅 Easter
Fifty days, Easter Sunday to Pentecost. 2027: 28 March to 16 May. Easter is the first Sunday after the first full moon on or after 21 March.🔥 Pentecost
The fiftieth day of Easter. 2027: Sunday 16 May.🌱 Ordinary Time / Season after Pentecost
Catholic Ordinary Time: from the day after the Baptism of the Lord to Ash Wednesday, and from the day after Pentecost to Christ the King. Lutheran "Time after Pentecost": 17 May to 27 November 2027.
