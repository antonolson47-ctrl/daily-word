WhenThe fiftieth day of Easter. 2027: Sunday 16 May.

History

Pentekoste is Greek for "fiftieth". It was first the Jewish harvest Feast of Weeks (Shavuot), fifty days after Passover, which in later Jewish tradition also remembered the giving of the Law at Sinai. On that feast the Holy Spirit came on the apostles in wind and fire (Acts 2). Tertullian (c. 200) already names Pentecost as a solemn time for baptism; in England it was called Whitsunday from the white robes of the newly baptized (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Pentecost").

Why it is observed

Pentecost completes Easter: the Risen Christ pours out the Spirit, and the Church goes out to every nation. The crowd hearing one message in many languages reverses the scattering at Babel. It is often called the birthday of the Church.

Colours and symbols

Red for fire, the dove, tongues of flame, wind, the sequence Veni Sancte Spiritus and the hymn Veni Creator Spiritus (9th century, attributed to Rabanus Maurus), red flowers, and readings in several languages.

Catholic practice

Pentecost is a solemnity closing the Easter season, often with a vigil Mass. The Paschal candle moves to the baptistery. Confirmations are common in this season.

Lutheran practice

Lutherans wear red and often celebrate Affirmation of Baptism (Confirmation). Luther's Come, Holy Ghost, God and Lord (Komm, Heiliger Geist, Herre Gott, 1524) expands an older Latin antiphon.
