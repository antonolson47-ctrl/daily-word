Light the wreath candle on Sunday, read the passage, then the meditation. Scripture quotations are from the King James Version (public domain); full readings are linked to the USCCB.

Sunday, 29 November 2026 · Candle of Hope

First Sunday of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 64:1-9 (Catholic: Isaiah 63:16b-17,19b; 64:2-7) · Psalm 80 · 1 Corinthians 1:3-9 · Mark 13:24-37 (Catholic: Mark 13:33-37) · USCCB · RCL

"O that thou wouldest rend the heavens, that thou wouldest come down" (Isaiah 64:1, KJV). The year begins not with a baby but with a cry. Isaiah's people have come home from exile to rubble, and they ask God to tear the sky open and act.

Jesus answers with a parable of a householder who leaves on a journey and tells the doorkeeper to stay awake. "Watch ye therefore: for ye know not when the master of the house cometh" (Mark 13:35). Mark's whole Gospel moves at a run (euthys, "immediately", appears over forty times), and here Jesus slows down to say one thing four times: watch.

Watching is not anxiety. A doorkeeper is calm because he knows who is coming. Paul tells the Corinthians that they "come behind in no gift, waiting for the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 1:7): the waiting itself is full of gifts.

This week: light the first candle, the candle of hope. Pick one daily moment (a coffee, a commute) to be your doorkeeper's watch: one minute of saying, "Come, Lord Jesus."

Monday, 30 November 2026

Feast of St Andrew, Apostle

Readings: Romans 10:9-18 · Psalm 19 · Matthew 4:18-22 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Andrew

The first to be called is the first saint of the Church year. Andrew leaves his nets "straightway" (Matthew 4:20). In John's Gospel he is always bringing someone to Jesus: his brother Peter, the boy with the loaves, the Greeks who want to see Jesus. Advent begins with a fisherman who made introductions. Who could you bring a step closer this season?

Tuesday, 01 December 2026

Tuesday, 1st week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 11:1-10 · Psalm 72 · Luke 10:21-24 · Full text at USCCB

A shoot from a cut-down stump (Isaiah 11:1). Jesse's family tree had been felled by exile, yet God grows the Messiah from what looked dead. The wolf and lamb lying down together is the world as it will be. Jesus thanks the Father that these things are revealed "unto babes" (Luke 10:21). Advent is for beginners.

Wednesday, 02 December 2026

Wednesday, 1st week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 25:6-10a · Psalm 23 · Matthew 15:29-37 · Full text at USCCB

Isaiah sees a banquet on the mountain "of fat things, of wines on the lees" where God swallows up death and wipes away every tear. On a hillside by Galilee Jesus heals the lame and blind and feeds four thousand with seven loaves. The feast has started early. Eat something today slowly and with thanks.

Thursday, 03 December 2026

Thursday, 1st week of Advent (St Francis Xavier)

Readings: Isaiah 26:1-6 · Psalm 118 · Matthew 7:21,24-27 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Francis Xavier

"Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee" (Isaiah 26:3). Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount with two houses and one storm: the difference is the foundation. Francis Xavier, today's saint, crossed oceans to India and Japan on that rock. What is your house standing on this week?

Friday, 04 December 2026

Friday, 1st week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 29:17-24 · Psalm 27 · Matthew 9:27-31 · Full text at USCCB

Two blind men follow Jesus shouting "Son of David", a royal title, which means they already see who he is. Jesus asks one question: "Believe ye that I am able to do this?" Isaiah promised the deaf would hear and the blind see "out of obscurity". Advent asks for the same faith: not certainty about how, but trust in who.

Saturday, 05 December 2026

Saturday, 1st week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 30:19-21,23-26 · Psalm 147 · Matthew 9:35-10:1,5a,6-8 · Full text at USCCB

"This is the way, walk ye in it" (Isaiah 30:21): a voice behind you when you turn to the right or left. Jesus sees crowds "as sheep having no shepherd" and his answer is to send the Twelve. Compassion in the Gospel always becomes a mission. Freely you have received, freely give.

Sunday, 06 December 2026 · Candle of Peace

Second Sunday of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 40:1-11 · Psalm 85 · 2 Peter 3:8-15a · Mark 1:1-8 · USCCB · RCL

Mark has no shepherds, no manger, no star. His Gospel begins with a man in camel hair at a river: "The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, the Son of God" (Mark 1:1). The good news starts in the wilderness.

Isaiah 40 was spoken to exiles who thought God had forgotten them. "Comfort ye, comfort ye my people" (40:1) is a double command: say it twice, because they will not believe it the first time. The way home across the desert will be made level. John the Baptist takes this ancient road-building and makes it personal: repent, turn round, clear the road in your own heart.

Peter adds that the Lord is "not slack concerning his promise", but patient, "not willing that any should perish" (2 Pet 3:9). Delay is mercy. Peace in Advent is not a feeling but a road being cleared.

This week: the second candle, peace. Name one valley to fill (a neglected person) and one mountain to bring low (a grudge), and do something about each before next Sunday.

Monday, 07 December 2026

Monday, 2nd week of Advent (St Ambrose)

Readings: Isaiah 35:1-10 · Psalm 85 · Luke 5:17-26 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Ambrose

The desert blossoms "as the rose" and a highway called the way of holiness runs through it. Four friends, finding no way in, take the roof off to lower a paralysed man to Jesus. Sometimes making a way means tearing something open. St Ambrose, whose hymns we still sing in Advent, made a way for Augustine.

Tuesday, 08 December 2026

Solemnity of the Immaculate Conception

Readings: Genesis 3:9-15,20 · Psalm 98 · Ephesians 1:3-6,11-12 · Luke 1:26-38 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Mary

In the garden God asks the first question in the Bible: "Where art thou?" Today the Church honours the woman whose life answered it without hiding: Mary, kept free from original sin from her conception, so that she could say a whole-hearted yes. Ephesians says we too were chosen "before the foundation of the world". (A holy day of obligation in the US.)

Wednesday, 09 December 2026

Wednesday, 2nd week of Advent (St Juan Diego)

Readings: Isaiah 40:25-31 · Psalm 103 · Matthew 11:28-30 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Juan Diego

"They that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles" (Isaiah 40:31). Jesus says his yoke is easy, which is not the same as no yoke. Juan Diego, a 57-year-old Aztec widower, walked miles to Mass and was given a mission he felt too small for. God chose him anyway.

Thursday, 10 December 2026

Thursday, 2nd week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 41:13-20 · Psalm 145 · Matthew 11:11-15 · Full text at USCCB

God calls Israel "thou worm Jacob" and in the same breath promises to make it a threshing sledge with new sharp teeth. Weakness is not the end of the story. Jesus says none born of women is greater than John the Baptist, yet the least in the kingdom is greater. Grace, not rank, decides.

Friday, 11 December 2026

Friday, 2nd week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 48:17-19 · Psalm 1 · Matthew 11:16-19 · Full text at USCCB

"O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments! then had thy peace been as a river." Jesus compares his generation to children in the market who refuse every game: John fasted and was called mad, the Son of Man ate and was called a glutton. Is there a way God has been calling that I keep dismissing?

Saturday, 12 December 2026

Our Lady of Guadalupe

Readings: Zechariah 2:14-17 · Judith 13 · Luke 1:39-47 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Juan Diego

"Sing and rejoice, O daughter of Zion: for, lo, I come, and I will dwell in the midst of thee." In 1531 Mary appeared on Tepeyac hill as a pregnant Native woman, speaking Nahuatl, carrying the Child within her. Like the Visitation, it is a story of Christ carried quietly to people who needed him. Patroness of the Americas.

Sunday, 13 December 2026 · Candle of Joy

Third Sunday of Advent (Gaudete)

Readings: Isaiah 61:1-4,8-11 · Psalm 126 or Luke 1:46b-55 · 1 Thessalonians 5:16-24 · John 1:6-8,19-28 · USCCB · RCL

Gaudete in Domino semper, "Rejoice in the Lord always" (Philippians 4:4): the old entrance antiphon gives this Sunday its name and its rose-coloured candle. Halfway through the waiting, the Church lets a little Christmas light in.

Isaiah 61 is the text Jesus will read in the Nazareth synagogue: good news to the poor, liberty to captives, "beauty for ashes" (61:3). And John's Gospel shows John the Baptist answering a delegation from Jerusalem with three refusals: I am not the Christ, not Elijah, not the prophet. "I am the voice" (1:23). His joy is to point away from himself: "there standeth one among you, whom ye know not" (1:26).

Paul gives a three-line rule of life: "Rejoice evermore. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks" (1 Thess 5:16-18). Joy here is a practice, not a mood.

This week: the rose candle. Each night, name three things to give thanks for, and once this week point someone else's attention to Christ rather than to yourself.

Monday, 14 December 2026

Monday, 3rd week of Advent (St John of the Cross)

Readings: Numbers 24:2-7,15-17a · Psalm 25 · Matthew 21:23-27 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: John Of The Cross

A pagan seer, Balaam, hired to curse Israel, blesses it instead: "there shall come a Star out of Jacob". God speaks through unlikely mouths. The leaders ask Jesus for his credentials and will not answer his question about John. St John of the Cross reminds us that God is often found in the dark night, not in our certainties.

Tuesday, 15 December 2026

Tuesday, 3rd week of Advent

Readings: Zephaniah 3:1-2,9-13 · Psalm 34 · Matthew 21:28-32 · Full text at USCCB

Two sons: one says no and then goes; one says yes and does not. Jesus says tax collectors and harlots are entering the kingdom ahead of the respectable. Zephaniah promises God will leave "an afflicted and poor people" who trust in his name. It is never too late to change a no into a yes.

Wednesday, 16 December 2026

Wednesday, 3rd week of Advent

Readings: Isaiah 45:6b-8,18,21b-25 · Psalm 85 · Luke 7:18b-23 · Full text at USCCB

"Drop down, ye heavens, from above, and let the skies pour down righteousness" (Isaiah 45:8), the Rorate caeli sung through Advent. John, in prison, sends to ask, "Art thou he that should come?" Jesus answers with evidence, not argument: the blind see, the poor hear good news. Doubt brought honestly to Jesus is met, not scolded.

Thursday, 17 December 2026

17 December · O Sapientia

Readings: Genesis 49:2,8-10 · Psalm 72 · Matthew 1:1-17 · Full text at USCCB

The O Antiphons begin: O Wisdom, coming forth from the mouth of the Most High. Matthew opens with a family tree of forty-two generations, with saints and scoundrels and four surprising women (Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and Bathsheba). God's Wisdom works through real families, including messy ones. Pray for your own family tree today.

Friday, 18 December 2026

18 December · O Adonai

Readings: Jeremiah 23:5-8 · Psalm 72 · Matthew 1:18-25 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Joseph

O Lord and Ruler of the house of Israel, who appeared to Moses in the burning bush. Joseph, "a just man", plans a quiet divorce to protect Mary, then obeys a dream and takes her home. He never speaks a word in the Gospels. Holiness sometimes looks like silent, costly obedience. (St Joseph has his own page on the Saints wiki.)

Saturday, 19 December 2026

19 December · O Radix Jesse

Readings: Judges 13:2-7,24-25a · Psalm 71 · Luke 1:5-25 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: John The Baptist

O Root of Jesse, standing as a sign for the peoples. Two barren couples, Manoah's wife and Zechariah and Elizabeth, receive sons: Samson and John. Zechariah asks for proof and is struck silent till the birth. Advent sometimes asks us to be quiet long enough to let God finish speaking.

Sunday, 20 December 2026 · Candle of Love

Fourth Sunday of Advent

Readings: 2 Samuel 7:1-11,16 · Luke 1:46b-55 or Psalm 89 · Romans 16:25-27 · Luke 1:26-38 · USCCB · RCL

King David, safe at last in his cedar palace, wants to build God a house. God turns the offer around through the prophet Nathan: you will not build me a house; I will build you one, a dynasty, and "thy throne shall be established for ever" (2 Sam 7:16).

A thousand years later the promise comes due in a small town in Galilee. Gabriel tells Mary that her son will be given "the throne of his father David" (Luke 1:32). God's house turns out not to be cedar but a young woman's life, and then a child's body. Mary's answer, "be it unto me according to thy word" (1:38), is the hinge of the whole story; the medieval writers loved to say that all creation waited for her yes.

Love in Advent is this kind of consent: letting God build instead of insisting on building for him.

This week: the fourth candle. Pray the Angelus or the Magnificat (Luke 1:46-55) once a day, and say one costly yes to someone who needs you before Christmas.

Monday, 21 December 2026

21 December · O Oriens

Readings: Song of Songs 2:8-14 or Zephaniah 3:14-18a · Psalm 33 · Luke 1:39-45 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Mary

O Morning Star, splendour of light eternal: the longest night of the year, and the antiphon sings of the dawn. Mary hurries to the hill country; the unborn John leaps in Elizabeth's womb. The first person to recognise Jesus in the Gospels is a baby who has not been born yet.

Tuesday, 22 December 2026

22 December · O Rex Gentium

Readings: 1 Samuel 1:24-28 · 1 Samuel 2 · Luke 1:46-56 · Full text at USCCB · Saint: Mary

O King of the nations, the cornerstone. Hannah gives her long-awaited Samuel back to God and sings; Mary sings the Magnificat in almost the same words: the mighty brought down, the lowly lifted up. Pray the Magnificat slowly today, as the Church does every evening at Vespers.

Wednesday, 23 December 2026

23 December · O Emmanuel

Readings: Malachi 3:1-4,23-24 · Psalm 25 · Luke 1:57-66 · Full text at USCCB

O Emmanuel, our King and Lawgiver. Read the antiphons' first Latin words backwards from today (Emmanuel, Rex, Oriens, Clavis, Radix, Adonai, Sapientia) and the initials spell ERO CRAS: "I will be there tomorrow." Zechariah writes "His name is John" and his tongue is set free.

Thursday, 24 December 2026

Christmas Eve morning

Readings: 2 Samuel 7:1-5,8b-12,14a,16 · Psalm 89 · Luke 1:67-79 · Full text at USCCB

Zechariah's Benedictus ends the waiting: "the dayspring from on high hath visited us, to give light to them that sit in darkness" (Luke 1:78-79). The Church prays it every morning. Advent closes at dawn with a promise of a sunrise. Tonight the candle in the window can be lit.

Meditations written for The Daily Word. Readings: Lectionary for Mass (USCCB) and Revised Common Lectionary (Consultation on Common Texts, via Vanderbilt Divinity Library); the O Antiphons and ERO CRAS from the Roman Breviary tradition.
