WhenFeast on 6 January (in the US Catholic calendar, the Sunday between 2 and 8 January: 3 January 2027). Lutheran "Time after Epiphany" runs to Transfiguration Sunday (7 February 2027).

History

Epiphaneia is Greek for "manifestation". It is older than Christmas: Clement of Alexandria (c. 200) mentions Egyptian Christians keeping Christ's baptism on 6 January. In the East it became the great feast of the Baptism ("Theophany"); in the West, the coming of the Magi. Ancient Western texts join three manifestations: the star to the Magi, the voice at the Jordan, and the water made wine at Cana (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Epiphany").

Why it is observed

Christmas shows that Christ is born; Epiphany shows who he is and for whom. The Magi are the first Gentiles to worship him, so the feast is about the whole world being drawn to Christ, and the weeks after it read his first signs and calls.

Colours and symbols

The star, the three gifts (gold for a king, frankincense for God, myrrh for one who will die), chalking the door (20+C+M+B+27, "Christus mansionem benedicat", May Christ bless this house), blessing of water in the East, King Cake and the Spanish Día de Reyes.

Catholic practice

Epiphany is a solemnity; the following Sunday is the Baptism of the Lord, and Ordinary Time begins the next day. On Epiphany the deacon may proclaim the dates of Easter and the movable feasts for the year, an old custom.

Lutheran practice

The ELCA and LCMS keep a "Time after Epiphany" with green vestments, framed by the Baptism of Our Lord and Transfiguration Sunday (the last Sunday before Lent), so the season moves from the river to the mountain.
