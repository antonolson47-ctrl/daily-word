WhenCatholic Ordinary Time: from the day after the Baptism of the Lord to Ash Wednesday, and from the day after Pentecost to Christ the King. Lutheran "Time after Pentecost": 17 May to 27 November 2027.

History

"Ordinary" comes from ordinalis, "numbered": these are the counted Sundays. Before 1969 the Roman calendar called them "Sundays after Epiphany" and "after Pentecost"; the reform after Vatican II joined them into one numbered season of 33 or 34 weeks. Lutherans and many Protestants keep the older names. Trinity Sunday (the first Sunday after Pentecost) spread through the West in the Middle Ages and was made universal by Pope John XXII in 1334. Christ the King, the last Sunday, was added by Pius XI in 1925.

Why it is observed

After the great feasts, the Church walks through Jesus' ministry Sunday by Sunday, reading one Gospel almost continuously (Matthew in Year A, Mark in Year B, Luke in Year C). Green is the colour of growth: this is where faith is lived out in ordinary weeks.

Colours and symbols

Green, the numbered Sundays, harvest festivals, All Saints and All Souls in November, and Christ the King as the year's crown.

Catholic practice

Major celebrations inside the season: Trinity Sunday, Corpus Christi, the Sacred Heart, the Transfiguration (6 Aug), the Assumption (15 Aug), the Exaltation of the Cross (14 Sep), All Saints (1 Nov) and All Souls (2 Nov), and Christ the King.

Lutheran practice

The Lutheran Time after Pentecost includes Trinity Sunday, Reformation Day (31 October, often kept the Sunday before), All Saints' Sunday (when the names of members who died that year are read), and Christ the King. The LCMS still counts "Sundays after Pentecost".
